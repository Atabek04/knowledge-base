The reliability evidence splits cleanly by question type, and it splits along the line between judgement and detection.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Unreliable: the band number.</mark> Per-essay agreement with examiners spans over a band, and the models compress toward the mean exactly where the 7.5-versus-8.0 decision lives. The same applies to any holistic verdict — Task Response quality, whether coherence "flows", speaking fluency.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Reliable: everything checkable.</mark> Finding article and tense errors, flagging collocations that native corpora do not attest, listing which paragraphs lack a single central topic, counting error-carrying sentences, generating practice prompts, explaining a question type, and acting as a conversation partner for spoken practice.

The rule that falls out: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">ask for the location and the name of the error, never for the score.</mark> "Which sentences contain an article error, and which rule does each break" has a verifiable answer you can check. "What band is this" does not.

The corollary is that the feedback loop needs an external anchor the model cannot supply. Official Cambridge sample scripts with published examiner commentary are that anchor — they let you calibrate your own judgement, which is the thing the model cannot lend you.

Source: [Koraishi (2024), *Language Teaching Research Quarterly* 43](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1457168.pdf)
