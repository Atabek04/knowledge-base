### The paradox

AGI lets firms produce far more, far cheaper.

But if wages collapse, most people lose the income they used to buy things with.

<mark style="background: yellow">Firms can produce more than ever, yet fewer consumers can afford to buy it.</mark>

Cheap supply is useless without demand to meet it.

---
### Why it bites

A market economy assumes the people who make goods are also the people who buy them — paychecks become purchases.

Break the wage link and that loop breaks: production and consumption come unhooked.

This is the engine behind every "we'll need UBI" argument — <mark style="background: cyan">redistribution becomes a way to keep demand alive, not just to be fair.</mark>
