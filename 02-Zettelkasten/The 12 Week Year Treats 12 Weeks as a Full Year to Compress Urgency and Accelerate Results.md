Brian Moran and Michael Lennington developed the 12 Week Year after observing a consistent pattern: most people execute well in December, because the year-end deadline creates urgency. Their insight was to manufacture that urgency year-round by treating every 12 weeks as a complete year.

Annual goals fail not because people lack ambition, but because the timeline is too long. With 12 months, there is always "later." With 12 weeks, there is no later.

### The Goal Hierarchy

The system runs in nested layers:

1. **Vision** — your long-term direction (3+ years). Provides meaning and direction.
2. **12-Week Goals** — 1–3 goals for the current cycle. Specific, ambitious, connected to the vision.
3. **Weekly Plan** — the tactics that execute this week's portion of each goal.
4. **Daily Actions** — the specific blocks that fill the day.

Each layer answers to the layer above. A daily action that does not connect to a weekly plan is a distraction.

### Review Cadence

Moran recommends two reviews embedded in every week:

- **Weekly Accountability Meeting (WAM)** — 15–20 minutes reviewing last week's execution score and setting intentions for the next
- **Weekly Plan** — specific task-level planning from the 12-week goals downward

An execution score is tracked: what percentage of your planned tactics did you complete? A score of 85% or above predicts goal achievement. This is the same principle as [[Daily Self-Accounting Closes the Gap Between Plans and Execution]].

### How OKRs Relate

OKRs (Objectives and Key Results), developed at Intel by Andy Grove and popularized by John Doerr at Google, operate on a similar quarterly cadence. An Objective names the direction; Key Results are 3–5 measurable outcomes that define success.

Both the 12 Week Year and OKRs share the same structural insight: **quarterly cycles create enough urgency to execute while allowing enough time to achieve something meaningful.** Annual cycles encourage delay; quarterly cycles do not.

OKRs are graded on a 0–1.0 scale where 0.6–0.7 is considered ideal — meaning goals should be ambitious enough that perfect achievement is a surprise, not an expectation.

### The Psychological Mechanism

Long timelines activate hyperbolic discounting: future effort feels cheap and distant, so starting is always "later." Compressing the cycle to 12 weeks eliminates the psychological safety net. Every week of inaction is visibly costly.
