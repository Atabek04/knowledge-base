### The rule

When reviewing a game, <mark style="background: yellow">write down your own reasoning for each key move *before* you switch on Stockfish.</mark>

The engine's job is narrow: classify a move as blunder, mistake, or inaccuracy.
Your job is to think first.

---
### Why the order matters

<mark style="background: cyan">The gap between your evaluation and the engine's *is* the lesson.</mark>

If you hit "analyze" first, you outsource the thinking and learn nothing — the answer arrives before you've formed a judgment to correct.

Annotating first exposes exactly where your judgment diverges from the truth.

---
### The engine-review discipline

1. Replay <mark style="background: green">engine-off</mark> first — mark where you felt comfortable, confused, or where the game turned.
2. Turn the engine on and find the **largest evaluation swing**, not every move — that swing is where the game was decided.
3. Compare candidates: your move, the engine's top move, and one or two alternatives.
4. Translate the line into a human concept — "which king is less safe?" — not a number.
5. Write **one plain-language takeaway**: *"I attacked before securing my king."*

<mark style="background: pink">A +1.8 evaluation trains nothing; "I attacked before castling" transfers to future games.</mark>
