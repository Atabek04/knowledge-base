Outcome-based goals create a finish line. Once reached (or failed), the motivation disappears. Identity-based habits have no finish line — they are a description of who you are.

James Clear: "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you want to become."

## The Difference

| Outcome Goal | Identity Statement |
|---|---|
| "I want to memorize Quran" | "I am someone who memorizes Quran daily" |
| "I want to get fit" | "I am someone who moves every day" |
| "I want to read more" | "I am a reader" |

## Why Identity Wins

- Outcomes depend on circumstances. Identity is portable.
- When you miss a day as an outcome-chaser, you failed. When you miss a day as an identity-holder, you just had an off day — you'll return tomorrow.
- Identity creates intrinsic motivation. Outcomes create pressure.

## How to Shift

Start by asking: "What kind of person would achieve this outcome?" Then act like that person — not to reach the goal, but because that's who you are.

Small consistent actions accumulate as *evidence* for the identity. The identity reinforces the actions. A feedback loop builds.
