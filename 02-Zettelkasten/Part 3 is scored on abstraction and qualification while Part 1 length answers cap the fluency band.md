Part 3 asks you to discuss a topic in depth — to speculate, evaluate and justify about society rather than to report about yourself. The commonest failure is not error but scale: giving Part 1-sized answers, which leaves too small a sample and drags the fluency average.

The working length is roughly 30-60 seconds, and the structures teachers converge on are the same shape: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">opinion, reason, example, consequence</mark> — answer directly, then ask yourself why or how, then illustrate, then name the alternative view.

What actually separates 7 from 8 here is not more structure. Band 7 candidates already concede and counter. Band 8 speakers <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">move between simple and complex forms without forcing complexity into every sentence</mark>, and qualify rather than assert.

Hedging does double duty and is worth drilling for that reason: *to some extent*, *generally speaking*, *arguably*, *in all likelihood* count as grammatical range, and they buy content-shaped thinking time — the only hesitation type band 8 permits.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The trap for an article-less L1 is that Part 3 is where articles concentrate.</mark> Abstraction means generic noun phrases — *the government*, *a lack of*, *the elderly* — so the section that earns the fluency marks is also the one that leaks grammar marks.
