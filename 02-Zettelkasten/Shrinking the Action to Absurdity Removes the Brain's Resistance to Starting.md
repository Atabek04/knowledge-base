The brain's resistance to action scales with the perceived size of the task. A large task triggers avoidance. A tiny task below the threshold of resistance gets executed automatically.

The technique: shrink the action until it feels almost embarrassing to skip.

## Examples

| Big Action (Avoided) | Tiny Action (Executed) |
|---|---|
| "Study for 2 hours" | "Open the book" |
| "Memorize 5 lines of Quran" | "Open the mushaf after Fajr" |
| "Write the report" | "Open the document and type one sentence" |

## Why It Works

Starting is the hardest part. Once the action has begun, the brain's momentum carries it forward — continuing is far easier than initiating. The tiny version just gets you past the starting line.

This is the core of BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits and James Clear's 2-Minute Rule.

## The Trap to Avoid

Don't stay tiny forever. The minimum version is for *initiating* the habit. Once the behavior is automatic (2–4 weeks), scale up.
