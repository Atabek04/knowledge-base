Every small urgent task that slips into your focus block is a context switch.

Context switches don't just cost you the time of the task itself — they cost the mental warm-up to get back into deep focus, which research estimates at 15-23 minutes per break.

The solution is a two-stage filter.

### Stage 1: The 2-Minute Rule (David Allen)

When a small task appears, ask: "Does this take under 2 minutes?"

If yes — do it immediately. Don't log it, don't schedule it. The cost of managing it later is higher than just finishing it now.

If no — it doesn't happen now. It goes on a list.

This filter prevents small tasks from accumulating into a growing backlog that creates anxiety and interrupts focus.

---

### Stage 2: Batching the Rest (Cal Newport)

Tasks that aren't immediate get batched into a dedicated time slot.

Instead of handling urgents as they arrive, you schedule a "triage block" — a fixed window (e.g., 30 min at 9am and 3pm) where you process everything that came in.

This protects deep work by containing urgents in time rather than letting them leak across the day.

The message to others and to yourself: outside the triage block, you are unavailable — not unresponsive, just scheduled.

---

### The Interruption Exception

Not every urgent is equal.

Real emergencies (production down, actual crisis) bypass the filter. The 2-minute rule and batching only work if you define, in advance, what counts as a true emergency.

Write that definition before your focus block starts. This is itself an [[If-Then Plans Pre-Decide Your Response to Interruptions Before They Happen|implementation intention]].

---

### Why Most People Fail This

They apply the 2-minute rule continuously — knocking out small tasks all day.

That turns it into an urgency treadmill, not a filter.

The rule is a gate, not a lifestyle. Apply it during transition moments; protect focus blocks from it entirely.

This mirrors the logic of [[Environment Design Shapes Behavior More Reliably Than Willpower]] — the system decides what gets in, not your real-time willpower.
