Workout videos are free, yet people pay for gyms; university lectures are free online, yet people pay for university. The knowledge was never the scarce part. What is scarce is the thing that makes a person actually show up.

### Present bias

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Present bias is the tendency to overweight today's comfort against tomorrow's goal, so a person plans to study tomorrow and then, tomorrow, plans the same again.</mark> The bias is in the *present*: the current moment always wins.

DellaVigna and Malmendier (2006), American Economic Review, found gym members on monthly contracts attended so rarely that they would have paid less buying single visits.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">People overestimate their future self-control, so they buy access expecting to use it and then do not.</mark>

Free learning has the same problem with no cost at all: a free course can be postponed forever.

---

### Paying is a weak lever

The intuition "I paid, so I will use it" is the **sunk cost effect**: money already spent pulls a person toward using what they bought.

It is real but short. Theatre season-ticket buyers who paid full price attended more than those given a random discount, but only in the first half of the season (Arkes and Blumer, 1985, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes). In a Zambian field experiment, paying more for a product did not make people use it more (Ashraf, Berry and Shapiro, 2010, American Economic Review).

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A higher price does not reliably make people learn more; the sunk-cost push fades within weeks.</mark>

### What actually makes people show up

#### Commitment devices

A **commitment device** is a choice made today that binds the future self: a deadline set by someone else, a booked session, a promise with a cost for breaking it. In an online course, a real commitment device raised completion where reminders did not (Patterson, 2018, Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization).

Learners want deadlines but set them badly for themselves (Bisin and Hyndman, 2020, Games and Economic Behavior), so the schedule works better when the system sets it.

#### A schedule and people

A gym gives a class at a fixed hour and people who notice when you miss it. A university gives timetables and classmates.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Showing up is driven by a fixed schedule and people who expect you, more than by access to the content or the price paid.</mark>

#### A strong reason why

A learner with a clear reason (an exam date, a job, reading the Qur'an in Arabic) needs less outside push. A reason with a date attached works as its own deadline.
