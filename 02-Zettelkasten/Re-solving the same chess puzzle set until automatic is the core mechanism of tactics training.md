### The shared mechanism

Every serious tactics-training method shares one engine: <mark style="background: yellow">re-solve a fixed set of puzzles repeatedly until recognition becomes automatic</mark> — moving tactics from slow conscious calculation to instant pattern recall.

The branded methods differ only in *scheduling*, not in mechanism.

---
### The three wrappers

- **Woodpecker Method** (Smith & Tikkanen) — solve one large set, then re-solve the *same* set, faster each cycle.
- **de la Maza's Seven Circles** — drill one tactics set through seven accelerating passes.
- **Spaced repetition** — re-surface each item at expanding intervals timed to forgetting.

<mark style="background: cyan">All three target automaticity; they disagree only on how much repetition and on what set.</mark>

Silman's dissent: drill ~2,000 *basic* tactics to recognition, but solve *hard* puzzles only once.

---
### Honesty about the evidence

<mark style="background: pink">The dramatic rating claims rest on anecdote</mark> — Tikkanen's "3 GM norms in 7 weeks," de la Maza's "+400 points." The "tactics is *all* you need" framing is widely rejected; gains are real at the low end and thin out higher up.

The *mechanism* (repetition → recognition) is sound. The *magnitude* is unproven.

---
### How to apply

Drill basic motifs to instant recognition; mix [[Lichess provides unlimited free engine analysis and tactics training|Puzzle Storm for speed and Streak for depth]].
Don't race for a fast solve on hard puzzles — extract the principle.
