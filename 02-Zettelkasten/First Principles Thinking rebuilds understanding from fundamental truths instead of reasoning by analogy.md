### What it is

First principles thinking breaks a problem down to its most fundamental, irreducible truths — facts that can't be deduced from anything more basic — then reasons upward from those truths to build a solution.

Aristotle called a first principle <mark style="background: yellow">"the first basis from which a thing is known."</mark>

---
### The alternative: reasoning by analogy

Most thinking copies what already exists.
You reason by analogy — *"X works for others, so I'll do a version of X."*

Analogy is fast and good enough for routine problems.
But it inherits every hidden assumption baked into the thing you're copying.

First principles thinking ignores precedent and rebuilds from verifiable facts.
You keep only what you can prove from the ground up.

---
### Why it produces breakthroughs

When Elon Musk asked what a rocket's raw materials actually *cost* — not what rockets sell for — he found SpaceX could build them at a fraction of the market price.

The market price was an analogy. The material cost was a first principle.

<mark style="background: cyan">The leverage comes from questioning the assumption everyone else accepts without checking.</mark>

---
### The cost

First principles thinking is slow.
Decomposing a problem to bedrock takes far more effort than copying a working pattern.

<mark style="background: pink">Reserve it for foundations you'll build on for years — not every decision.</mark>

Feynman's warning applies: *"The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."*
