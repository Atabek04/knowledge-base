### What it is

Retrieval practice is the act of actively recalling information from memory rather than re-reading or re-watching it.

The "testing effect" (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006): a retrieval attempt is not just assessment — it is a powerful learning event that strengthens the memory trace itself.

---

### The mechanism

Re-reading creates an illusion of mastery. Familiarity with material *feels* like knowledge but does not strengthen the memory trace.

Active retrieval forces the brain to reconstruct the memory path, which:
1. Strengthens the neural pathway (storage strength grows)
2. Flags gaps — you cannot fake recall the way you can fake re-reading

This is a core [[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency|desirable difficulty]]: the effort of recall produces durable learning that passive review cannot replicate.

---

### The research

Roediger & Karpicke (2006) compared three conditions:
- SSSS (repeated study)
- SSST (one test)
- STTT (three tests)

The re-study group outperformed on a 5-minute delay test.

<mark style="background: yellow">At 1-week delay, the STTT group outperformed by ~50%.</mark>

Short-term advantage reverses to long-term disadvantage for passive methods.

### Practical methods

| Method | How to apply |
|---|---|
| Free recall | Close the book; write everything you remember on a blank page; then check |
| Flashcards (Anki/RemNote) | Spaced retrieval for definitions, API signatures, algorithm patterns |
| Practice problems without hints | Solve before looking at solutions — not alongside them |
| Blank-page protocol | Before re-reading a topic, write what you know first; gaps become visible |
| Exit tickets / retrieval starters | Start/end every study session with a quick recall check |

<mark style="background: cyan">Key distinction:</mark> reviewing notes is not retrieval practice. Closing notes and reconstructing them is.

---

### Connection to spaced repetition

Retrieval practice is most powerful when combined with spacing — retrieving after a gap (when memory has partially decayed) produces bigger consolidation gains than immediate re-testing.

[[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency|Spacing]] is the scheduling mechanism; retrieval practice is the operation performed at each interval.
