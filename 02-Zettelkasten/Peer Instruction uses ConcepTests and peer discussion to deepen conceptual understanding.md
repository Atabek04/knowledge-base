### What it is

Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur, Harvard, 1991) replaces passive lecture segments with short conceptual multiple-choice questions — ConcepTests — that force students to commit to an answer, discuss with a peer who chose differently, then re-vote.

The key insight: <mark style="background: yellow">a student who just understood a hard concept explains it more effectively than an expert who can no longer remember not knowing it.</mark>

---

### The ConcepTest cycle

1. Instructor poses a multiple-choice question targeting a known misconception
2. **Individual think time** (1–2 min) — students commit silently; triggers retrieval and metacognition
3. **First vote** — all students respond simultaneously (clickers, hands, polling app)
4. **Peer discussion** (2–3 min):
   - If >85% correct → explain briefly and move on
   - If 30–70% correct → students find someone with a different answer and argue
   - If <30% correct → reteach before discussion
5. **Re-vote** — typically shows significant improvement
6. **Instructor explanation** — reveals answer, explains reasoning, invites student explanations

---

### Why it works

- The pre-discussion vote prevents social conformity from bypassing individual thinking
- Peer explanation forces the explainer to restructure their understanding (generation effect)
- The listener receives an explanation calibrated to their actual confusion level
- Requires active retrieval and argumentation — not passive reception

<mark style="background: cyan">This is essentially [[Think-Pair-Share gives every learner think time before committing to whole-class discussion|Think-Pair-Share]] with a retrieval commitment step added before the Pair phase.</mark>

---

### Transfer beyond physics

Validated in: medical education, mathematics, biology, computer science, humanities.

The mechanism — targeted conceptual questions + structured peer discussion — is domain-agnostic.

---

### Online vs offline

**Offline:** Colored cards (A/B/C/D) for simultaneous voting; physically pair students with different answers.

**Online:** Mentimeter, Poll Everywhere, or Zoom polls for anonymous simultaneous voting; breakout rooms (2–3 min) for peer discussion; show live poll results to drive the debrief.
