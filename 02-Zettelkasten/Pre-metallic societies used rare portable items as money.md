Before metal coins, people used naturally scarce items as currency.

Examples: seashells, animal teeth, beads.

Key requirement: rarity (hard to counterfeit) + portability.

Jewelry emerged from this — drilling holes in shells/teeth to wear them made wealth easy to carry and display.

## The Value Consensus Problem

Money only works if the community values it. You can't arbitrarily pick random items like simple river stones—they must be inherently valuable.

This is why rare, portable items (shells, teeth, beads) succeeded as money: communities universally desired them for beauty, status, or scarcity.

**Example**: Seashells worked as money in coastal tribes because everyone valued their beauty and rarity. Simple river stones failed because nobody wanted them—they had abundance, not scarcity.
