Every claim that AI tutoring can transform education traces back to one 1984 number. That number is real evidence that tutoring works, and also an overstatement that later research cut down.

### Bloom's two sigma result

**Sigma** (σ) is a standard deviation, the typical spread of scores around the average. A student at +2σ scores above about 98% of the group.

Bloom (1984) reported that students tutored one-to-one scored about 2σ above students in a conventional 30-student class: the average tutored student beat 98% of the classroom students.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The 2 Sigma Problem is Bloom's challenge to find group teaching methods as effective as one-to-one tutoring, since a tutor for every student is too expensive for any society.</mark>

#### What the tutored group actually got

The tutored students also received **mastery learning**: a short check after each unit, corrections, and no moving on until the unit was mastered.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">The two sigma came from tutoring combined with mastery learning, and from help at the moment of being stuck with no audience to fear.</mark>

---

### What later evidence found

VanLehn (2011) re-examined the comparisons: human tutoring was worth about 0.79σ. Nickow et al. (2024) pooled modern tutoring experiments and found about 0.29σ.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Two sigma came from a few short studies by Bloom's own students; cite it as the motivating benchmark, never as the expected effect of tutoring.</mark>

#### Step-based computer tutors nearly match humans

VanLehn also found computer tutors giving feedback on each step of a problem reached about 0.76σ, close to human tutors. The cognitive part of tutoring can be automated.

---

### What a human mentor still adds

The part left over is the relationship: trust, motivation, and someone who notices you. Personal emails from professors cut dropout among the least prepared students (Carrell and Kurlaender, 2023), and human coaching raised persistence (Bettinger and Baker, 2014).

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Mostly automated, low-touch coaching changed students' beliefs but not their grades or persistence (Oreopoulos and Petronijevic, 2023), so the human in the loop may be the active ingredient.</mark>

This is the opposite of a buy-and-forget video course, where nobody notices when a learner stops.
