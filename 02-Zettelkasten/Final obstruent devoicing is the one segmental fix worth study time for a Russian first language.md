Russian devoices obstruents at the end of a word, and the habit transfers directly into English — *bed* becomes *bet*, *bag* becomes *back*, *prove* becomes *proof*.

It is the costliest sound-level habit because <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">it produces real minimal pairs rather than an accented version of the same word</mark>. That is the test for whether a sound is worth study time at all: not whether it differs from the native realisation, but whether the listener can still recover which word was said.

The second L1 transfer worth naming is intonation range: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Russian statements use a narrower pitch range and a sharper terminal fall than English</mark>, which reads across a long turn as flat or abrupt. This one is prosodic, so it sits directly in the band 8 Pronunciation bullets and outranks every individual sound.

Missing phonemes — /θ/, /ð/, /w/ realised as /v/, and the ship/sheep vowel-length contrast — come last, and only where they produce a real minimal-pair confusion. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Consistently substituting these is compatible with band 8, because they do not cost intelligibility.</mark>
