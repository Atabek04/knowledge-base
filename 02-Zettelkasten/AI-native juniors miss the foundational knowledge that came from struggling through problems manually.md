Developers who started their careers with AI face a specific hazard: the understanding that older engineers built by *struggling through problems* is the very step AI now skips for them.

This is the learning-path root beneath the [[Over-relying on AI coding tools without reading output erodes genuine programming confidence|collapse of confidence when the AI is taken away]] — not just shaky confidence, but a real gap in foundations.

---

### The mechanism

Engineer Namanyay Goel's widely-shared 2025 line captures it:

> the foundational knowledge that used to come from struggling through problems is just… missing

The struggle wasn't waste — it was where understanding got built. <mark style="background: yellow">Remove the struggle and you remove the learning that came with it.</mark>

This is the same dynamic the data shows directly: [[AI-assisted learners score about two letter grades lower with debugging the most degraded skill|AI-assisted learners scored two grades lower, with debugging worst hit]].

---

### The labor-market pressure is concrete

This isn't only a skills worry. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab found employment of <mark style="background: pink">22-25-year-old software developers fell ~20% from its 2024 peak</mark>, while older cohorts in the same roles held steady.

The entry rung is where AI bites first — consistent with the broader pattern that [[AI automates codified book-knowledge, exposing entry-level roles most|AI automates book-knowledge and exposes entry-level roles first]].

---

### What this is *not*

<mark style="background: cyan;">The gap is in the path, not the person.</mark> Foundations can still be built deliberately — by reading every line, asking *why* it works, and using AI for inquiry rather than bypass. The danger is only when AI is used to skip the struggle entirely. Whether foundations built without the historical struggle prove as durable is still an open question.
