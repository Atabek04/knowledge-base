### The idea

Adopting better habits causes a <mark style="background: yellow">temporary performance *dip* while you're still inexperienced in the new positions and routines.</mark>

Most players quit during the dip — and stay stuck. (GM Noël Studer.)

---
### Why the dip happens

New habits — slowing down, a different opening, a real thinking process — are clumsy at first.
Your rating falls before the new skill matures.

<mark style="background: pink">Chasing rating recovery during the dip reinforces the old habits you were trying to drop.</mark>

---
### How to push through

- Commit to a change for <mark style="background: green">1–3 months before judging it.</mark>
- Experiment on a *second* account you don't care about, so the rating doesn't tax your ego.
- Reduce serious tournaments during the dip.
- <mark style="background: cyan">Decouple your identity from your rating number.</mark>

A flat rating while you keep playing without studying isn't a wall — it usually means you stopped *studying*; the fix is the [[Analyzing your own games is the highest-ROI chess training activity|diagnostic loop]], then deliberate work on the gap.
