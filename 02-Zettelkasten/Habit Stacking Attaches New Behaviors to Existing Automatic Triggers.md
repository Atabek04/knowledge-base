Every behavior you do automatically is a potential anchor for a new habit. Instead of relying on willpower or reminders, you borrow the momentum of an existing routine.

**Formula:** "After [existing automatic habit], I will [new behavior] for [duration]."

## Examples

| Existing Habit | Stacked New Habit |
|---|---|
| Fajr prayer | Open mushaf, read 3 lines |
| Morning coffee | Review today's one priority |
| Closing laptop at work | 10 minutes of back stretches |
| Maghrib prayer | Arabic study, 15 minutes |

## Why This Works

The existing habit provides the cue. The brain doesn't have to decide — the trigger fires automatically. Decision fatigue never enters the picture.

Research shows habit stacking increases success rate by ~64% compared to scheduling new habits at arbitrary times.

## One Stack at a Time

Never stack more than one new habit at a time. Adding two creates cognitive load and collapses both. Stabilize the first (2–4 weeks consistent) before adding the next.
