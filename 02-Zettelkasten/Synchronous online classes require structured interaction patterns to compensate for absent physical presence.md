### What it is

Online synchronous classes suffer a structural engagement deficit: the physical accountability cues that keep students focused in-person — visible instructor, visible peers, no easy escape — do not exist through a screen.

Specific interaction patterns can partially compensate — not by mimicking the classroom, but by exploiting the platform's native affordances deliberately.

---

### The evidence

- 67% of students report decreased motivation in online settings due to lack of structure (Frontiers, 2024, N=27)
- 74% report loss of spontaneous social interaction — the main driver of community and academic persistence
- Polls rated M=4.22/5.0 for engagement; breakout groups M=4.29/5.0 — the two highest-rated tools (PMC, N=55)

---

### Camera-on is a weak lever

Byers et al. (2024): camera-on increases *attendance perception* (instructors feel students are present) but does not improve emotional or cognitive engagement.

<mark style="background: pink">Camera-on ≠ cognitive engagement.</mark> Mandating cameras may backfire via self-view fatigue (see [[Online delivery increases extraneous cognitive load through self-view split-attention and screen fatigue|extraneous load note]]).

The peer cascade effect is real: a student's camera use is most predicted by peer camera behavior (β = .73). Norm-setting in session 1 — not mandates — triggers the cascade.

### Strong engagement tools

**Polls (Mentimeter, Slido, Zoom Polls)**
- Anonymous simultaneous response — everyone must commit before seeing others' answers
- Mirrors [[Peer Instruction uses ConcepTests and peer discussion to deepen conceptual understanding|Peer Instruction]]'s first-vote step
- Use every 10–12 minutes as an attention reset

**Breakout rooms as ZPD scaffolds**
- Smaller groups lower the participation threshold
- Optimal size: ~5 (small enough to jump in, large enough for varied views)
- Post the discussion prompt visibly before sending students in
- <mark style="background: cyan">Breakout rooms are [[Zone of Proximal Development defines the teachable gap between independent and guided performance|ZPD]] micro-environments:</mark> students attempt ideas they wouldn't risk in front of the full class

**Chat as a parallel participation channel**
- Not just Q&A — use chat for simultaneous idea-sharing
- Low-stakes entry for introverted students
- "All type your answer, hold Enter, release on my cue" — synchronous reveal prevents anchoring

**Interactivity extends attention beyond the "6-minute myth"**
- Embedded polls and questions increased completion rates by 20%+ and extended engagement
- But sessions >15 minutes without interaction still see steep attention drops

---

### Practical patterns for online delivery

| Rule | Basis |
|---|---|
| Break every 10–12 min with poll, typed response, or chat prompt | Attention research + interactivity data |
| Use chat as parallel channel, not only Q&A | Introverted learner access; weak verbal/visual correlation |
| Breakout rooms in groups of 4–6 with visible prompt | ZPD scaffolding; prompt disappears when slides move |
| Establish camera norms in session 1; model it; don't mandate | Peer cascade effect; camera ≠ engagement |
| Warm up cold-calls online: "I'll come to you in 30 seconds" | Wait-time research extrapolated to online |
| Always debrief after breakout: groups report back to main room | Participation scaffolding loop |
| Hard cap on live sessions: 60 minutes or insert a full break | 15-minute attention cliff + Zoom fatigue |
