### What it is

The ZPD (Vygotsky, 1930s) is the distance between what a learner can do *alone* and what they can do *with knowledgeable guidance*.

Instruction pitched below the ZPD wastes time. Instruction pitched above it causes frustration with no learning.

The teacher's job: operate precisely inside the ZPD, then fade support as competence grows.

---

### Scaffolding

<mark style="background: yellow">**Scaffolding**</mark> (Wood, Bruner & Ross, 1976 — applying Vygotsky) is the set of temporary supports that make the ZPD stretch possible:

- Demonstrations and modeling
- Partial completion of tasks
- Think-aloud prompts
- Graduated hints rather than direct answers

<mark style="background: pink">**Fading is mandatory.**</mark> Scaffolding without fading creates dependency, not competence. Supports must be removed as the learner internalizes the capability.

---

### Prerequisite knowledge's role

Before scaffolding can begin, you must assess the learner's current independent floor.

If content has no connection to any prior knowledge, it lies *outside* the ZPD entirely — the ZPD has no lower wall to scaffold from.

A missed prerequisite doesn't just slow learning. It prevents the ZPD from forming for dependent concepts.

---

### Application to sequencing

- Diagnose independent performance floor before teaching — not just "what level are they"
- Each new concept should sit a ZPD-sized gap above the learner's current capability
- Map prerequisites explicitly so no step has a gap that's too wide
- Continuously reassess: as learners grow, the ZPD shifts upward
