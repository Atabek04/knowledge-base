The dominant expert read of AI in software (2025-2026) is not replacement but *transformation*: the work moves up a level, from producing code to directing and owning it.

This is the developer-craft counterpart to the macro finding that [[AI displaces workers where it automates and spares them where it augments|AI augments more than it automates]] — here the augmentation reshapes the day-to-day job itself.

---

### What the job becomes

Thoughtworks (Dec 2025) frames it cleanly: software work becomes <mark style="background: yellow">less about writing code from scratch and more about defining, reviewing, testing, and orchestrating systems</mark>.

The developer becomes a <mark style="background: cyan;">steward of systems — accountable for reliability, performance, and value</mark>, not just the author of lines.

Gartner projects ~75% of developers will be orchestrating rather than hand-writing code by the end of 2026.

---

### The four shifted activities

- <mark style="background: green">Define</mark> — turn fuzzy intent into a precise specification the model can execute.
- <mark style="background: green">Review</mark> — judge machine-written code that looks correct even when it isn't.
- <mark style="background: green">Test</mark> — verify behavior against intent, not just against syntax.
- <mark style="background: green">Orchestrate</mark> — compose models, tools, and components into a working system.

The skill that survives is judgment about *what* and *whether*, not speed at typing *how*.

---

### The honest caveat

<mark style="background: pink">The "steward / orchestrator" framing is partly aspirational vendor thought-leadership.</mark> It is the prevailing expert opinion, not a measured fact — and business API usage still skews heavily toward automation, not augmentation.
