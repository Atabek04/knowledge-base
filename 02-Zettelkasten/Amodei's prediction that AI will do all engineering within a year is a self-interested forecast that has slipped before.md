The loudest replacement claim comes from a CEO selling the product. That doesn't make it false — but it is exactly the kind of forecast to weigh skeptically, and this one has a track record of slipping.

It is a concrete instance of the general pattern that [[AI CEOs have an incentive to overstate job replacement|AI CEOs overstate job replacement]].

---

### The claim

At Davos (Jan 2026) Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei said AI might be <mark style="background: yellow">"six to 12 months away" from doing "most, maybe all" of what software engineers do end-to-end</mark>, and that some Anthropic engineers already "don't write any code anymore — I just let the model write it, I edit it."

The attribution is solid; the quotes are verified across multiple outlets.

---

### Three reasons to discount the *forecast* (not the quote)

- <mark style="background: pink">Track record</mark> — he made a similar March 2025 claim ("90% of code in 3-6 months") that observers say did not materialize as stated.
- <mark style="background: pink">Conflict of interest</mark> — he is the CEO of the company selling the coding model.
- <mark style="background: pink">Anecdotal evidence</mark> — "engineers say" is self-reported, not measured.

#### Peers disagree

DeepMind's Demis Hassabis and Meta's Yann LeCun publicly push back on replacement timelines. <mark style="background: cyan;">A forward-looking, commercially-motivated opinion is not the same as a measured outcome</mark> — and the strongest *evidence* (METR, Anthropic's own RCTs) is about skill effects, not imminent full replacement.
