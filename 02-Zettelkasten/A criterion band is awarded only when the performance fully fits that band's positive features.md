Each Writing and Speaking criterion is a column of bullet points per band. The notes printed at the foot of the official Speaking descriptors state the rule the whole rubric runs on: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">a candidate must fully fit the positive features of the descriptor at a particular level</mark>.

This is a floor rule, not an average. Meeting two of three band-8 bullets and one band-6 bullet does not produce a band 7 by averaging — <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the lowest bullet you fail drags the criterion down to the band you do fully fit</mark>.

The practical reading: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">band 8 is limited by your worst habit, not raised by your best sentence.</mark> One recurring article error, one paragraph without a central topic, or one unclear overview holds the criterion at 7 regardless of how strong the rest is.

The second note adds that Speaking is rated on average performance across all three parts, so a strong Part 2 does not offset a weak Part 3.

Source: [ielts.org — Speaking band descriptors (public version), notes (i) and (ii)](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)
