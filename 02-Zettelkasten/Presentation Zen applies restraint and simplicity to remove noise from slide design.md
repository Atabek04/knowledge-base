### What it is

Presentation Zen (Garr Reynolds, 2008) is a design philosophy rooted in Japanese Zen aesthetics — *wabi-sabi* (beauty in simplicity), *ma* (meaningful empty space), *ikigai* (purpose and authenticity) — applied to slide presentations.

Its core claim: most slides are cluttered, text-heavy, and trying to do too many jobs at once.

---

### The three principles

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Restraint** | In content selection — say less; include less; cut ruthlessly |
| **Simplicity** | In design — one idea per slide; white space is not empty, it is clarity |
| **Naturalness** | In delivery — speak conversationally; make genuine eye contact; do not read slides |

---

### The slideument problem

A "slideument" is a hybrid that tries to be both a projected slide AND a standalone reading document.

<mark style="background: pink">It fails at both jobs:</mark>
- As a **slide:** Too much text competes with the speaker's words — violates [[Mayer's Multimedia Learning Principles reduce cognitive load through twelve evidence-based design rules|Mayer's redundancy principle]]
- As a **document:** No context, no transitions, no argument — impossible to read without the speaker

**The fix:** three separate artifacts:
1. **Slides** — sparse visuals for projection only
2. **Speaker notes** — your full script
3. **Handout** — a real document with prose and context

---

### Key design rules

1. <mark style="background: yellow">One idea per slide.</mark> If you need to say three things, use three slides.
2. Replace bullets with visuals, sequences, or spoken narration
3. Use full-bleed images — the image should *be* the slide, not decorate it
4. Six words per slide as a forcing function (Seth Godin's rule — a discipline, not a hard law)
5. Every element is signal or noise — remove noise ruthlessly
6. Empty space is a design tool: breathing room signals confidence and guides the eye

### Relation to cognitive science

Reynolds explicitly grounds these rules in [[Cognitive load theory explains why working memory bottlenecks determine learning speed|CLT]]: "People cannot read and listen well at the same time."

The overlap with Mayer is near-complete:
- "Remove clutter" = Coherence Principle
- "One idea per slide" = Segmenting Principle
- "Big images, no text" = Modality + Redundancy Principles
- "Picture superiority" = [[Dual coding theory shows that pairing images with verbal explanation creates stronger memory traces|Dual Coding Theory]]'s imagery advantage

Presentation Zen is Mayer and Paivio's findings translated into practitioner design language.
