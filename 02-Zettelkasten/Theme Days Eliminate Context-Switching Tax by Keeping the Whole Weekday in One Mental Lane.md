Jack Dorsey ran Twitter and Square simultaneously by assigning each weekday a single theme: Monday for management, Tuesday for product and engineering, Wednesday for marketing and communications, Thursday for partnerships, Friday for culture.

Every meeting, decision, and message on a given day fit one mental domain.

Context-switching research shows that moving between cognitively distinct tasks costs up to 40% of productive time — not from the switch itself, but from the warm-up cost of re-entering the prior state of mind.

---

### How Theme Days Handle Urgent Interruptions

Most interruptions are not truly timeless emergencies — they belong to a category.

A marketing question that arrives on Tuesday (product day) gets logged for Wednesday.

A partnership request that arrives on Monday (management day) gets routed to Thursday.

The theme acts as a **natural triage filter**: instead of deciding whether to drop what you're doing, you ask "does this fit today's theme?"

If yes, absorb it. If no, schedule it — and return to the lane you were already in.

---

### The Cognitive Physics Behind It

Staying in one mental domain lets you **compound context** across the day.

Each conversation, document, and decision you handle in the morning makes the afternoon easier because the relevant knowledge is already active.

Switching domains resets that accumulation.

This is why Dorsey described theme days as letting him see each company "in a broader light instead of getting caught up in day-to-day distractions."

---

### Who Benefits Most

Theme days are most effective for knowledge workers managing multiple projects, responsibilities, or companies.

For individual contributors with one primary project, [[Time-Blocking With Buffer Slots Survives Surprises by Refusing to Fill the Whole Day]] and [[Timeboxing Assigns a Fixed Duration to Each Task So Time Pressure Replaces Open-Ended Drift]] provide finer-grained control within a single domain.

---

### Mark Forster's Variation

Mark Forster (author of *Do It Tomorrow*) proposed a similar system: batch all same-type tasks on the same day so your brain stays in one mode.

Where Dorsey organized by business function, Forster organized by task type — administrative, creative, meetings.

Both share the same core logic: deliberate monotony at the day level produces depth that variety cannot.

---

### Implementing Without a C-Suite Calendar

You do not need two companies to use this.

A student or developer might theme: Monday (coding), Tuesday (study/reading), Wednesday (communication and admin), Thursday (project work), Friday (review and planning).

The key is committing to the theme for the full day and routing off-theme items to their correct day rather than handling them immediately.

This transforms interruptions from derailments into **rescheduled items** — preserving the current day's coherence.
