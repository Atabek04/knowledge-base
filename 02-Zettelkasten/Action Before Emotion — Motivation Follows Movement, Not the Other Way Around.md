Waiting to "feel motivated" before starting is the most common reason plans fail. The assumption is: motivation → action. The reality is the opposite.

**Action → momentum → motivation.**

The feeling of motivation arrives *after* you start, not before.

## The Neuroscience

The brain has two systems: the prefrontal cortex (rational, long-term) and the limbic system (emotional, comfort-seeking). In the moment of decision, the limbic system almost always wins — unless you act before it can process the decision.

Mel Robbins' 5-Second Rule exploits this: count 5-4-3-2-1 and move physically before the brain completes the avoidance calculation.

## Practical Application

- Don't ask "do I feel like it?" — ask "is it time?"
- The cue fires → you move → motivation builds after 2–3 minutes
- Remove all friction between the cue and the first physical action (book already open, clothes already laid out)
