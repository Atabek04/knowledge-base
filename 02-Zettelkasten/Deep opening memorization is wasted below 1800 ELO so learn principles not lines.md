### The rule

Below roughly 1800, <mark style="background: yellow">memorizing deep opening lines is largely wasted time — learn opening *principles* and typical plans instead.</mark>

Develop pieces, control the center, castle early, don't move the same piece twice, connect the rooks. These need zero memorization and work fine below master level.

---
### Why

Games at this level rarely turn on opening theory — they turn on a [[Below roughly 1800 ELO chess games are decided by tactics not strategy or openings|tactic in the middlegame]].

<mark style="background: pink">Memorized lines also collapse the moment the opponent deviates — which amateurs do constantly.</mark>

And the payoff inverts later: as your tactics improve, openings become much easier to learn anyway.

---
### When openings finally pay off

Around 1600–1800+, a real repertoire with concrete lines starts to matter — but even then, <mark style="background: cyan">depth beats breadth.</mark>
See [[A small chess opening repertoire kept for a year beats collecting many openings]].
