GOST (Государственный Стандарт, State Standard) is a family of Russian cryptographic standards.
These algorithms provide cryptographic sovereignty independent of Western standards like RSA and SHA.

The GOST standards were developed by the Russian Federal Agency on Technical Regulating and Metrology.
They serve as mandatory cryptographic algorithms for Russian government and critical infrastructure.

**GOST R 34.10** is the digital signature standard based on elliptic curve cryptography.
It provides functionality equivalent to ECDSA but uses different curves and parameters.

**GOST R 34.11** is the cryptographic hash function standard called Streebog.
It produces 256-bit or 512-bit hash values similar to SHA-256 and SHA-512.

**GOST 28147-89** was the original block cipher standard, later replaced by Kuznyechik.
This symmetric encryption algorithm served as Russia's equivalent to DES and AES.

Kazakhstan adopted GOST algorithms as its national cryptographic standards.
All qualified electronic signatures in Kazakhstan must use GOST R 34.10-2015.

The use of GOST algorithms prevents dependency on cryptographic systems controlled by other nations.
Countries can maintain cryptographic sovereignty by developing and mandating their own standards.

GOST algorithms have been analyzed by international cryptographers.
While initially treated with skepticism, modern GOST versions are considered cryptographically sound.
