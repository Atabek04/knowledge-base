### The finding

Captions have a medium positive effect on L2 acquisition overall (g = 0.56, Kurokawa et al. 2025 meta-analysis). But caption *type* determines the magnitude of that effect.

<mark style="background: yellow">Same-language captions</mark> — subtitles in the target language rather than the learner's L1 — produce consistently larger acquisition gains than translated captions, particularly for vocabulary form encoding and listening-skill development.

---

### Why same-language outperforms translated

Translated captions provide comprehension quickly but reduce exposure to the L2 form. Eye-tracking research (Wang 2022) shows learners fixate primarily on the L1 translation when both options are equally salient — meaning the L2 written form never receives the sustained visual processing that drives acquisition.

Same-language captions force three things simultaneously:
- Orthographic-phonological mapping (connecting the written form to the spoken sound)
- Word segmentation in the continuous speech stream
- Form recognition in context, at the moment of meaningful use

---

### Proficiency moderation

Proficiency is a significant moderator. Lower-proficiency learners benefit most from same-language captions — particularly glossed or keyword captions — because the written form helps them segment and identify words they cannot yet parse from audio alone.

Higher-proficiency learners benefit from same-language captions primarily for unfamiliar lexical items: the caption catches words their listening speed cannot yet process in real-time.

Translated captions remain appropriate for comprehension-focused viewing (documentary content for background knowledge). For acquisition-focused instruction, same-language is the stronger default.

---

### Practical implementation

- Primary caption track: target language
- For beginner content: add inline L1 glosses only for target vocabulary items (not every word) — keyword captioning
- Follow [[Sequential L1-then-bilingual subtitle exposure balances comprehension scaffolding with target-language form encoding|sequential viewing protocol]] when a second pass is feasible
