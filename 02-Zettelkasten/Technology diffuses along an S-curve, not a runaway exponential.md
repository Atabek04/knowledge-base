### Innovation pace vs adoption pace

Doomsday timelines assume capability and impact rise together, exponentially.

But Narayanan and Kapoor argue the two run on **different clocks**:

<mark style="background: yellow">The tempo of change is set not by how fast technology improves, but by how fast it gets *adopted* — and adoption follows an S-curve.</mark>

---
### The four stages

Invention → product development → **diffusion** → **adaptation**.

The last two — organizations and people actually changing how they work — are slow, and they gate everything.

---
### The historical pattern

Electricity and the internet were transformative, yet their S-curves unfolded over **decades**.

Factory electrification only paid off once plants were *redesigned* around distributed power — not the moment the motor existed.

<mark style="background: green">Even now, under 7% of users adopt advanced reasoning models — the limit is behavioral, not capability.</mark>

---
### The takeaway

<mark style="background: pink">A capability existing is not the same as it being deployed at scale — and deployment, not invention, is what moves the labor market.</mark>
