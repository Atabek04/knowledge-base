> <mark style="background: yellow">The BRD (Business Requirements Document) is always the first formal document in requirements engineering. It captures the business case — not technical details.</mark>

---

### What the BRD contains

- **Business objectives** — what the organization wants to achieve
- **Project scope** — boundaries of what's in and out
- **Stakeholders** — who is involved and their roles
- **Constraints** — budget, timeline, regulatory limits
- **Success criteria** — how we measure if the project delivered value

---

### What the BRD does NOT contain

<mark style="background: pink">No technical detail. No UML. No database schemas. No API specs.</mark>

The BRD answers **"WHY are we building this?"** — not how or what exactly.

---

### Before the BRD — Phase 0

Before writing the BRD, there's a discovery phase: interview the sponsor, shadow users, review existing documentation.

An initial meeting with the owner/sponsor (not more than an hour) kicks this off. The analyst then drafts a **one-page scope statement** or project vision document — this is not the BRD itself, but its precursor.

---

### Signoff

<mark style="background: green">The BRD gets signed off by the client before any further work begins. It's the contractual agreement on what the project is about.</mark>
