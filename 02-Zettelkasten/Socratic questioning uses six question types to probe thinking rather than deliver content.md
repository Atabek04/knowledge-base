### What it is

Socratic questioning is systematic use of targeted questions to drive a student's thinking deeper — rather than delivering content directly.

The teacher asks; the student reasons. Questions reveal and develop understanding rather than simply transmit information.

---

### The 6 question types (Paul's taxonomy)

| Type | Purpose | Example questions |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Conceptual clarification** | Expose what the student actually means | "What do you mean by that?" / "Can you give an example?" |
| **2. Probing assumptions** | Surface unstated beliefs driving the claim | "What are you assuming?" / "Could that assumption be wrong?" |
| **3. Probing evidence** | Test the quality of support for a claim | "What evidence supports this?" / "What's a counter-example?" |
| **4. Exploring perspectives** | Show alternatives exist | "What would someone who disagrees say?" |
| **5. Probing implications** | Follow the logic to its conclusion | "If that were true, what would follow?" / "What happens next?" |
| **6. Meta questions** | Reflect on the inquiry itself | "Why do you think I asked this?" / "What does this question assume?" |

---

### How to use in practice

- Use Type 1 first to establish common ground before probing deeper
- Use Type 3 when claims are asserted without support
- Use Type 6 to close a discussion or shift the level of abstraction
- <mark style="background: pink">Don't ask all six types sequentially</mark> — pick the type that fits the current moment

### Connection to other frameworks

[[Think-Pair-Share gives every learner think time before committing to whole-class discussion|Think-Pair-Share]]'s Share phase is where Socratic questioning begins — once students have committed to an idea with a partner, targeted Socratic probing deepens it.

[[Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction describe ten behaviors common to the most effective teachers|Rosenshine's Principle 3]] (ask many questions to all students) is most powerful when the questions are Socratic, not factual recall only.

---

### Online vs offline

**Offline:** Write 2–3 Socratic questions on the board as Socratic seminar anchors; prompt students to question each other using the taxonomy.

**Online:** Post one probing question as a discussion thread anchor; require students to reply using a *different* question type from the original; use in synchronous video to guide real-time discussion.
