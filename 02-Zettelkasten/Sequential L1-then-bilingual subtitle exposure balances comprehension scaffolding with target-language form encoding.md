### The finding

Yuan et al. (2025, *British Journal of Educational Psychology*) tested four subtitle conditions on L2 learners watching the same video:
1. L1 only throughout
2. L2 only throughout
3. Bilingual throughout
4. **L1 first pass → bilingual second pass** (sequential)

The sequential condition produced the strongest outcomes: highest scores on word-form recall, meaning recall, and form-meaning association — combined with the lowest cognitive load ratings across all groups.

---

### Why it works

The two passes serve distinct cognitive functions:

<mark style="background: cyan">**Pass 1 (L1-only):**</mark> Builds a comprehension scaffold without cognitive overhead. The learner understands the full semantic content — meaning, context, pragmatics — while freed to attend to prosody and rhythm.

<mark style="background: cyan">**Pass 2 (bilingual):**</mark> Re-exposes the learner to L2 word forms in a now-semantically-understood context. The meaning is already mapped; working memory can be fully dedicated to the L2 form itself.

This directly targets the most resource-limited activity in vocabulary acquisition: encoding the L2 word's *form*. When meaning is already secured on pass 1, form receives full attention on pass 2.

---

### Single-pass compromise

When re-watching is unlikely, the best single-pass design is bilingual subtitles with L2 on top and L1 glosses only for target vocabulary items — not for every word. Glossing every word reintroduces the comprehension-vs-form split that sequential viewing resolves.

---

### Connection to contiguity

The bilingual pass is most effective when L2 and L1 are placed contiguously ([[Spatial contiguity between L2 target and translation eliminates split-attention cost in bilingual slide design|spatial contiguity]]) — both on the same subtitle card, L2 above, L1 immediately below.
