IELTS reports a band per section and an overall band. The overall band is the arithmetic mean of the four section bands, rounded to the nearest half band — and the rounding is <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">upward at both .25 and .75</mark>, so an average of 7.75 is reported as 8.0.

The consequence is arithmetic, not motivational: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">an overall 8.0 needs the four bands to sum to 31.0</mark>, not four 8s.

Listening 9 + Reading 8.5 + Speaking 7.5 + Writing 6.5 sums to 31.5 and reports as 8.0. Listening 8.5 + Reading 8.5 + Speaking 7.5 + Writing 6.5 sums to 31.0 and also reports as 8.0.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This inverts the usual study priority.</mark> Writing is the hardest band to raise, and Listening and Reading are the two sections where technique alone moves the score — so the cheapest route to 31 points is to push the objectively-marked sections to their ceiling and let Writing be the section that is merely adequate.
