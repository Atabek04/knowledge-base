Automated essay scoring improves sharply when the model is given graded examples rather than a rubric alone: zero-shot agreement of 0.306 rises to 0.531 with six anchor essays. That is the single best-measured intervention available to a self-studier.

It also fixes the thing that makes the raw number unusable — <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the model's absolute scale moves when the provider ships a version update</mark>, with the same essays and the same prompt scoring differently across releases.

So the loop needs two or three of your own genuinely marked scripts, with their bands, pasted in as anchors. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Then the question is comparative, never absolute</mark>: where does this essay sit relative to my band 7 anchor, and on which criterion? Models order much better than they calibrate.

Paste the official public descriptors in as well. The model's internal rubric is roughly right but less detailed than the published one, and less detail means less discrimination at exactly the 7.0-to-8.0 boundary.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">The anchors have to come from a human</mark> — an early real sitting, an enquiry on results, or a paid marker. Without them there is no scale, and the feedback loop is measuring against nothing.

Source: [Song et al. (2024), few-shot anchoring in AES](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1075293526000413) · [Koraishi (2024)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1457168.pdf)
