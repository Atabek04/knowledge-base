### Where the advantage holds

AI is strongest at *implementation* — turning a clear spec into output.

Economist Bharat Chandar points to three areas where humans keep an edge:

- **Physical tasks** — embodiment is hard and expensive (short-to-medium term).
- **Strategic thinking** — deciding *what* to do and *why*, not just executing.
- **Social interaction** — trust, persuasion, care, negotiation.

<mark style="background: yellow">Roughly 30% of workers — cooks, mechanics, bartenders — show near-zero AI exposure for exactly these reasons.</mark>

---
### How to position

The move is to climb from implementation toward direction.

<mark style="background: cyan">Use AI to handle the implementation, and spend your effort on the strategic guidance that steers it.</mark>

---
### The honest caveat

"Short-to-medium term" matters — the physical edge depends on robotics being expensive, which may not last.

<mark style="background: pink">These are advantages of timing and cost, not permanent human monopolies.</mark>
