A to-do list tells you *what* to do. Timeboxing tells you *when* and *for how long*.

The difference is decisive: open-ended tasks expand to fill available time (Parkinson's Law), while a task with a fixed end-time creates natural urgency that overrides procrastination.

Marc Zao-Sanders, in a 2018 Harvard Business Review survey of 100 productivity techniques, found timeboxing ranked **#1 in perceived usefulness** — above systems like GTD, Pomodoro, and Eat the Frog.

---

### What Timeboxing Actually Is

You assign each task a calendar slot with a hard end time before the day begins.

Not "work on report" → but "work on report: 9:00–10:30."

When the box closes, you stop — or consciously choose to extend, which is itself a deliberate decision rather than accidental drift.

---

### Why It Handles Interruptions Better Than a To-Do List

A to-do list is orderless and timeless. When a surprise task appears, there is no obvious place to put it, so it either hijacks your attention or gets ignored with guilt.

A timeboxed calendar has visible open slots. You can see immediately: "I have a buffer block at 14:00 — that's where this goes."

This turns interruptions from invisible threats into **scheduled items**.

Zao-Sanders also notes that timeboxing improves team collaboration: when your calendar is visible, others can see your constraints and self-interrupt less often.

---

### The Commitment Mechanism

Timeboxing works partly through the same mechanism as [[Killing the Decision Removes Willpower from the Execution Equation]]: the decision about *when* to work was already made.

When 9:00 arrives, there is no question of whether to start — the box is there, the task is assigned.

This removes the daily tax of re-deciding.

---

### Key Principle: Fixed Duration, Not Fixed Outcome

Aim to complete the task in the box — but if you don't, the box still ends.

This is different from working until done. Incomplete work gets rescheduled into a new box, which forces better scoping over time: you learn how long tasks actually take, not how long you wish they took.

---

### Relationship to Buffer Blocks

Timeboxing and buffer blocks are complementary.

Timeboxing structures the work; buffer blocks absorb the unexpected.

Together they form a schedule that is both committed and resilient.
