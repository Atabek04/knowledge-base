Skipping breaks does not just cause tiredness. It causes measurable physical degradation in the brain's blood supply — and that has direct consequences for attention, memory, and decision-making.

---

### The Mechanism

During prolonged sitting, blood pools in the lower limbs.
Cerebrovascular blood flow — the circulation that delivers oxygen and glucose to the brain — **decreases**.

A 2018 PubMed study measured cerebral blood flow in participants who sat continuously for 3 hours and found significant decline, which was reversed by short walking breaks every 30 minutes.

Lower cerebral blood flow → reduced oxygen delivery → degraded prefrontal cortex function → impaired attention, working memory, and executive control.

This is not metaphor. It is a vascular process.

---

### What Degrades First

The prefrontal cortex — responsible for planning, sustained attention, impulse control, and complex reasoning — is the first to feel reduced blood flow.

Practically: the tasks that require the most cognitive effort become harder precisely when you've been sitting longest and working hardest. The longer you skip the break, the worse the tool you're using to do the work.

---

### The Cognitive Cost of Pushing Through

The instinct to skip a break because "there's still so much to do" is self-defeating.

What actually happens when you push through the [[Vigilance decrement causes sustained attention and accuracy to degrade within 20 minutes of monotonous focus|vigilance decrement]] without a break:

- Error rate increases (you produce work that needs rework)
- Decision quality drops (you accept worse options)
- Re-reading and re-doing multiply actual time spent
- Recovery takes longer because fatigue has compounded

One well-timed 5-minute movement break produces more net output than 5 additional minutes of degraded work.

---

### The Physical Break Requirement

Not all breaks equally reverse the blood flow effect.

Passive sitting in a different chair does not restore cerebrovascular flow.
**Light physical movement** does — standing, walking 2–3 minutes, stretching legs.

The 2022 Springer review found: interrupting sitting with low-to-moderate physical activity every 30–60 minutes improved multiple cognitive markers, including attention and executive function, compared to uninterrupted sitting.

This is why "take a walk" is not motivational advice. It is vascular intervention.

---

### Recommended Pattern

- Movement break every **30–60 minutes** of seated work
- Minimum **2–3 minutes** of walking or standing
- Aligns with the [[Pomodoro session cycle alternates four timed focus blocks with short breaks and one long recovery break|Pomodoro short break]] — use the 5-minute Pomodoro break to move, not to sit in a different position

For deeper restoration across a full work session, combine movement breaks with the [[Restorative breaks recover directed attention by shifting the brain to involuntary attention through low-demand activity|restorative break protocol]].
