aliases: [Chinese Room, Searle's Chinese Room]

John Searle's 1980 thought experiment in "Minds, Brains, and Programs" (Behavioral and Brain Sciences) attacks the behaviorist foundation of the Turing Test directly.

Imagine a person locked in a room. Chinese symbols are passed in. The person follows a rulebook to produce correct Chinese symbols as output — but understands no Chinese whatsoever.

From outside, the room behaves like a fluent Chinese speaker. From inside, nothing is understood.

Searle's conclusion: syntax (symbol manipulation by rule) never produces semantics (meaning). Behavior without comprehension is not thinking.

Applied to LLMs: a model that predicts the next token according to learned statistical patterns may produce correct outputs while understanding nothing. The Chinese Room is running at scale.

The "Systems Reply" counters that maybe the *system as a whole* understands, even if the person inside doesn't. Searle rejects this: you could internalize the whole rulebook, and still understand nothing.
