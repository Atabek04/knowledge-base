### The finding

Mayer's <mark style="background: yellow">Coherence Principle</mark> predicts that adding interesting but irrelevant audio to an instructional video harms learning by occupying the phonological loop — the same resource that processes verbal instruction.

A 2022 controlled study in medical education confirmed it with a **d = 0.48** detrimental effect. Crucially, music made the content *feel less demanding*, inducing a false-fluency effect that reduced effortful processing while lowering actual performance.

---

### Why language instruction is especially vulnerable

For language learning, the verbal channel is the primary learning pathway. Background music with lyrics competes directly for the same phonological resources needed to decode and store new vocabulary, grammar patterns, and spoken form.

A 2025 study (Ding et al., *Applied Cognitive Psychology*) confirmed music with lyrics damages both L1 and L2 reading, with <mark style="background: pink">L2 readers more severely impaired</mark> — because L2 processing already demands more phonological resources than processing a known language.

The same principle applies to any instruction that is heavy on verbal content: narrated code walkthroughs, lecture recordings, explainer videos.

---

### The false-fluency trap

The false-fluency effect is particularly dangerous: learners in music conditions report *higher* confidence while performing *worse*. Subjective enjoyment and reported engagement are unreliable signals of learning when competing audio is present.

This means post-session learner satisfaction surveys cannot distinguish "felt good" from "learned well" when music is present.

---

### In practice

<mark style="background: pink">No background music during instructional segments.</mark>

Brief musical stingers (1–2 seconds) at section transitions are acceptable — they signal structure without competing for phonological processing.

The same logic applies to ambient sound effects and anything auditory that is unrelated to the instructional content.
