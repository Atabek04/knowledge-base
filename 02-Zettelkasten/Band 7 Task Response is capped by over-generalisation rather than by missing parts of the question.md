Candidates who reach band 7 in Task Response have usually already solved the obvious failure — they address all parts of the task and hold a clear position throughout. Those are both band-7 bullets, and band 6 is where "some parts more fully covered than others" lives.

The band-7 bullet that stops the score going higher is the third one: ideas are presented, extended and supported, <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">but there may be a tendency to over-generalise and/or supporting ideas may lack focus</mark>.

Band 8 replaces this with a well-developed response carrying relevant, extended and supported ideas.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">The 7 → 8 move on Task Response is therefore about the specificity of support, not about coverage or structure.</mark> A body paragraph that asserts a claim, restates it in different words, and closes is over-generalised; the same paragraph with one named mechanism, consequence or concrete case is developed.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This is also why template essays plateau at 7.</mark> A template guarantees coverage and position — the two things band 7 already has — and does nothing for the bullet that is actually blocking.

There is no excuse for staying general, either. IDP states that statistics and facts <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">"don't have to be accurate in the context of the IELTS exam"</mark> — examiners assess whether support is relevant and correctly used, not whether it is true. So a specific invented figure carries no accuracy risk, while the vague sentence it replaces carries a real Task Response cost.
