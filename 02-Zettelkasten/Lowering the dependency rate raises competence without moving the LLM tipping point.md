Solé et al. (2026) ask which policy levers keep a population thinking for itself in their [[Regular LLM use is not dependency until the coupling persists and replaces the user's own thinking|three-state LLM dependency model]]. Each lever changes one rate, and the levers do different jobs.

Source: Solé et al. (2026), *Large-Language Models as a Cognitive Virus*, arXiv preprint, [arXiv:2609.03344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03344).

---

### Cognitive immunization

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Cognitive immunization is resistance to dependency-producing use that still allows beneficial use.</mark>

The name borrows from vaccines: the goal is not zero contact with the virus, but contact that does not lead to the disease.

---

### Levers that move the tipping point

#### Reducing adoption pressure

Lowering $\lambda$, for example by keeping LLMs out of settings where original thought is the product (exams, journalism, research writing), stops the [[A social reinforcement term makes LLM dependency easier to prevent than to reverse|tipping point]] from being crossed. After lock-in it must fall further, below the recovery threshold.

#### Encouraging disengagement

Raising $\rho$, the rate at which regular users return to non-use, pushes the invasion threshold up. As $\rho$ approaches $\kappa$, the bistable trap disappears and the transition becomes smooth and reversible.

#### Reinforcement cuts both ways

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Stronger social reinforcement of autonomous practice delays the flip but widens the trap once it happens, so the paper balances it against the return rate instead of maximising it.</mark>

---

### Levers that protect competence

Lowering $\mu$ (fewer regular users harden into dependency) or raising $\sigma$ (more dependent users recover) leaves both thresholds where they were.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">The dependent share of LLM users settles at μ divided by (μ + σ), so these levers decide how many users still think for themselves, not whether adoption happens.</mark>

This is the "firewall" option: adoption can be high while users keep verifying, reasoning actively and recovering from dependence. In practice it means deliberate [[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency|desirable difficulty]], keeping the hard thinking for yourself as in [[Effective AI learning removes friction from busywork and adds friction to thinking|friction placed on thinking]].

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">These are properties of a theoretical model, not tested interventions; no trial yet shows which lever works in a real population.</mark>
