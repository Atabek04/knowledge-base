When an interruption hits, your brain has to make a real-time decision under pressure: stop what I'm doing or push through?

That decision costs willpower, time, and often the current task.

Implementation intentions, developed by psychologist Peter Gollwitzer, remove that decision entirely.

### The Format

"If X happens, then I will do Y."

You write out the interruption scenario in advance — and you write out exactly what you'll do when it happens.

Examples:
- "If a colleague asks me something while I'm in a focus block, I'll say: 'I'm finishing something — can we talk at 3pm?'"
- "If I get an urgent Slack message during work, I'll flag it and return to it after the current session ends."
- "If I feel the urge to check my phone, I'll write it down and keep going."

### Why It Works

The plan is already decided.

When the cue appears (the interruption), the pre-committed response fires automatically — without negotiation, without debate, without willpower drain.

Gollwitzer's meta-analysis of 642 independent tests showed that implementation intentions increase follow-through 2-3x compared to having a goal alone.

The effect is especially strong in high-pressure situations where self-regulation would otherwise fail — exactly the conditions interruptions create.

---

### Linking to Existing Decisions

Implementation intentions are most powerful when the response is already drilled into a habit.

The moment you notice the interruption is just the cue — the if-then script handles the rest.

This works because the brain delegates the response to the situation itself, not to your in-the-moment judgment.

This is the same mechanism behind [[Killing the Decision Removes Willpower from the Execution Equation]] — pre-deciding eliminates the decision cost at the worst possible moment.

---

### Practical Setup

Write 3 specific if-then plans before any focus session:
1. What happens if someone interrupts me physically?
2. What happens if I get an urgent digital notification?
3. What do I do when I feel internally pulled to switch tasks?

Review them once before you start. That single rehearsal activates the trigger.
