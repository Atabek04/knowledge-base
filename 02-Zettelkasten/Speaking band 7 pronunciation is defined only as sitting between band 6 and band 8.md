Three of the four Speaking criteria describe every band with its own bullets. Pronunciation does not. At the odd bands the descriptor is a pointer: band 7 <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">displays all the positive features of band 6, and some, but not all, of the positive features of band 8</mark>. Band 5 and band 3 are worded the same way against their neighbours.

So there is nothing to study *at* band 7 for pronunciation. The only two definitions that exist are the ones on either side, and the work is to close the specific gaps between them.

Band 6 is variable control, chunking generally appropriate but rhythm affected by lack of stress-timing or rapid speech, intonation and stress used effectively but <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">not sustained</mark>, some phonemes mispronounced.

Band 8 is a wide range of phonological features conveying precise or subtle meaning, <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">sustained appropriate rhythm</mark>, flexible stress and intonation across long utterances, easily understood throughout.

The repeated discriminator is sustaining, not accuracy — band 6 already achieves the features occasionally.

Source: [ielts.org — Speaking band descriptors (public version), Pronunciation column](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)
