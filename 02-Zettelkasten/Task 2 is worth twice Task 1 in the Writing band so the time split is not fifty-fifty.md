The Writing test is 60 minutes for two tasks, and the two tasks do not count equally: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Task 2 contributes twice as much as Task 1 to the Writing band</mark>. The official advice is to spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and about 40 on Task 2.

Minimum lengths are 150 words for Task 1 and 250 for Task 2. The official framing of the short-answer risk is not a fixed deduction but an evidence problem: <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">a short answer gives too little evidence of the language features the higher bands require</mark>. Writing over the minimum is not itself penalised.

Run the arithmetic before concluding Task 1 can be sacrificed, though. A 2:1 weighting means the Writing band is roughly (Task 1 + 2 × Task 2) ÷ 3. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Task 1 at 7.0 with Task 2 at 8.0 gives 7.67, which reports as 7.5</mark> — so a strong Task 2 cannot carry a neglected Task 1 to an 8. Reaching Writing 8 needs roughly 7.5 and 8, or 8 and 8.

Two consequences that candidates get wrong in opposite directions:

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Doing Task 2 first is a defensible strategy</mark> — if time runs out, it should run out on the task worth one third, not the task worth two thirds.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Over-running on Task 1 is the more common failure and the more expensive one.</mark> Task 1 has a fixed amount to say; the extra minutes spent there produce no additional marks while removing the planning time that Task Response and Coherence in Task 2 depend on.
