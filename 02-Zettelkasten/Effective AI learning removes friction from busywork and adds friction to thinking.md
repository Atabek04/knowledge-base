The practical rule for learning with AI is about <mark style="background: yellow">where you place friction</mark>.

- <mark style="background: green">Low-impact busywork</mark> → remove friction, let AI do it
- <mark style="background: green">Skill-building thinking</mark> → add friction, keep it for yourself

This turns the abstract warning about [[Using AI to bypass work causes skill atrophy while using it for inquiry builds skill|skill atrophy]] into a daily decision.

---

### The gym-spotter model

A spotter does not lift the weight for you — it lets you push harder and safer.

Use AI the same way for transformation tasks:

1. Study the concept yourself first.
2. Ask AI to quiz you, then escalate difficulty.
3. Have it <mark style="background: cyan">challenge your assumptions and force you to defend answers</mark>.

The discomfort is the point — learning strengthens at the edge of ability.

---

### What to safely offload

For *information* tasks where effort is capped and adds no impact — reformatting, summarizing, first drafts, cleaning data — remove friction freely.

Be lazy where impact is capped; be obsessed where impact compounds.

---

### Stay the student

The biggest blocker is ego. AI is a safe space to ask "explain this like I'm ten" without judgment.

<mark style="background: purple">If you never feel foolish, you aren't stretching.</mark>
