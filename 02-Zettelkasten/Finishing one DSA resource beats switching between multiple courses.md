### The trap

Switching between resources feels like progress.
Each new course or book promises a better explanation.
In reality, switching resets momentum and creates shallow coverage of everything.

---
### The principle

Pick one structured DSA resource and follow it to completion.
Coverage gaps are real — depth is the goal, not breadth of sources.

Switching is acceptable only if the current resource has a fundamental flaw (wrong explanations, missing topics), not just because another resource looks appealing.

---
### Why it works

A single resource builds a connected mental model — concepts refer back to each other.
Switching mid-course breaks those connections before they solidify.

This pairs with [[DSA mastery comes from recognizing patterns not memorizing individual solutions|pattern-based practice]]: patterns compound only when you finish a structured sequence.
