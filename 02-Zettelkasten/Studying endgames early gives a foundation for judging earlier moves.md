### Capablanca's argument

<mark style="background: yellow">Study endgames early, because the endgame is the one phase you can master in isolation.</mark>

Openings and middlegames must be understood *in relation to* the endgames they lead to — but an endgame can be understood by itself.

---
### Why this is leverage

If you know where positions ultimately resolve, you have a <mark style="background: cyan">yardstick for evaluating earlier decisions</mark> — "is this trade good?" often means "is the resulting endgame won?"

Beginners routinely throw away winning games by not knowing basic conversions: K+Q vs K, K+R vs K, the two-rook ladder mate, and king-and-pawn opposition.

---
### What to actually study

Learn only the endgames you need *at your current rating* — see [[Learn the endgames for your rating level not exhaustive endgame theory]].

<mark style="background: green">Foundational set first: basic mates, the opposition, the rule of the square, the passed-pawn breakthrough.</mark>
Rook endings (Lucena, Philidor) become important around 1600+.

<mark style="background: pink">This is a strong heuristic from great players, not a controlled study — treat the *magnitude* of benefit as advice, not proof.</mark>
