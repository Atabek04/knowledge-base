When you write a detailed plan, your brain treats it as partial progress. The prefrontal cortex simulates the future outcome — and the reward system fires dopamine as if you've already started.

This is why planning feels productive even when nothing gets done. The satisfaction of a finished plan *substitutes* for the satisfaction of execution.

## Why This Traps You

- Elaborate planning creates a false sense of accomplishment
- The motivation peak arrives during planning, not during action
- By the time execution moment comes, motivation has already discharged

## The Fix

Keep plans short and ugly. A plan that takes 5 minutes to write forces you to spend the remaining time executing, not refining.

**Rule:** If your plan is satisfying to look at, it's probably too detailed.
