There are two ways to find the [[Slope measures how much y changes when x changes|slope]] of a curve at a point.

---

### Method 1: Calculate Derivative

**Use when:** You have the equation.

**Process:**
- Find [[Derivative measures how fast something is changing at a specific point|derivative]] algebraically
- Plug in x value
- Get exact slope

**Example:** For $y = x^2$ at x = 3:
- $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x$
- Slope = $2(3) = 6$

<mark style="background: green">Pros: Exact, fast</mark>
<mark style="background: #FF5582A6">Cons: Need equation</mark>

---

### Method 2: Draw Tangent Line Visually

**Use when:** You only have a graph (no equation).

**Process:**
1. Pick point on curve
2. Mentally zoom in until curve looks straight
3. Draw [[Tangent line touches curve at exactly one point and shows instantaneous slope|tangent line]] touching curve at that point (by eye)
4. Measure rise/run: $m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$

<mark style="background: green">Pros: Works without equation</mark>
<mark style="background: #FF5582A6">Cons: Approximate, depends on drawing skill</mark>

**Real-world use:** Lab data, experimental graphs

---

### Key distinction

- Method 1: **Calculation** → precise
- Method 2: **Visual estimation** → approximate
