Every Academic Task 1 visual type reduces to the same first decision, made before any sentence is written: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">what are the two or three groups, and on what axis are they grouped</mark> — by shared behaviour, by category, or by time period.

The failure mode is identical across types too: describing items in the order they appear on the page. One line at a time, one bar at a time, one table cell at a time. That is a Coherence and Cohesion problem as much as a Task Achievement one, because the reader gets no logical progression.

Per type, the grouping axis differs. Line graphs group lines that move alike. Bar charts group by trend or by category, never left to right. Pie charts pair the dominant and smallest segments across the charts. Tables use row and column extremes as the spine. Multiple charts get one body section each, but <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the overview must carry key features of both</mark> or the answer fails "covers all requirements". Process diagrams group by stage block and state whether the process is linear or a cycle. Maps group by what changed against what stayed the same.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Length is the diagnostic.</mark> A Task 1 answer running past roughly 200-215 words is usually reporting rather than grouping — the extra words are ungrouped detail, and there is no upper word limit to punish them, only the twenty minutes they steal from the double-weighted Task 2.
