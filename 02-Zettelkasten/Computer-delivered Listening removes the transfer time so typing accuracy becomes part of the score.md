On paper, Listening ends with ten minutes to copy answers onto the answer sheet — which is also ten minutes to fix spelling, reconsider plurals and count words. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Computer-delivered Listening has no transfer time</mark>, only a short check period of around two minutes.

You type answers while the recording plays. So the mechanical errors that cost whole marks — a busted word limit, a missing plural, a typo — now have to be avoided in real time rather than repaired afterwards. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Typing at audio speed is itself a trainable skill and should be practised as one</mark>, not discovered on the day.

Reading gains rather than loses. The screen is split with the passage left and questions right; text can be highlighted in colour and annotated with on-screen notes; navigation is free and answers auto-save. Font size and background colour are adjustable. A stopwatch sits at the top and flashes at ten and five minutes remaining. Reading has no transfer time in either format, so nothing is lost there.

Scratch paper is provided and is not marked. Computer-delivered results arrive in a few days against roughly thirteen for paper.

Not confirmed on any official page: in-passage copy-paste and a flag-for-review list. The confirmed equivalent is free navigation between questions.
