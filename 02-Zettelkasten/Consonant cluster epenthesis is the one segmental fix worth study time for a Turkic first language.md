Turkic languages prefer consonant-vowel syllable structure, so English consonant clusters get a vowel inserted to break them up — *sport* becomes *siport*, *strong* becomes *sitrong*. This is documented for both Uzbek and Turkish learners of English.

It is the costliest sound-level habit because <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">it does not merely alter a phoneme, it adds a syllable</mark>. The word's stress pattern changes, and the descriptors penalise disrupted rhythm directly.

The second L1 transfer worth naming is stress placement: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Turkic stress defaults to the final syllable, English stress is lexically fixed and variable</mark>. The fix is to learn stress with each new word rather than after it.

Missing phonemes — /θ/, /ð/, /ʒ/, and the ship/sheep vowel-length contrast — come last, and only where they produce a real minimal-pair confusion. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Consistently substituting these is compatible with band 8, because they do not cost intelligibility.</mark>
