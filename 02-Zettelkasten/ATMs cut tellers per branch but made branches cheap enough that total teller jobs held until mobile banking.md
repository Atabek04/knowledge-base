The bank teller is the most cited case of automation that did not destroy a job, and later did. Its two stages show both halves of the task model in one occupation.

### Glossary

- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"><b>ATM (automated teller machine)</b>: a cash machine that automates the teller's cash and deposit tasks.</mark>
- **Branch**: one local office of a bank, a *branch* off the main company.
- **Relationship banking**: a bank winning and keeping customers through personal contact and advice, not only transactions.
- **Mobile banking**: doing banking through a smartphone app instead of at a branch.

---

### Stage one: ATMs and more branches

ATMs spread in the US from the mid-1990s; over 400,000 are now installed. In the average urban market a branch needed about 20 tellers in 1988 and about 13 in 2004, yet total teller jobs did not fall ([Bessen, IMF F&D, 2015](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2015/03/bessen.htm)).

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">ATMs lowered the cost of running a branch, so banks opened more branches, and the extra branches offset the fewer tellers each one needed.</mark> Urban branches rose 43%.

#### Why branches still hired tellers

ATMs automated only two of the teller's tasks: dispensing cash and taking deposits. They could not advise a small business owner, sell a loan, or build trust.

Banks were competing for customers, and those remaining tasks won customers. So the teller job shifted from counting cash toward sales and relationships, the work ATMs could not do (Bessen).

---

### Stage two: mobile banking removes the branch

From about 2010, smartphone apps let customers do almost all their banking without visiting a branch. US branches per person peaked in 2009 and have fallen nearly 30% since. Full-time teller employment fell from about 332,000 in 2010 to 164,000 in 2022 ([David Oks](https://davidoks.blog/p/why-the-atm-didnt-kill-bank-teller)).

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A technology that automates some tasks inside a workplace can leave jobs intact; a technology that makes the workplace itself unnecessary removes every task at once.</mark>

The two sources count tellers differently (Bessen all tellers, Oks full-time only), so compare their trends, not their totals.
