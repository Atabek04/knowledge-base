The single biggest gap between elite AI users and everyone else is <mark style="background: yellow">how much specific context they provide</mark>.

Average users send vague prompts. Elite users front-load the model with rich, personal detail.

---

### What "rich context" means

Drew Bent points to three kinds of context that average users skip:

- <mark style="background: green">Personal documents</mark> — your notes, drafts, code, past work
- <mark style="background: green">Professional goals</mark> — what you're actually trying to achieve and why
- <mark style="background: green">Stream-of-consciousness thinking</mark> — your messy, unfiltered reasoning, not a cleaned-up question

The model cannot read your situation. Vague prompt in, generic answer out.

---

### Why this is the lever

A capable model with no context produces average output for *everyone* — it has nothing to specialize on.

The same model with your goals, your constraints, and your half-finished work produces something only useful to *you*.

<mark style="background: cyan">Context is the part the human supplies and the model cannot.</mark> That is exactly why it differentiates.

This is the fuel that makes the [[Treating AI as a collaborator rather than an assistant separates top learners|collaborator stance]] pay off.

---

### The skeptic's accidental agreement

Some argue "there's nothing to learn about AI — it's just a tool." But even that camp concludes output quality depends on <mark style="background: purple">domain knowledge and the context you can supply</mark> — which is this exact point.
