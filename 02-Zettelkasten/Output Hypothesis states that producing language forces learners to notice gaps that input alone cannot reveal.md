### The claim

Merrill Swain's Output Hypothesis (1985, 1995) challenges the sufficiency of Krashen's input-only model: *producing* language (speaking, writing) serves acquisition functions that comprehending input cannot replicate.

When learners attempt to produce output that exceeds their current ability, they notice the **gap** between what they want to say and what they can say. This gap-noticing triggers focused attention to the linguistic forms needed to close it.

---

### Three functions of output (Swain, 1995)

1. **Noticing/triggering function** — production failures reveal what the learner does not yet control
2. **Hypothesis-testing function** — output is an experiment: interlocutor feedback confirms or disconfirms a grammatical hypothesis
3. **Metalinguistic/reflective function** — discussing *how* to say something explicitly consolidates grammatical knowledge

---

### The evidence — French immersion

Swain's evidence came from French immersion students in Canada who received years of comprehensible French-medium instruction. Despite the massive input exposure, students remained grammatically inaccurate in production — fluent comprehenders, but persistent error-makers when speaking.

Input had brought them to functional comprehension. What they lacked was being *pushed* to produce beyond their comfort zone: **pushed output** — tasks that required grammatical precision they could not avoid.

---

### Implication for the silent period

Krashen's model supports a *silent period* before output — don't speak until ready. Swain's evidence suggests this delays the gap-noticing process that input alone cannot trigger.

<mark style="background: cyan">Some production failures are pedagogically valuable</mark>: they surface what the learner doesn't know in a way that passive comprehension never will.

---

### Connection to Noticing Hypothesis

Swain's output functions align with Schmidt's [[Noticing Hypothesis states that conscious attention to form is a prerequisite for converting input to intake|Noticing Hypothesis]]: output *creates* moments of conscious form attention that input comprehension skips. The two hypotheses describe different triggers for the same underlying mechanism.
