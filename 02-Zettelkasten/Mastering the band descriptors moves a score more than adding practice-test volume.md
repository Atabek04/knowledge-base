The recurring community consensus, across a year of r/IELTS threads sampled for a competitive analysis, is that <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the unlock is the band descriptors, not practice volume</mark>. Authentic reading and listening material is already free and abundant; the constraint is knowing what is being marked.

The peer-reviewed evidence points the same way from a different angle. Green (2005) found that among repeat candidates the initial score predicted the gain better than the length of the course did — so undirected volume is precisely the input that fails to convert.

The mechanism is visible in the rubric itself. A criterion is awarded only when the performance <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">fully fits</mark> that band's features, so a score is held down by one specific recurring habit. Another practice test re-measures that habit; it does not identify it.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This makes the practice test a measuring instrument, not a training method.</mark> The training happens between tests, on the named fault — and a test taken without a diagnosis of what to fix afterwards has produced a number and nothing else.
