### What it is

Backward Design (Wiggins & McTighe, *Understanding by Design*, 1998) builds lessons and units by starting with the **end goal** — what students should understand and be able to do — then working backwards to assessment, then to instruction.

This prevents "coverage-first" teaching where activities fill time without producing lasting understanding.

---

### The 3 stages

**Stage 1 — Desired Results**
What should students know, understand, and be able to do?

Identify *enduring understandings* (big ideas that outlast the unit) and *essential questions* that frame inquiry.

"Students will understand that… / Students will be able to…"

**Stage 2 — Acceptable Evidence**
What will count as proof of Stage 1?

<mark style="background: yellow">Design assessments *before* planning activities.</mark> This forces alignment and prevents the trap of teaching content that won't be assessed.

For enduring understanding: authentic assessments (case studies, projects, portfolios).
For knowledge/skill: quizzes, tests, direct observation.

**Stage 3 — Learning Plan**
Only now: what experiences, instruction, and activities get students to Stage 1 and prepare them for Stage 2?

Every activity must trace back to a desired result and an evidence target.

---

### Why starting from outcomes changes planning

Traditional design: choose content → choose activities → (sometimes) write a test.

Result: loosely connected activities that cover topics without producing coherent understanding.

Backward Design: define understanding → define proof → select instruction.

Result: every lesson answers "why are we doing this?" — traceable directly to Stage 1.

---

### Connection to Bloom's Taxonomy

Stage 1 learning objectives are written using [[Bloom's Taxonomy sequences learning objectives from recall to original creation|Bloom's verbs]].

The verb determines the cognitive level of Stage 2 evidence needed.

A Create-level objective requires a Create-level assessment (project, design). A Remember objective can be satisfied by a quiz.

---

### Online vs offline

**Offline:** Write the final assessment rubric before writing any lesson plans. Work backwards from the rubric to individual lessons.

**Online:** Map each module objective to a specific graded activity in the LMS. The gradebook structure *is* your Stage 2 scaffold.
