Yann LeCun (Turing Award winner, Meta Chief AI Scientist) argues that LLMs are a dead end for achieving general intelligence — not because they are wrong, but because they are missing foundational components.

His diagnosis: LLMs lack a world model — an internal simulation of how reality works causally and physically. Without it, a system cannot plan, cannot reason about consequences, and cannot generalize beyond linguistic surface patterns.

He identifies four missing properties in current LLMs:
- Persistent memory across interactions
- Causal reasoning about actions and effects
- Hierarchical planning over multiple steps
- A grounded model of the physical and social world

LeCun's proposed alternative is Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) — systems that learn compact representations of how the world changes, not how to predict raw tokens.

His position is not that AI can never think, but that the current dominant paradigm is architecturally insufficient for the task.
