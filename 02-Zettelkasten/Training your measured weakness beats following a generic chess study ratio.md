### The meta-rule

Published study splits ("50% tactics, 30% openings…") <mark style="background: pink">contradict each other</mark> — one coach says spend 5% on openings, another says 30%.

The rule that survives the disagreement: <mark style="background: yellow">weight your training toward where *your* games are actually being lost.</mark>

---
### Why ratios fail

A generic ratio guesses at the average player's weakness.
It can't know that *you* keep losing rook endgames or hanging your queen.

<mark style="background: cyan">The diagnostic loop overrides any fixed percentage:</mark> [[Analyzing your own games is the highest-ROI chess training activity|analyze your games]] → find the recurring error → train that specific gap.

---
### Using ratios anyway

Fixed splits are fine as a *starting default* before you have data — for sub-1800 the consensus direction is: <mark style="background: green">tactics and own-game analysis dominate, openings smallest.</mark>

But once you have a few analyzed games, replace the default with your measured leak.
Tools like Aimchess automate the measurement across six dimensions.
