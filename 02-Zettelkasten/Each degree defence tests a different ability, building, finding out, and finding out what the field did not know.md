A student who did well at bachelor level often fails the master's defence with the same kind of work, because the examiners are no longer asking the same question. The three degrees test three different abilities. This is academic convention rather than the claim of one text; Booth's split between reporting and [[Research is a written argument that something not previously known is now known and can be checked|arguing]] and Wieringa's split between building a [[Design science builds an artifact to test an idea and the knowledge from evaluating it is the contribution|treatment and evaluating it]] are what make the convention precise.

---

### The three questions

| Degree | Examiners ask | Graded on | Passes with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bachelor | can you build? | execution | a working system, sensibly written up |
| Master's | can you find out? | contribution and method | a checkable claim, tested by a defensible method |
| PhD | can you find out what the field did not know? | novelty confirmed by the field | publications: the field itself attests it did not know |

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">A bachelor project is graded on execution, a master's on contribution and method, a PhD on novelty the field has confirmed in print.</mark>

Each step keeps the previous ability as a prerequisite and stops rewarding it. A master's student still has to build, but building earns nothing on its own; a PhD student still has to find out, but finding out something the field already knew earns nothing.

---

### Why "we built a system" fails a master's

The report that describes a system answers the bachelor question. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">"We built a system" fails a master's defence not because the system is bad but because it answers a question the examiners stopped asking one degree ago.</mark> The system is evidence only when a claim about it is being tested; a chapter that describes it without that claim is a chapter with no reader role.

The practical consequence is a change of proportion. A master's dissertation describes the artifact only as far as the reader needs in order to judge the test: which mechanisms are separately switchable, how the measurement isolates what it claims to measure. The weight goes to the evaluation, the results and the threats to their validity. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">In a master's dissertation the artifact chapter earns its length only by what the evaluation needs from it; everything past that is a bachelor chapter in a master's binder.</mark>

---

### Why publications gate the PhD

"The field did not know" is a claim about other people, and only they can confirm it. Peer review at a recognised venue is the mechanism by which the field says, in writing, that the contribution is new to it. A PhD without publications has a private claim of novelty; the publication converts it into a public one.
