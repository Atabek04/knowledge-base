You cannot reason your way to knowing what AI can and can't do. You have to <mark style="background: yellow">do reps</mark> — many small, hands-on experiments.

Drew Bent's advice for staying current is exactly this: experiment constantly to feel where the current frontier actually sits.

---

### Why reps beat reading about AI

The capability frontier moves every few weeks. Any fixed mental model goes stale fast.

Reps keep your model fresh because you're testing the *current* tool, not last quarter's reputation of it.

<mark style="background: cyan">Intuition for the limits is itself the skill</mark> — knowing when to trust output and when to step in.

---

### What a rep looks like

- Hand AI a task you could verify yourself, then check where it broke
- Push a problem past where you expect it to fail, to find the real edge
- Re-prompt the same task differently and compare — learning from what you *don't* want

This is how you build the [[AI agent building is the foundational career skill of the next thirty years|agent-building literacy]], and it pairs with deliberately [[Effective AI learning removes friction from busywork and adds friction to thinking|placing friction on the thinking parts]].

---

### The honest caveat

Reps also surface failure modes: stochastic output, confident wrong answers, and degradation on large/complex context.

<mark style="background: pink">Reps teach trust *and* distrust</mark> — both are the point.
