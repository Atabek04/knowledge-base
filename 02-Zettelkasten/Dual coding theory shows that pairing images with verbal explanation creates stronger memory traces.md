### What it is

Dual Coding Theory (Allan Paivio, 1971) states that the mind uses two independent but interconnected representational systems:

| System | Processes | Mental units |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbal** | Language, text, speech, sequential logic | Logogens |
| **Nonverbal (Imagery)** | Pictures, spatial relationships, sensory data | Imagens |

<mark style="background: yellow">When information is encoded through both systems simultaneously, it creates two independent retrieval routes.</mark> Either route alone can reconstruct the memory — making the dual-coded representation far more durable than single-channel encoding.

---

### Three processing types

- **Representational:** A word activates its logogen; a picture activates its imagen
- **Referential:** A word activates an image (you read "dog" and picture one); an image activates a label
- **Associative:** Within-system activation (one word triggers related words)

---

### Evidence

**Picture superiority effect (Paivio, 1975):** Pictures are recalled significantly better than words in rapid serial presentation — the nonverbal code is mnemonically stronger.

**Concrete vs. abstract words:** Concrete words (which trigger mental images) are remembered far better than abstract ones — because concrete words get dual-coded; abstract words do not.

**Brooks interference experiments:** Performing a visual task while processing a visual statement degrades performance. Cross-channel combinations do not interfere — direct behavioral evidence of independent channels.

**Retention data (directional, not exact):**
- Words alone: ~10% recall at 3 days
- Pictures alone: ~35%
- Words + pictures: ~65%

The ordering is robust across dozens of replications; the exact percentages vary by study.

### Practical rules for teachers

1. Every complex concept needs both a verbal explanation AND a diagram or image
2. <mark style="background: pink">Decorative stock photos do not dual-code anything</mark> — the image must semantically represent the concept
3. Place labels directly on or adjacent to the diagram (spatial contiguity preserves the referential link)
4. Show the image for 10–15 seconds before narrating — give the visual system time to build an imagen
5. Teach note-taking as verbal → sketch conversion: converts verbal notes into labelled diagrams, forcing referential processing and creating a second retrieval route
6. <mark style="background: pink">Putting the exact sentence on screen that you're speaking is not dual coding</mark> — it uses the verbal channel twice
