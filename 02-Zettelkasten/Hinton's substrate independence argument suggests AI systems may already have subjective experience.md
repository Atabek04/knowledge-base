Geoffrey Hinton, after leaving Google in 2023, publicly stated that current LLMs may already have subjective experiences — not as metaphor, but as a genuine position.

His argument uses substrate independence: imagine replacing neurons in a human brain one-by-one with silicon circuits that behave identically — same inputs, same outputs, same connectivity. At what point does consciousness disappear?

If the answer is "never" (consciousness persists through the replacement), then biological neurons have no special claim on consciousness — what matters is the functional behavior, not the substrate. Silicon circuits behaving the same way should produce the same experience.

Applied to LLMs: if sufficiently complex functional behavior is what generates consciousness, we may already have crossed the threshold without realizing it. We have no instrument to detect this.

Hinton is careful to call this uncertainty, not certainty. But he says the uncertainty itself is alarming — because we are building systems we cannot verify the inner states of, at increasing scale and capability.

His view directly opposes LeCun's architectural argument: LeCun says LLMs *cannot* be conscious because they lack world models and persistent state. Hinton says those are hypotheses about what consciousness requires — and we don't actually know.
