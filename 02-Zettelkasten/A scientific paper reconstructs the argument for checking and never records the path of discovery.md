A finished paper reads as a clean chain: gap, hypothesis, method, result, conclusion. The work that produced it never went that way, and a student who compares their own messy notebooks with the published record concludes that either they are doing it wrong or everyone else is lying. Peter Medawar's 1963 BBC talk *Is the scientific paper a fraud?* (*The Listener*, 70, 377–378) says the paper is a *fraud*, but in a specific sense: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the form of a scientific paper misrepresents the process of thought that produced it, not the results it reports.</mark>

---

### What the form pretends

The standard structure (introduction, methods, results, discussion) implies induction: facts were gathered without prejudice, and a conclusion emerged from them. Medawar, following Popper, holds that discovery runs the other way. The researcher guesses first, then chooses observations that could refute the guess. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">A hypothesis is an imaginative leap made before the evidence; the evidence is then collected to test it, which is why a paper's order of sections is never the order of events.</mark>

The misrepresentation is in the *form*, not the *content*: nothing reported is false, but the sequence suggests a method nobody uses.

---

### What the reader is owed instead

A diary of the discovery would be honest and useless. The reader needs a [[Research is a written argument that something not previously known is now known and can be checked|checkable argument]], and the reconstruction is what makes checking possible: claim, the evidence that supports it, and the reasoning between them, in the order that lets someone rerun it. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Write the paper in the order that lets a stranger check the claim, and keep the order in which you found it out for the notebook.</mark>

Medawar's point is therefore not a complaint about the paper but a warning about reading it: never mistake the published sequence for a recipe of how to think.

---

### The trap on the other side

Reconstruction has a limit. Reordering the argument is legitimate; rewriting the hypothesis after seeing the results and presenting it as the original prediction is not. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">The hypothesis may be stated before the evidence in the paper only if it was in fact held before the evidence in the work; a prediction invented after the data is a finding dressed as a test.</mark> That distinction is why pre-registration exists, and why a protocol paper written before the study (as Paper A is) has value: it fixes the guess in public before the data can move it.
