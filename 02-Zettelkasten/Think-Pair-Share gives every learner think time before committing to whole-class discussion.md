### What it is

Think-Pair-Share (Frank Lyman, 1981) is a structured 3-step routine that gives every student think time before discussion, reducing the filter between idea and expression.

<mark style="background: yellow">The core problem it solves:</mark> in unstructured discussion, fast responders dominate and slow processors never engage. TPS forces processing before exposure.

---

### The 3 steps

**Think** — Teacher poses a question; students think silently for 1–3 minutes.

Non-negotiable: without individual think time, only already-confident students engage. The Think step is where learning happens, not the Share step.

**Pair** — Students discuss with a partner.

The Pair step forces articulation — students must externalize and negotiate their thinking. This pushes beyond superficial recall and often surfaces misconceptions neither student noticed alone.

The teacher circulates during Pair to gauge understanding and identify which misconceptions to surface in Share.

**Share** — Selected pairs share with the whole class; teacher facilitates and connects responses.

---

### Why the Pair step is critical

Articulating an idea to one person organizes and deepens encoding.

The social pressure of being heard by one person is far lower than speaking to a full class — the [[High affective filter blocks language acquisition even when input is comprehensible|affective filter]] drops, increasing genuine participation from students who would otherwise stay silent.

---

### Key limitation

Students may reinforce each other's misconceptions during Pair.

Fix: combine with [[Peer Instruction uses ConcepTests and peer discussion to deepen conceptual understanding|Peer Instruction]]'s vote step — pair students who chose different answers. Disagreement drives deeper reasoning than agreement.

---

### Online vs offline

**Offline:** Assign designated seat partners; use "turn and talk" as a lightweight version; explicitly name the three steps so students know the routine.

**Online:**
- Think: 60-second silent timer before unmuting
- Pair: 2-minute breakout rooms
- Share: return to main room; use chat for simultaneous Share (all type, hold Enter, release on cue) to avoid the loudest-voice-first problem
