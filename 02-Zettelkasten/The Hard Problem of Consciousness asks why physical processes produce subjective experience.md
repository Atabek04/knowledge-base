Philosopher David Chalmers formulated the Hard Problem of Consciousness in 1995: why does any physical process produce subjective experience at all?

We can explain mechanistically how the brain processes visual information — light hits the retina, signals travel through the optic nerve, visual cortex activates. These are the "easy problems" (not easy to solve, but tractable in principle — they are questions about mechanism).

The Hard Problem is different: why does seeing red feel like something? Why isn't it just computation happening in the dark, producing behavior without any inner experience?

**Qualia** are the subjective, felt qualities of experience — the redness of red, the pain of pain, the taste of coffee. No physical or functional explanation has successfully accounted for why qualia exist rather than simply not existing.

This matters critically for AI: without a scientific theory of consciousness, we have no instrument to detect it. We cannot test whether a system is conscious — in other minds or in AI — except by assumption or analogy.

Any claim that AI is or is not conscious is currently unfalsifiable. That makes the debate simultaneously philosophically serious and scientifically unresolvable with present tools.
