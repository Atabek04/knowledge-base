### The counter-intuitive claim

Economist Pascual Restrepo argues most jobs *won't* be automated by AGI — not because AI can't do them, but because <mark style="background: yellow">they aren't economically important enough to spend scarce compute on.</mark>

---
### Bottleneck vs supplementary work

He splits work into two kinds:

- **Bottleneck work** — gates future growth: energy, infrastructure, science, defense, existential-risk reduction.
- **Supplementary work** — everything else: arts, hospitality, customer support, much research.

Scarce compute gets pointed at bottleneck tasks (fusion, asteroid defense) where the payoff is huge.

<mark style="background: green">Supplementary work survives by neglect — automating it would waste compute better spent elsewhere.</mark>

---
### The sting in the tail

Survival is not victory.
These jobs persist, but wages still decouple from GDP — humans keep working while labor's share of output shrinks.

<mark style="background: pink">"Your job is safe" here means "your job isn't worth taking," not "you're irreplaceable."</mark>
