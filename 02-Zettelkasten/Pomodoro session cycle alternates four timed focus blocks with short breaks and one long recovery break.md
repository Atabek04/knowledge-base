The Pomodoro Technique is not just "work 25 minutes, take a break."

The full cycle is a **four-round rhythm** with two tiers of recovery:

1. Work 25 min → short break 5 min
2. Work 25 min → short break 5 min
3. Work 25 min → short break 5 min
4. Work 25 min → **long break 15–30 min**

Four completed pomodoros = one full cycle. Then the long break resets fatigue before the next cycle begins.

---

### Why the Structure Works

**Short breaks (5 min)** prevent acute fatigue from compounding between sessions.
They exploit the [[Vigilance decrement causes sustained attention and accuracy to degrade within 20 minutes of monotonous focus|vigilance decrement]] — attention starts dropping around 20 min, so the break arrives before the decline becomes costly.

**Long breaks (15–30 min)** address deeper fatigue that accumulates across four sessions.
Ultradian rhythms (90–120 min cycles) mean that after ~4 pomodoros the body is in a natural trough — the long break aligns with this biological signal rather than fighting it.
See: [[Scheduling by Energy Peaks Makes Interruptions Less Costly]]

**Timer pressure** activates [[Parkinson's Law Means Your Time Estimate Is the Ceiling, Not the Floor|Parkinson's Law]] in reverse — the 25-minute ceiling compresses scope and kills open-ended drift.

---

### The Zeigarnik Effect and Session Continuity

Incomplete tasks stay mentally active (Zeigarnik effect).

A 25-minute pomodoro deliberately ends mid-flow. This keeps the task "open" in working memory across the break, making it easier to re-enter. Ending at a natural stopping point kills that continuity.

---

### What the Short Break Is Not

A 5-minute break is not enough for deep cognitive restoration.

Its function is **preventing accumulation**, not full recovery. Scrolling social media or reading news during a 5-min break re-engages directed attention and cancels the recovery benefit.
See: [[Restorative breaks recover directed attention by shifting the brain to involuntary attention through low-demand activity]]

---

### Adapting the Interval

25 min is Cirillo's default, not a neurological law.

A 2025 study (Behaviors, 15:7) comparing Pomodoro, Flowtime, and self-regulated breaks found no overall difference in fatigue or motivation across techniques — what mattered was **using any structured break system** rather than pushing through.

Some people work better at 50/10 or 90/20 (aligning with ultradian rhythms). The principle holds: fixed work block → mandatory break → long reset after N cycles.
