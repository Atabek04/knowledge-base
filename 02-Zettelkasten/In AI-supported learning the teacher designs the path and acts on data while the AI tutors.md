If an AI can tutor every student patiently and without limit, the teacher's old core job (delivering the same content to thirty people) is no longer scarce. The question is what the human does instead.

### The orchestrator role

An **orchestrator** *arranges* who plays what and when, without playing every instrument. Dillenbourg (2013) used the term for a teacher who manages many learning activities at once under real classroom limits: time, attention, and the curriculum.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The teacher as orchestrator designs the learning path and acts on learner data, while the AI does the one-to-one explaining.</mark>

---

### Three jobs the AI cannot do for itself

#### Designing the tutor

Kestin et al. (2025) found an AI tutor roughly doubled learning gains over an in-class active-learning session. The tutor was not plain ChatGPT: course instructors split each problem into steps, scripted hints that never revealed the answer, and checked its explanations in advance.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">A plain chatbot has no curriculum: it cannot know what to teach first or what to leave out, so the sequence must come from a human expert.</mark>

The same split shows in self-study: a book author supplies the roadmap, and the AI explains each topic with endless patience, simpler wording, and repetition on demand.

#### Watching the data and stepping in

The AI tutors; the teacher sees who is stuck and intervenes. Holstein et al. (2018) gave teachers real-time awareness of AI-tutor data, and learning improved most for students who were behind. Knoop-van Campen et al. (2023) found dashboards spread teacher feedback more evenly across ability levels.

#### Being coached by the AI

The roles can also flip: in Tutor CoPilot (Wang et al., 2024, working paper), an AI suggested next moves to human tutors, and students gained most where the tutors were weakest.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">The relationship itself (trust, belonging, someone who notices you) is not covered by any of these three jobs.</mark>
