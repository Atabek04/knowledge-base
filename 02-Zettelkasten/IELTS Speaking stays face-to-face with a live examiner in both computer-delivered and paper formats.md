Choosing the computer-delivered test changes Listening, Reading and Writing only. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Speaking is a live conversation with a certified examiner in both formats</mark>, and the questions do not differ by mode. It may fall on a different day from the other three papers.

Many centres also offer Video-Call Speaking: a live examiner based elsewhere, over video with a noise-cancelling headset, officially identical in content, timing, scoring and structure.

The research on whether the medium changes the score is reassuring but not empty. An IELTS-commissioned study found video-conferencing <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">slightly more difficult overall, with a negligibly small score difference</mark> — on 99 test takers and 10 examiners in a single city, and conditional on examiners having had mode-specific training.

The finding worth acting on is behavioural rather than statistical: <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">candidates asked for clarification 63% of the time on video against 27% face-to-face</mark>. Asking is permitted and not penalised, but needing to repeatedly is friction. Where both are offered, face-to-face removes the audio variable at no cost.

Source: [British Council — computer-delivered IELTS](https://www.britishcouncil.es/en/exam/ielts/which-test/computer) · [ielts.org — face-to-face vs video-conferencing delivery, phase 2](https://www.ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/exploring-performance-across-two-delivery-modes-for-the-ielts-speaking-test-face-to-face-and-video-conferencing-delivery-phase-2)
