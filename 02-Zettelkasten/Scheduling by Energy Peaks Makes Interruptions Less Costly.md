Most people schedule by clock: "I have 2 hours here, I'll do deep work."

Tony Schwartz and Jim Loehr's research in "The Power of Full Engagement" reframes the question: it's not about how many hours you have — it's about how much energy you bring to them.

### Ultradian Rhythms

The body operates in natural 90-120 minute cycles of high alertness followed by 20-minute recovery troughs.

During a peak: concentration, creativity, and emotional resilience are high.
During a trough: the body actively signals rest — yawning, hunger, difficulty focusing, restlessness.

Most people interpret the trough as laziness and push through. This burns the next peak too.

---

### Why This Matters for Interruptions

When you're interrupted during a trough, you lose almost nothing — you weren't in deep focus anyway.

When you're interrupted during a peak, the cost is enormous — you lose the rare state of high cognitive performance that doesn't come back just by waiting.

Scheduling defensively means: **protect the peak, let the trough absorb the urgents.**

Batch meetings, messages, and reactive work into your energy troughs.

Reserve peaks for work that requires the most cognitive load.

---

### The Practical Pattern

- Identify your personal energy peaks (most people: 90-120 min after waking)
- Block those hours for deep/focused work — no meetings, no Slack, no triage
- Place your triage block and communication windows inside natural troughs
- Take real breaks at the trough — not "check email" breaks, but actual rest

This is not time management. You are managing the *quality* of your hours, not the quantity.
