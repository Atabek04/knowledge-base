Most worries about AI and skill are about the long run: months of offloading slowly wearing ability down.

A 2026 set of experiments found an effect that shows up within a single sitting, and it hits persistence rather than knowledge.

---

### The experiments

Liu, Christian, Dumbalska, Bakker and Dubey (2026) ran randomized controlled trials with 1,222 participants on mathematical reasoning and reading comprehension.

Some participants had an AI assistant for a first block of problems. Then everyone worked without it.

With the AI, people scored higher. Once it was gone, they scored lower than people who never had it, and they abandoned problems sooner.

The effect appeared after roughly ten minutes of AI use.

---

### Why it appears so fast

Ten minutes is too short to erase anything a person knows. What changed is how they behaved when stuck.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Persistence is how long a person keeps working on a hard problem before giving up.</mark> It is a separate capacity from knowledge, and it is the one that moved.

#### Instant answers reset the expectation

The authors' explanation is conditioning. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">An assistant that answers immediately teaches people to expect an immediate answer, so being stuck starts to feel like a reason to quit instead of a normal stage of solving.</mark>

That is the opposite of what [[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency|desirable difficulties]] require. The struggle before the answer is where the learning happens, and the habit of struggling is what the AI wore down.

It also explains why [[AI-assisted learners score about two letter grades lower with debugging the most degraded skill|debugging degrades first]]: debugging is mostly the discipline of staying with a problem that does not yet make sense.

---

### Limits of the finding

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">The tasks were maths and reading, not programming, and persistence was measured right after the AI was removed, so the study shows a short-term shift, not a lasting trait.</mark> It is an arXiv preprint.

What it does establish is the direction and the speed: the cost of leaning on AI starts accruing in minutes, not months.
