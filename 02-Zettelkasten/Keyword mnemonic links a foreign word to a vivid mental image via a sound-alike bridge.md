### What it is

The Keyword Method (Atkinson & Raugh, 1975) is a two-stage encoding strategy for vocabulary retention.

**Stage 1 — acoustic link:** Find a native-language word or phrase that sounds like part of the target word. This is the *keyword*.

**Stage 2 — imagery link:** Form a vivid mental image connecting the keyword to the target word's meaning.

Example: Spanish *caballo* (horse). Keyword: "eye" (sounds like the *-ba* in ca-ba-llo). Image: a horse with a giant eye on its forehead.

When you later see *caballo*, the acoustic link triggers the keyword → the keyword triggers the image → the image retrieves the meaning.

---

### Why it works

It creates two retrieval routes — phonological and visual — rather than relying on rote repetition alone. This is a direct application of [[Dual coding theory shows that pairing images with verbal explanation creates stronger memory traces|dual coding]]: verbal and imagistic memory systems reinforce each other.

Meta-analyses (Pressley et al., 1982; Shapiro & Waters, 2005) consistently show 2–3x better recall on immediate tests compared to rote repetition.

Long-term retention advantage fades unless the keyword bridge is revisited periodically — it is a *temporary scaffold*, not a permanent memory structure.

---

### Limitations

- Creation effort is high — generating a good keyword takes time; impractical for bulk vocabulary
- Works better for concrete nouns than abstract vocabulary (harder to image *ephemeral* than *elephant*)
- Not all target words have obvious sound-alikes in the native language
- Does not help with pronunciation of the target word itself

---

### Application for teaching

- Teach the two-stage process explicitly; don't assume students will invent it
- Model 10–15 example keywords before asking students to create their own
- <mark style="background: green">Best for high-stakes recall sets</mark> (certification exams, standardized tests) where students need fast, accurate retrieval of a bounded word set
- Pair with [[Spaced repetition schedules vocabulary review at expanding intervals matched to the forgetting curve|spaced repetition]] to maintain the encoded words
