Once research is understood as an [[Research is a written argument that something not previously known is now known and can be checked|argument addressed to a reader]], the next question is who that reader is and what they are supposed to do with the argument. Booth, Colomb and Williams (*The Craft of Research*, 5th ed., ch 1, "Creating roles for writers and readers") answer it with three roles. The writer picks one, and the choice fixes the reader's role too.

---

### The three roles

| Writer says | Reader is cast as | Is it research? |
|---|---|---|
| "I found something new and interesting" | someone to be **entertained**: curiosity satisfied | no; a magazine piece |
| "I found a solution to a practical problem" | someone to be **helped**: does something differently | yes, applied |
| "I found an answer to a question worth asking" | someone to be **educated**: understands something they did not | yes, conceptual |

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">A research report casts its reader as either helped or educated; a reader who is only entertained has been given no reason to act or to change their understanding.</mark>

The two research roles map onto Booth's applied and pure problems: helping answers "what should we do", educating answers "what is true". A single paper can contain both, but one of them is the reason the paper exists and the other is a consequence.

---

### Role zero: the report that casts nobody

A fourth possibility is not in Booth's table because it is not a role at all: "here is what I did". A description of a built system, a feature list, an architecture chapter with no claim attached. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A description casts the reader in no role, because there is nothing for them to be entertained by, helped with, or educated about; it is the "we built a system" failure that examiners and reviewers reject.</mark>

Role zero is not a weak form of the helping role. Helping requires a claim that the solution works, and evidence for it; a description makes no claim, so it cannot help anyone.

---

### Choosing the role for a venue

The role is chosen by asking what the reader of that venue will do with the paper. An academic reviewer will not install the artifact, so the helping role has no audience there; the reader can only be educated. A practitioner conference or a product report is the reverse.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Pick one role per document and let the other appear only as a consequence; a paper that tries to help and educate equally reads as a product description with a hypothesis attached.</mark>

For a [[Design science builds an artifact to test an idea and the knowledge from evaluating it is the contribution|design-science thesis]], one that builds an artifact in order to test an idea, this settles what the artifact is for: it is the instrument that makes the educating claim testable, and it is described only as far as the reader needs to judge that test.
