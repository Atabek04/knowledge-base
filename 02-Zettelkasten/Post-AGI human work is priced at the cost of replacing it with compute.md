### The shift in what is scarce

Today the scarce thing is skilled human intelligence, so we pay for it.

In an AGI economy, intelligence is cheap and abundant — the scarce input becomes <mark style="background: yellow">compute (and the energy that feeds it)</mark>.

---
### How work gets repriced

Once a task can be done by compute, your work is no longer valued by what it contributes.

It is valued by <mark style="background: cyan">what it would cost to replace you with compute.</mark>

If replacing your task burns expensive compute, your labor keeps value. If it's trivial to automate, your wage tracks the falling cost of the chips that replace it.

---
### Why this is the key idea

It reframes the whole debate: the question stops being *"can AI do my job?"* and becomes *"is it worth the compute to do my job?"*

That single reframing leads straight to the bottleneck argument.
