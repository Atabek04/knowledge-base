### The rule

Pick a <mark style="background: yellow">small repertoire — roughly one White system plus one or two Black defenses — and keep it for a year.</mark>

Resist "repertoire bloat": collecting more openings does not make you stronger.

---
### Why depth beats breadth

You learn an opening's <mark style="background: cyan">plans and typical pawn structures by repeatedly reaching the same positions in real games</mark> — not by adding new openings to a list.

The hundredth time you reach your structure, you understand its middlegame.
The first time you reach a new one, you are guessing.

System openings like the London or Colle are fine <mark style="background: pink">*only if* you actually learn their plans, not just the move order.</mark>

### Fits the bigger order

This pairs with [[Deep opening memorization is wasted below 1800 ELO so learn principles not lines|principles over lines]]: keep the repertoire narrow and shallow until tactics are solid, then deepen the few openings you already play.
