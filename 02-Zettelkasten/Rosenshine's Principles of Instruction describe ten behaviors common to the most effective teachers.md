### What it is

Rosenshine's Principles (2012) are ten evidence-based instructional behaviors derived from three converging research traditions: cognitive science, classroom observations of master teachers, and studies on cognitive supports.

They describe what the most effective teachers consistently do — not a rigid script, but a planning and reflection checklist.

---

### The three research bases

- Cognitive science: working memory has strict limits; instruction must manage load carefully
- Master teacher observations: effective teachers question frequently, check understanding constantly, correct errors promptly
- Cognitive support studies: scaffolds, worked examples, and models help learners handle difficult content

---

### The 10 Principles

| # | Principle | Core application |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Daily review | 5–8 min retrieval at lesson start |
| 2 | Small steps | Present one chunk; practice it before advancing |
| 3 | Ask many questions | Cold call, TPS, hinge questions — all students respond |
| 4 | Models and worked examples | Think-aloud; compare strong/weak responses |
| 5 | Guided practice | Work through examples together; reduce prompts gradually |
| 6 | Check understanding frequently | All-learner responses, exit tickets |
| 7 | Aim for ~80% success rate | Adjust scaffolding so most succeed during guided practice |
| 8 | Scaffolds for difficult tasks | Writing frames, sentence stems — faded as competence grows |
| 9 | Independent practice | Only after accuracy is secured; builds fluency |
| 10 | Weekly and monthly review | Cumulative quizzes, spaced retrieval grids |

<mark style="background: cyan">Most critical cluster:</mark> Principles 2, 3, 5, 6 — small steps + constant questioning + guided practice + checking understanding form the core instructional loop.

---

### Online vs offline application

**Offline:** Start every lesson with a 5-question retrieval starter; use mini-whiteboards for whole-class response; exit ticket at the door.

**Online:** Poll tools (Mentimeter, Slido) for all-student questioning; screen-share worked examples with live think-aloud; low-stakes retrieval quiz via Google Forms.
