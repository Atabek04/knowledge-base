### The bottleneck is physical

Automating whole industries doesn't just need clever models — it needs **enormous compute**, and compute is bound by the physical world.

<mark style="background: yellow">GPU supply, data-center capacity, and electrical-grid power are hard ceilings on how fast AI can be deployed.</mark>

Deep learning itself was held back for years by inadequate hardware; inference-time scaling hits the same wall today.

---
### Why it caps the doom timeline

You cannot automate everything at once if you can't **build the chips and power the data centers** fast enough.

This is the same scarcity Restrepo names — and it cuts two ways:

- It **slows** mass automation (tempers the doom story).
- It **forces prioritization** — scarce compute goes to high-value bottleneck work, leaving much labor untouched.

<mark style="background: cyan">Compute scarcity is why "AI can do it" and "AI will do it everywhere, soon" are different claims.</mark>
