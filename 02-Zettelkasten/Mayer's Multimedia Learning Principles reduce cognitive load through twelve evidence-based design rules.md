### What it is

Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (CTML) is grounded in three assumptions:

- **Dual channel** — humans process visual and auditory information through two separate systems
- **Limited capacity** — each channel handles only a small amount at once
- **Active processing** — learners must select, organize, and integrate to actually learn

These three assumptions converge: dual channels explain *where* information goes; limited capacity explains *why overload fails*; active processing explains *what the learner must do*.

---

### The 12 principles (grouped by function)

**Reduce extraneous load** (highest priority for slide design)

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Coherence** | Remove irrelevant words, images, animations, music. Largest effect size in meta-analysis. |
| **Signaling** | Add arrows, highlights, bold to guide attention to what matters |
| **Redundancy** | Don't put the same words on screen that the narrator is speaking |
| **Spatial Contiguity** | Labels and captions must be physically close to the image they describe |
| **Temporal Contiguity** | Show narration simultaneously with the corresponding visual, not sequentially |

**Manage essential load**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Segmenting** | Break long content into learner-controlled chunks; cap segments at 10–12 min |
| **Pre-training** | Before teaching a complex process, teach the names and properties of key components |
| **Modality** | Pair visuals with *spoken* narration, not on-screen text — free the visual channel |

**Foster generative processing**

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Multimedia** | Always combine words and pictures rather than words alone |
| **Personalization** | Write narration in conversational, first-person style — not formal academic prose |
| **Voice** | Use a natural human voice, not TTS |
| **Image** | A talking-head instructor image does not improve learning and may increase load |

---

### Top 5 rules for teachers building slides

1. <mark style="background: yellow">Strip every decorative element</mark> — if it doesn't explain content, it costs capacity and teaches nothing
2. <mark style="background: pink">Never duplicate spoken words as on-screen text</mark> — redundancy principle; the most common slide mistake
3. Use spoken narration + visuals as default — modality principle
4. One visual focal point per slide; use color, arrows, or size contrast to guide the eye
5. Segment aggressively — never more than 6–10 minutes per online segment

---

### Evidence base

2025 Cromley & Chen meta-analysis (592 effects, 1990–2022): overall effect g = 0.37, consistent.

Largest effects: removing seductive detail, modality principle, personalization, multimedia principle.

Weakest: animations, games, VR showed inconsistent results.
