Most people use AI <mark style="background: pink">transactionally</mark> — a quick question in, a quick answer out, conversation closed.

Drew Bent, Anthropic's Education Lead, calls this "chatbot-only" usage. It treats the model as a vending machine.

The <mark style="background: yellow">top 1% of learners do the opposite: they treat AI as a collaborator</mark>, a thinking partner they argue with, hand hard problems to, and push back against.

---

### What changes in practice

An assistant gets closed questions: "What is X?"

A collaborator gets open, hard ones: "Here's my half-formed plan and three constraints — poke holes in it."

The shift is from <mark style="background: pink">extracting answers</mark> to <mark style="background: cyan">building understanding together</mark>.

This is why the differentiator is rarely the model — it's the user's stance toward it.

---

### Why the stance matters more over time

Anthropic's own usage data shows high-tenure users attempt higher-value tasks *and* succeed more often.

The habit compounds. Treating AI as a partner trains you to bring richer input, which produces richer output, which makes the partnership more valuable.

This depends entirely on what you feed it — see [[Rich personal context is the main differentiator between elite and average AI users]].
