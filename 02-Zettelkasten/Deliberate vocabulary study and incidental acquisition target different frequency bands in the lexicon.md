### Two pathways, different jobs

Nation (2001) distinguishes two primary vocabulary acquisition pathways — *deliberate* (intentional study) and *incidental* (pick-up during reading/listening) — and argues each is optimal at a different frequency band.

Neither pathway alone is sufficient. They are sequential and interdependent.

---

### When deliberate study wins: the core frequency bands

The first **2,000–3,000 most frequent word families** should be learned deliberately.

Waiting to acquire these incidentally is slow because:
- High-frequency words appear often but rarely stand out as "new" — attention skips them
- A learner cannot reach 98% text coverage through extensive reading until they already know these words
- The first 2,000 families cover ~80% of most written English — the leverage is enormous

<mark style="background: green">Tools: BNC/COCA frequency lists + Anki SRS. Study these words as a deliberate project before prioritizing extensive reading.</mark>

---

### When incidental acquisition wins: the long tail

Words beyond the **3,000–5,000 frequency band** number in the tens of thousands. Deliberate study of all of them is neither practical nor efficient.

Incidental acquisition through extensive reading and listening is the only scalable path for the long tail.

But incidental acquisition *requires* ~98% text coverage to work — the learner must already know enough words to read comfortably. Deliberate core-band study is the prerequisite.

---

### The dangerous traps

<mark style="background: pink">Learners who only study deliberately</mark> build academic vocabulary that never sounds natural — words are known in isolation, never embedded in contextual use.

<mark style="background: pink">Learners who only read without deliberate study</mark> have core-band gaps (high-frequency words they misuse or half-know) that persist for years because those words never stand out as "unknown."

---

### The productive cycle

1. Deliberate study of core frequency bands → raise coverage to ~98%
2. Extensive reading/listening at i+1 → incidental acquisition of mid-frequency vocabulary
3. Coverage threshold rises → access harder authentic materials
4. Deliberate study of Academic Word List (if needed) → academic/professional register
5. Repeat at higher difficulty level
