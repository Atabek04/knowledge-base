### What the research established

Paul Nation's corpus-based research (2001, 2006) quantified the vocabulary size needed to comprehend texts at different registers — providing one of the most actionable frameworks in applied linguistics.

**Core coverage findings (approximate):**

| Text type | Word families needed for 98% coverage |
|---|---|
| Conversational English | 3,000–4,000 |
| Fiction (novels) | 6,000–7,000 |
| Newspapers | 8,000–9,000 |
| Academic texts | 8,000–10,000 (+ Academic Word List) |

<mark style="background: yellow">98% coverage</mark> is the threshold at which comprehension is comfortable enough for acquisition from context. Below it, unknown words interrupt reading flow frequently enough to block acquisition.

---

### The Academic Word List (AWL)

Coxhead (2000) identified ~570 word families that appear frequently across academic disciplines but fall *outside* the most frequent 2,000 general-English word families.

The AWL covers approximately **10% of academic texts**. For learners aiming at academic or professional English, mastering the AWL is a high-ROI deliberate study target — it unlocks a substantial coverage increment across all academic domains simultaneously.

---

### Repetition thresholds — how many encounters to acquire a word

Nation's research on vocabulary acquisition suggests a word needs to be encountered in **meaningful context at least 10–20 times** across varied contexts before full acquisition.

Massed repetition (the same word 10 times on one page) is far less effective than distributed repetition. This is the empirical basis for [[Spaced repetition schedules vocabulary review at expanding intervals matched to the forgetting curve|spaced repetition]]: it simulates distributed encounters efficiently.

---

### Why vocabulary size is the binding constraint

<mark style="background: cyan">Vocabulary size is the binding constraint on comprehensibility.</mark>

No amount of grammatical instruction compensates for a vocabulary gap that drops a text below 98% coverage. For learners in acquisition-mode, **vocabulary breadth** (number of word families known) should be prioritized over vocabulary **depth** (nuanced knowledge of words already known), until the key coverage thresholds are reached.

---

### Implication for instruction order

1. First 2,000 word families → deliberate study (frequency lists, SRS)
2. Academic Word List → deliberate study (especially for academic/professional contexts)
3. Beyond 5,000 → incidental acquisition through extensive reading at i+1

See [[Deliberate vocabulary study and incidental acquisition target different frequency bands in the lexicon|deliberate vs. incidental acquisition]] for the full model.
