### What it is

Of all chess training activities, <mark style="background: yellow">reviewing the games you actually played — especially your losses — produces the most improvement per hour spent.</mark>

Playing trains your instincts; analysis trains your understanding.
A single deeply analyzed game teaches more than ten games played and forgotten.

---
### Why it works

Your own games contain the <mark style="background: cyan">recurring pattern of *your* mistakes</mark> — which is exactly the information that tells you what to train next.

A generic study plan guesses at your weakness.
Your game history measures it.

This is why it feeds [[Training your measured weakness beats following a generic chess study ratio|training your measured weakness]]: the analysis *is* the diagnosis.

---
### How to do it

Take a finished game — a loss is most instructive.
Replay it and <mark style="background: green">write your reasoning on each critical move in your own words first.</mark>
Only then check with the engine.

Deep analysis doesn't need to happen every game — once or twice a month on a representative loss is enough to surface the pattern.

<mark style="background: pink">Skipping straight to the engine eval hides *why* you erred — see [[Annotate a chess game yourself before turning on the engine]].</mark>
