AI tools made a finished-looking video cheap. A single prompt now returns music, motion and polish, which is why feeds fill up with videos that are technically fine and instantly forgettable.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">A one-shot prompt gets the model's most typical answer to the request, so the result carries no point of view that the prompt did not already contain.</mark>

---

### What the story supplies

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Story-first direction means deciding what the viewer should feel or understand by the end, and what specific experience it comes from, before any tool is opened.</mark>

The music video "LGTM" (2026) shows the difference. Its creator started from real frustration at work: deadlines cut because leadership expected AI to make everyone ten times faster, with code review and testing the first casualties. The animation and song were made with AI; the argument was not.

The creator put it plainly: it "wasn't a one-shot". The quality came from the story and from rounds of [[Frame-anchored review notes turn vague video feedback into precise fix requests for an AI agent|frame-anchored review]].

---

### The division of labour

| The human owns | The AI owns |
|---|---|
| What the video is about and for whom | Writing the code or generating the clips |
| What to cut | Producing variations quickly |
| Whether a render is good enough | Applying precise fix requests |

This is the same shift seen in software, where [[The software engineer's role is shifting from writing code to specifying reviewing and orchestrating it|the engineer's role moves toward specifying, reviewing and orchestrating]].

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Write the one sentence the video exists to say before writing any prompt, and cut every scene that does not serve it.</mark>

---

### Where the human contribution stops

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Directing an AI is real authorship of the idea and the edit, but it is not authorship of every frame, and crediting it as either more or less misleads the audience.</mark>

The LGTM creator drew the same line from both sides: they refused to undersell the story and the review rounds, and refused to oversell the result, saying they had approved it with an LGTM themselves.
