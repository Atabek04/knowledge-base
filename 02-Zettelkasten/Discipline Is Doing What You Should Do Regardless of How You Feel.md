Discipline is not a feeling. It is the execution of a prior decision in the face of present resistance.

The formula: you decided yesterday (when calm) what you should do. Today (when resistant), you execute that decision without renegotiating.

## Why Starting Is the Hardest Part

The moment of initiation is when the resistance peaks. The brain presents the full cost (effort, discomfort, uncertainty) while the reward is still abstract and distant. Once you start, the cost becomes sunk and the reward feels closer — resistance drops sharply within 2–3 minutes.

This is why "just start" is not empty advice. The neurological resistance is *front-loaded*. Push through the first 90 seconds and the task becomes manageable.

## Discipline vs Motivation vs Willpower

| Concept | Definition | Reliable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Motivation** | Feeling like doing the task | No — fluctuates with mood, sleep, context |
| **Willpower** | Forcing yourself through resistance | Partially — depletes over the day |
| **Discipline** | Pre-committed action regardless of feeling | Yes — does not depend on the present moment |

Motivation-dependent people work when they feel like it. Disciplined people work when it's time.

## The Pre-Commitment Trick

Because discipline is a *prior* decision, you strengthen it by:
1. Setting the rule when you're calm, not in the moment
2. Telling someone else (accountability)
3. Removing the alternative (environment design)

The goal is to make non-action harder than action when the moment arrives.
