The official assessment guide describes Academic Writing Task 1 as having a defined input and a largely predictable output: <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">an information-transfer task, relating narrowly to the factual content of the visual — not to speculative explanations that lie outside the given data</mark>.

So "this rise was probably caused by industrialisation" earns nothing. It is not a small stylistic flaw; the sentence is outside the criterion, consuming words and minutes that Task Achievement cannot reward.

The same guide names what Task Achievement *does* reward, and the fourth bullet is where the marks concentrate: comparing or contrasting the information by adequately highlighting the identifiable trends, principal changes or differences — explicitly <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">rather than mechanical description reporting detail</mark>.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Task 1 is a comparison task wearing a description task's clothing.</mark> The band-8 skill is choosing which two numbers to put next to each other, not describing all of them.

Source: [ielts.org — IELTS Writing Key Assessment Criteria (PDF), p.2](https://ielts.org/cdn/Guides/ielts-writing-key-assessment-criteria.pdf)
