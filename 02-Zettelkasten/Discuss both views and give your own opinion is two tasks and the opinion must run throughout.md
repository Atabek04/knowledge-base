The discussion prompt is the Task 2 type that most reliably produces a 6.5, and it fails in three distinct ways.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The opinion is missing.</mark> The prompt asks two things — discuss both views **and** give your own — so dropping either means the answer does not address all parts of the task, which is a band-7 requirement, not a band-8 refinement.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">The opinion appears only in the conclusion.</mark> Band 7 requires a clear position *throughout*. An essay that reports view A neutrally, then view B neutrally, then picks one in the final line has no position for three quarters of its length. The fix is mechanical: state it in the introduction, echo it in an evaluative clause inside each topic sentence, restate it at the end.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The views are reported rather than discussed.</mark> Restating what "some people argue" without extending or supporting it is precisely the band-7 ceiling — supporting ideas that lack focus.

Two related type-level traps. "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages" demands a verdict; the plain advantages-and-disadvantages prompt does not, and treating the first as the second is a Task Response cap. A two-part question needs both parts answered directly, one per body paragraph.
