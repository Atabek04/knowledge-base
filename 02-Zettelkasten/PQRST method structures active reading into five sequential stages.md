PQRST is a study method by Francis P. Robinson — an educational psychologist who studied how college students learn from textbooks.
It prevents passive reading by forcing active engagement at every stage.

---

### Preview

Skim the entire material before reading a single word.
Look at headings, subheadings, bullet points, diagrams, and summaries.

The goal is a mental map — not comprehension yet.

---

### Question

Convert each heading into a question before you read that section.

> "The Human Respiratory System" → "What is the human respiratory system and how does it function?"

This sets up **active learning**: you're now reading to find answers, not just absorbing words.
Questions stimulate curiosity and focus attention on key details.

---

### Read

Read the section thoroughly to answer the questions you created.
Don't rush — make sure you understand before moving on.

Your brain is in search mode: looking for specific answers, not scanning.

---

### State

After finishing a section, explain what you just learned — in writing or out loud.

Restating in your own words reveals gaps and reinforces understanding.
If you can't explain it, you haven't learned it yet.

---

### Test

At the end of the session, answer all the questions you created in the Question stage without looking at the material.

Struggle signals which areas need extra review.
