### What the 2026 data actually shows

The post-AGI wage-collapse story is a model. The *measured* effect so far is narrower and runs through a different channel.

<mark style="background: yellow">Adjustment shows up in employment — fewer people hired — not in pay cuts for those still working.</mark>

---
### The Stanford finding

Stanford's Digital Economy Lab ("Canaries in the Coal Mine") found that since generative AI took off:

- Workers **aged 22–25** in the most AI-exposed jobs saw a **~16% relative decline in employment**.
- Older and less-exposed workers stayed flat or kept growing.

<mark style="background: green">Entry-level workers are the canaries — the first to feel it, because their tasks are the easiest to automate.</mark>

---
### The caveat

Anthropic's own analysis found *no* systematic rise in unemployment for highly exposed workers through late 2022 onward — only tentative signs of a youth hiring slowdown.

<mark style="background: pink">The signal is real but early and contested — "first inning," not settled science.</mark>
