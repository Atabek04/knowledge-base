### What it is

Online delivery increases *extraneous* cognitive load — mental effort that serves no learning purpose — through structural features of the medium itself.

In-person, these extraneous demands are absent. A teacher switching modes must actively compensate by redesigning delivery, not just translating in-person methods to a screen.

---

### The four primary extraneous load sources (online)

| Source | Mechanism | Effect size |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-view** | Constant self-monitoring while processing content | d = 0.71 — disabling self-view is the highest-impact single intervention |
| **Grid view** | Sustained gaze from multiple faces triggers social threat processing | Biologically costly; no learning benefit |
| **Virtual backgrounds** | Distract peripheral attention | Natural backgrounds produced 0.68 higher germane load than virtual |
| **Multi-app environment** | Notifications, other tabs, messaging apps open simultaneously | No social accountability equivalent to in-person to counter this |

---

### Self-view is the highest leverage intervention

Zoom fatigue study (Scientific Reports, 2025, N=39, within-subjects): disabling self-view produced the largest significant effect across all interventions tested — d = 0.71 for both subjective fatigue and extraneous cognitive load.

The mechanism: continuous self-monitoring recruits working memory resources that should be assigned to content processing.

<mark style="background: yellow">Teach students to disable self-view in session 1. Or build it into the class setup checklist.</mark>

---

### Video lecture attention cliff

Multiple studies confirm completion drops sharply after ~15 minutes of continuous video, even with interactivity.

In-person, social accountability (visible instructor, visible peers) maintains attention for longer sessions. Online, that social monitoring is absent or degraded.

<mark style="background: pink">The 6-minute attention cap is a myth</mark> — interactivity extends engagement. But 15 minutes without an interaction point is the reliable ceiling.

---

### Split-attention online

Students attending online often have browser tabs, notifications, and messaging apps open simultaneously.

Second-screen viewing produces higher cognitive load than single-screen and leads directly to lower factual recall and comprehension (ScienceDirect).

This is the [[Cognitive load theory explains why working memory bottlenecks determine learning speed|split-attention effect]] (Sweller) at the environmental level — spatially separated, mutually referencing sources force integration work that consumes working memory capacity.

---

### Practical compensations for online delivery

| Rule | Basis |
|---|---|
| Teach students to disable self-view — build into session setup | d = 0.71 effect on extraneous load |
| Use plain, natural, low-contrast backgrounds — no virtual | 0.68 germane load advantage for natural backgrounds |
| Cap continuous lecture at 10–12 minutes; insert active tasks | 15-minute attention cliff |
| Make slides self-contained — no need to toggle to external documents | Split-attention; spatial contiguity principle |
| Active response every segment: polls, typed answers, verbal questions | Active participation crowds out extraneous processing |
| "Close other tabs, phone away" at session start | No social accountability equivalent online |
| 5-minute break per 45 minutes of session | Cumulative screen fatigue; attention recovery |
| Camera-on optional — don't mandate; address via norm-setting instead | Camera ≠ cognitive engagement (Byers 2024); mandatory camera adds self-view burden |

---

### The switching heuristic

**Offline:** presence compensates — movement, eye contact, and voice do the cognitive management work.

**Online:** structure compensates — without physical presence, build accountability and attention management into the session architecture via timed interactivity, load reduction by design.

The same topic, same teacher, same content — but offline you *feel* attention loss and respond. Online you must *predict* it in advance and pre-schedule the interventions.
