The widely quoted figure — 200 hours of study per band — originated in an IELTS partner statement, was later withdrawn, and is <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">no longer published by IELTS in any form</mark>. Prep vendors still market it as current research.

The refutation is specific to Writing. Green (2005) examined 15,380 repeat candidates plus 476 EAP learners tested at course entry and exit, and found that <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the initial score predicted the gain better than the length of the course did</mark> — gains shrink sharply as the starting level rises, and the 200-hour guideline was unrealistic.

Elder and O'Loughlin found about half a band after 200-240 hours of intensive instruction, and that gain was concentrated in Listening with no significant movement in Reading.

IELTS's own current wording only covers low-level learners: about three months for up to half a band, with the largest gains going to candidates starting below 5.5.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">There is no evidenced hour figure for 7.0 → 7.5 or 7.5 → 8.0 in any section.</mark> Any specific number for those transitions is folklore. What the evidence does support is the shape: the rate is not constant, and Writing is where hours convert into band movement worst.

Source: [Green (2005), *Assessing Writing* 10(1)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1075293505000152) · [ielts.org — how long will it take](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/how-long-will-it-take-to-get-the-ielts-band-score-i-need)
