Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky, former Google and Google Ventures designers, built their productivity system around one insight: most days end without a single meaningful task completed, even though the day felt busy.

Their solution is the Daily Highlight — choosing **one task** at the start of each day that you commit to completing. Not the most urgent, and not the longest. The one that matters most.

### How to Choose Your Highlight

The authors offer three selection criteria:

- **Urgency** — what genuinely has a deadline today?
- **Satisfaction** — what would make you feel the day was worthwhile when you look back tonight?
- **Joy** — what do you actually want to spend your energy on?

The highlight should take 60–90 minutes. Long enough to do real work. Short enough that it fits in any schedule.

### Why One Task Works

The brain cannot prioritize under ambiguity. When every task is "important," none of them is.

Naming one highlight collapses the decision problem. It creates a concrete answer to the question "what should I work on right now?" This removes the friction of re-deciding dozens of times throughout the day — and friction is what keeps important work perpetually deferred.

This is related to [[Killing the Decision Removes Willpower from the Execution Equation]]: the highlight kills the decision in advance.

### The Goal Connection

The highlight is not a random task. It is chosen by asking: what would move me closer to something I actually care about?

This makes it the bridge between long-term goals (which live in reviews and plans) and the specific hour in front of you. Without this bridge, goals remain abstract intentions that never touch today's work.

### Reflect at the End

The fourth step in the Make Time framework is reflection — a short nightly check on whether the highlight was completed and what could improve tomorrow. This closes the feedback loop and prevents drift.
