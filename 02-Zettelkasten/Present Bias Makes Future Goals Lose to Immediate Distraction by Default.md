Present bias is the tendency to overweight immediate rewards relative to future ones — even when you explicitly prefer the future outcome.

It's not irrationality in the everyday sense. It's a systematic, predictable asymmetry in how the brain values time.

---

### The Research Foundation

David Laibson formalized this in his 1997 paper "Golden Eggs and Hyperbolic Discounting" in the Quarterly Journal of Economics. Richard Thaler's work on intertemporal choice converged on the same finding from a different angle.

The key insight: human discounting is not exponential (steady, consistent across all time gaps) but **hyperbolic** (steep drop between now and soon, then flat after that).

This produces time-inconsistent preferences. Asked on Monday whether you'd prefer $100 today or $110 in a week, you take the $110. Asked on Friday whether you want $100 right now or $110 next Friday, you take the $100. Same trade-off. Different answers. You changed your mind without updating your values.

---

### Why "Hyperbolic" Matters

Exponential discounting would make you consistent. You'd stick with your long-run preference as it approaches.

Hyperbolic discounting creates a cliff between now and any future point, no matter how near. This means:

- The future-you who planned to work out tonight was making a rational plan
- The present-you who cancels at 6pm is also making a locally rational decision
- Neither "you" is lying. The preferences are genuinely different depending on the time of evaluation.

This is sometimes called the **present self / future self problem** — they are, in a functional sense, different decision-makers.

---

### Real-World Patterns

Laibson's model explains why:
- Savings rates decline when liquid accounts (credit cards) replace illiquid assets
- People miss deadlines they sincerely intended to meet
- Gym memberships get purchased in January and abandoned in February

The problem isn't commitment at the planning stage. The problem is that the plan was made by a different temporal version of the self.

---

### The Design Fix

Because present bias is structural, not motivational, fighting it with motivation doesn't work reliably.

Commitment devices exploit the gap. You make yourself bind future-you to the decision made by present-you-in-planning-mode — before present-bias can flip the preference.

Pre-commitment (locking in choices in advance), defaults (making the desired behavior the path of least resistance), and removing optionality all reduce the power of present bias by eliminating the moment where it strikes.
