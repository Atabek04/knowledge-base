Derwing and Munro compared two pronunciation-instruction regimes. Learners taught <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">global features — stress, rhythm, intonation — improved comprehensibility and fluency in spontaneous narrative</mark>. Learners taught individual segments improved on read-aloud sentences and <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the gains did not transfer to spontaneous speech</mark>.

IELTS Speaking is entirely spontaneous speech. The rubric agrees with the research: every band 8 Pronunciation bullet is prosodic — sustained rhythm, flexible stress and intonation across long utterances — while mispronounced individual phonemes only appear as a complaint at band 6 and below.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">So the priority order is chunking, sentence stress and weak forms first; word stress on your own topic vocabulary second; individual consonants last.</mark>

Shadowing is the practice method with the matching evidence base: a systematic review finds it improves prosody, fluency, comprehensibility and accentedness, while its effect on segmental control is inconclusive — the same asymmetry, from the training side.

Source: [Derwing & Munro, *Language Learning* (1995)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-1770.1995.tb00963.x) · [Systematic review of shadowing (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827)
