Both test owners publish a position on this, and IDP names a mechanism rather than just discouraging it: a memorised answer is delivered without natural emphasis or emotion, so <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">it damages Pronunciation — a quarter of the Speaking band</mark>. IELTS's own guidance puts it as sounding like a monotonous robot reading a script.

The second cost is that examiners are trained to notice it, and the reported response is to raise question difficulty until they see your real level. A script therefore buys you a harder test.

The detection cue repeatedly named is a profile, not a phrase: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Part 2 markedly stronger than Parts 1 and 3</mark>. That is exactly the shape a bank of pre-written cue-card answers produces.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The line is between preparing ideas and preparing sentences.</mark> Rehearsing topic collocations and positions is invisible; rehearsing wording is the thing being detected.
