### The mechanism

A wage is set by the marginal productivity of labor — what one more hour of your work adds.

If an AGI or robot can do that same hour at near-zero marginal cost, then the value the market puts on *your* hour falls toward that floor.

<mark style="background: yellow">As AGI labor approaches zero cost, the marginal productivity of human labor — and therefore wages — declines toward zero.</mark>

---
### The second consequence

Once wages fall, **labor's share of GDP converges toward zero** too.

Economic output keeps growing, but a shrinking slice flows to people who work for a living. The surplus accrues to whoever owns the machines.

---
### The disputed part

This is a *modeling* result, not a prophecy.
It assumes AGI is a near-perfect substitute for human labor across most tasks — an assumption others reject (see the compute-cost argument).

<mark style="background: pink">"Wages → zero" is the conclusion of a model, not an observed fact in 2026.</mark>
