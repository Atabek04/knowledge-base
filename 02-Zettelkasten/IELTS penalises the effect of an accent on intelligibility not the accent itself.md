The Pronunciation column of the Speaking descriptors closes each of the top bands with a statement about accent, and the wording is about consequences, not about sounding native.

Band 9: <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">accent has no effect on intelligibility</mark>. Band 8: accent has minimal effect on intelligibility, and the candidate can be easily understood throughout. Band 6: can generally be understood throughout without much effort.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">An L1 accent is compatible with band 8.</mark> What is not compatible is the listener having to work — and the band 6 and band 4 wording shows where that work comes from: mispronounced individual phonemes causing lapses in clarity, and rhythm disrupted by absent stress-timing.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This makes general accent-reduction work a poor use of study time and targeted work on the specific phonemes that break words, plus word and sentence stress, a good one.</mark> The band 8 bullets are all prosodic — rhythm sustained, stress and intonation flexible over long utterances — while the segmental complaints only appear at band 6 and below.

Source: [ielts.org — Speaking band descriptors (public version), Pronunciation column](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)
