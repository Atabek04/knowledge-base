Drew Bent frames AI agent building as the <mark style="background: yellow">foundational career skill for the next 30 years</mark> — the way spreadsheet proficiency was foundational for the previous 40.

The analogy is precise. Spreadsheets did not require you to be an accountant; they became a baseline literacy across nearly every white-collar job.

Agent building is positioned to play that role next.

---

### Why the spreadsheet comparison holds

A spreadsheet turned anyone into someone who could model, calculate, and decide without specialized software.

An AI agent turns anyone into someone who can <mark style="background: cyan">delegate multi-step work to software that reads, decides, and acts</mark> — without being a programmer.

You don't build the model. You orchestrate it. That is the new baseline literacy.

---

### How you actually build the skill: reps

You don't learn this from theory. You learn it by <mark style="background: green">doing reps</mark> — repeated hands-on experiments that reveal where AI works and where it breaks.

This is the practical entry point: [[Doing reps with AI builds intuition for its real limits and capabilities]].

---

### A sharper bar, not a lower one

A recurring counter-view: as AI makes basic output cheap, the human bar *rises*. When everyone can produce mediocre work instantly, the remaining edge is <mark style="background: pink">quality and judgment</mark>.

So "learn to build with AI" and "get genuinely skilled" are the same instruction, not opposites.
