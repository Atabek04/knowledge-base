### The problem it answers

If wages collapse but goods still need buyers, *someone* has to put income in people's hands — or the economy stalls on the demand side.

Universal Basic Income (UBI) is the most-cited fix: <mark style="background: yellow">redistribute the productivity gains of AGI as a regular, unconditional payment to everyone.</mark>

---
### The variants on the table

- **UBI** — cash to all, no strings.
- **Universal Basic Services** — guarantee housing, healthcare, food directly instead of cash.
- **Negative income tax** — top up incomes below a floor.
- **Wage-free / cooperative models** — if production costs hit near-zero, distribute goods at little or no charge.

---
### Why it's framed as economics, not charity

In the post-labor story, redistribution isn't only fairness — it's <mark style="background: cyan">how you keep aggregate demand alive so the productive machine has a reason to run.</mark>

<mark style="background: pink">Open question: UBI sustains *consumption* but does nothing about the *power* concentrated in whoever owns the AGI.</mark>
