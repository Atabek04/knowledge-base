Almost every price in a market economy, from bread to a programmer's salary, comes out of one tug of war between people selling and people buying. The model of **supply and demand** describes that tug of war.

### Glossary

- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"><b>Supply</b>: how much of a good sellers are willing and able to offer at each price.</mark>
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"><b>Demand</b>: how much of a good buyers are willing and able to buy at each price.</mark>
- **Equilibrium**: the balance point, where the amount offered equals the amount wanted. From Latin *aequi-librium*, "equal balance".
- **Equilibrium price**: the price at that balance point.
- **Surplus**: more offered than wanted, which happens when the price is above equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: more wanted than offered, which happens when the price is below equilibrium.
- **Shift**: a change in supply or demand caused by something other than the price itself (new technology, more sellers, changed tastes).

---

### How the price finds the balance

Sellers offer more when the price is high, because selling pays better. Buyers want less when the price is high, because it costs them more.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">A price above equilibrium leaves unsold goods, so sellers cut it; a price below equilibrium leaves buyers unserved, so sellers raise it.</mark> Both pressures stop only at the **equilibrium price** ([OpenStax, Principles of Economics 3e, 3.1](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/3-1-demand-supply-and-equilibrium-in-markets-for-goods-and-services)).

---

### What a shift does to the price

#### More supply lowers the price

Around 2000, few people could build a simple website. Today millions can, and website builders do much of the work. Supply grew enormously while demand grew far less, so the price of a simple website fell.

This is the water-diamond logic at market scale: once a thing is abundant, the next unit sells for little.

#### More demand raises the price

When many buyers suddenly want the same thing (masks in 2020, GPUs for AI training), sellers can charge more until supply catches up.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A price change alone does not tell you which side moved: a falling price can mean supply rose or demand fell.</mark> To judge a claim like "this work is losing value", ask which curve shifted and why.
