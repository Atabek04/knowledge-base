The dirham (silver coin) comes from the Greek drachma, transmitted through Persian empires into Islamic use.

**Etymology**: Greek drachma (δραχμή, "to grasp") → Persian drahm → Arabic dirham (درهم)

Originally a drachma meant a handful of metal rods—literally a fist's worth of value.

## Path Through History

When Sassanid Persia (224-651 CE) adopted Greek monetary systems, they called their silver coin the drahm. The Islamic empires adopted this name and weight standard, making dirham the official silver currency across the Islamic world.

**Weight**: 50-60 grains of barley (~2.9-3 grams)
**Purity**: High-grade silver

The name persists today in **Morocco** and **UAE** currencies.
