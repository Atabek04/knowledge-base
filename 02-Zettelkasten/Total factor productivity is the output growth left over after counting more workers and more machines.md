An economy can produce more in two ways: use more inputs, or get more out of the same inputs. Only the second makes people richer per hour worked, and economists measure it as **total factor productivity**.

### Glossary

- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"><b>Productivity</b>: how much output comes from each unit of input.</mark>
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;"><b>TFP (total factor productivity)</b>: the part of output growth not explained by more labor or more capital.</mark> *Total factor*: it is measured against all inputs (factors) together, not just workers.
- **Residual**: what is left over after everything measurable has been subtracted. TFP is computed as a residual, so it is also called the **Solow residual**, after the economist Robert Solow.
- **Input**: anything used up or put to work in production: hours of labor, machines, materials.
- **Output**: what production yields, such as loaves of bread, or GDP for a whole country.

---

### Where extra output comes from

A bakery has 2 bakers and 1 oven and makes 1,000 loaves a day. A year later it has the same 2 bakers and the same oven but makes 1,300 loaves, because the bakers reorganized the order of their work.

Neither labor nor capital grew, so the extra 300 loaves came from better methods. That 30% is TFP growth.

#### Subtracting what the inputs explain

Suppose instead the bakery hires a third baker, so labor grows 50%, and output rises from 1,000 to 1,600 loaves (60%). Some of that growth is explained by the extra baker. Say the extra baker accounts for 400 loaves. The remaining 200 loaves are not explained by any added input, so they are TFP growth.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">TFP cannot be observed directly; it is calculated as whatever output growth remains after the growth of measured inputs is subtracted.</mark> It captures better methods, ideas, organization and technology ([Wikipedia: Total factor productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_factor_productivity)).

#### Why the leftover matters for AI

Adding workers or machines raises GDP but not output per worker. A rise in TFP does: the same people and machines make more.

Acemoglu estimates that AI will raise US TFP by no more than about 0.66% over 10 years, and by less than 0.53% once hard-to-learn tasks are accounted for. He calls the effect "nontrivial but modest" ([NBER w32487](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487)).

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A forecast of AI's GDP gain and a forecast of AI's TFP gain are different numbers; compare like with like before calling one estimate optimistic and another pessimistic.</mark>
