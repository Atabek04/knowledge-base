### The theory (brief)

Krashen's Input Hypothesis (1982) states that acquisition happens when learners receive input slightly above their current level (i+1) — not too easy (no growth), not too hard (incomprehensible). The *theoretical* claim is covered in [[High affective filter blocks language acquisition even when input is comprehensible|the affective filter note]].

This note focuses on **practical implementation**: how to operationalize i+1.

---

### Nation's 98% threshold — operationalizing i+1

Paul Nation's vocabulary research provides a workable definition: a learner needs to understand approximately <mark style="background: yellow">**98% of the words in a text**</mark> for reading to be comfortable enough for acquisition from context to occur.

At 95% coverage (roughly 1 unknown word per 20), comprehension is possible but acquisition breaks down — too much effort goes to decoding unknowns, leaving none for acquisition.

This is the basis for **graded readers**: texts engineered to stay within a specific vocabulary band so that unknown words stay below 2%.

---

### Extensive vs. intensive reading

| Mode | Purpose | Material | Unknown word rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extensive** | Fluency, consolidation, pleasure | Slightly below i+1 | <1% |
| **Intensive** | Vocabulary expansion | At i+1, with dictionary | ~2–3% |

Nation recommends approximately a **4:1 extensive-to-intensive ratio** for balanced progress. Learners who read only intensively burn out; those who read only extensively plateau.

---

### What i+1 is NOT

<mark style="background: pink">i+1 does not mean "one new word per sentence."</mark>

It means the *overall challenge level* keeps the learner in an acquisition-productive zone. A text can have 5–10 unknown words per page and still be i+1 if context allows inference. The 98% threshold is a coverage metric, not a per-sentence word-count rule.

---

### Monitoring i+1 in practice

A workable heuristic: if you encounter more than 2–3 unknown words per page during reading, the material is above i+1 — step back one level.

For listening: if you cannot follow the main idea of a segment without replaying it, the audio is above i+1.

See [[Input laddering sequences listening and reading materials by difficulty to maintain comprehensible i+1 throughout acquisition|input laddering]] for how to sequence materials as vocabulary grows.
