Gary Marcus (cognitive scientist, NYU) argues in "Rebooting AI" (2019) and ongoing writing that deep learning has a structural limitation: it cannot compose familiar concepts reliably to handle novel combinations.

Systematic generalization means: if you understand "blue ball" and "red cube," you can immediately understand "red ball" — you combine the concepts rather than learn each combination separately. Children do this naturally.

Deep learning models, Marcus argues, learn correlations in training data. They fail on combinations they haven't seen in sufficient frequency, even when the components are individually familiar.

He calls this the compositionality problem. It is distinct from data size — more training data doesn't fix a structural incapacity.

Marcus is not anti-AI; he advocates for hybrid systems that combine neural networks (good at pattern recognition) with symbolic reasoning (good at rule-following and composition). He views current LLM capabilities as impressive but brittle.
