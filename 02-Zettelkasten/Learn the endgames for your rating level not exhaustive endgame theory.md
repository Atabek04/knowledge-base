### The rule

Study <mark style="background: yellow">only the endgames you actually need at your current rating — plus one level ahead.</mark>

Mastering theory beyond your level is wasted effort that crowds out the positions you'll really face. (Jeremy Silman, *Silman's Complete Endgame Course*.)

---
### Why level-gating works

Silman organizes endgames into chapters by rating class (beginner → master 2200+).
<mark style="background: cyan">The structure self-selects:</mark> a 1200 player learns basic mates, opposition, and the square rule — not the Vancura defense they won't see for years.

This is the *what to study* companion to [[Studying endgames early gives a foundation for judging earlier moves|why to study endgames early]].

---
### The practical-positions alternative

A complementary rule (de la Villa, *100 Endgames You Must Know*): <mark style="background: green">master the ~100 endgames that recur in real games, then steer toward those known positions</mark> rather than memorizing exhaustive theory.

<mark style="background: pink">Practical endgame strength is *reaching known positions*, not knowing every theoretical ending.</mark>
