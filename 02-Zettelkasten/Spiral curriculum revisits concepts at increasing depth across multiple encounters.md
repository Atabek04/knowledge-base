### What it is

Spiral curriculum (Bruner, 1960): any concept, taught honestly at the right level of representation, can be introduced to any learner.

The curriculum should return to the same concepts repeatedly — each time at greater depth, complexity, and formalism.

<mark style="background: yellow">Deep understanding is not built in one pass. It grows through successive encounters.</mark>

---

### The three principles

1. **Cyclical revisiting** — topics return at planned intervals, not randomly. The return must be designed into the curriculum, not left to chance.

2. **Increasing depth per iteration** — each revisit adds formal rigor, more complex examples, or new connections. It is *not* re-teaching. It is deepening.

3. **Explicit connection to prior encounters** — each revisit activates what was learned before and extends it. Learners are told: "You saw X before; now we're going to formalize it."

---

### Contrast with Gagné's linear model

| | Gagné (cumulative hierarchy) | Bruner (spiral) |
|---|---|---|
| Prerequisite handling | Full mastery required before advancing | Partial understanding on first pass is acceptable |
| Gap tolerance | None — a missed step compounds | Built in — gaps are filled on later spirals |
| Best for | Procedural skills with clear dependencies | Conceptual understanding that grows over time |

<mark style="background: cyan">These are complementary, not competing.</mark> Foundational procedural skills use Gagné-style linear mastery. Conceptual domains layer a spiral on top.

---

### Where it fits in the system

[[Concept prerequisite mapping sequences instruction along the dependency graph|Prerequisite mapping]] defines the minimum sequencing constraints (what must come before what).

Within those constraints, spiral design decides *how often to revisit* and at what depth.

A concept with many dependents and high conceptual richness benefits from spiral treatment. A pure prerequisite leaf (no further growth) is taught once and mastered linearly.

---

### Application to curriculum design

- Map all target concepts with their prerequisite dependencies first
- Identify which concepts benefit from spiral treatment vs. one-pass linear mastery
- For each spiral concept: design 3+ encounters at increasing abstraction (intuitive → operational → formal)
- Build retrieval practice into each revisit — re-exposure alone is insufficient
- Flag first encounters as intentionally incomplete: "we'll return to this with more tools"
