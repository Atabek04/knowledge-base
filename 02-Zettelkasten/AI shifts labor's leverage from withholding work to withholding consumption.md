### The old lever

For two centuries, workers' power came from one threat: <mark style="background: yellow">withhold labor</mark> (strike), and production stops.

That lever only works while production needs human work.

---
### The new lever

If robots do the producing, withholding labor threatens no one.

The only bargaining chip left is the other side of the loop: <mark style="background: cyan">withholding *consumption and demand*.</mark>

A population that can refuse to buy still holds something owners need — buyers.

---
### Why it's weaker

Strikes are coordinated and concentrated; consumption is diffuse and individual.
Organizing millions to *not buy* is far harder than organizing a workforce to *not work*.

<mark style="background: pink">The replacement lever exists, but it is structurally weaker — which is why policy, not bargaining, dominates post-labor proposals.</mark>
