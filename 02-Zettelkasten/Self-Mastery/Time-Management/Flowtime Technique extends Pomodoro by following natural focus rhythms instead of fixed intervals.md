The Pomodoro Technique (25 minutes on, 5 minutes off) works well for people who struggle to start. But for deep, complex work, an arbitrary timer breaking mid-flow destroys the very state you worked to build.

The **Flowtime Technique** keeps the core insight — structured blocks with tracked breaks — but removes the fixed interval. You start a block, work until focus genuinely fades, then record how long you worked and take a proportional break.

### Why it matters

Flow states take 10–20 minutes to enter. A 25-minute Pomodoro may interrupt you just as focus peaks. For a developer solving a complex problem, or a student mastering a difficult concept, this is net negative.

The question isn't "did you follow the timer?" — it's "did you protect your attention from distraction while working?"

The Prophet ﷺ said to Salman al-Farsi: *"Your body has a right over you."* Proportional breaks are not laziness — they are fulfilling the right of the body Allah created and entrusted to you.

---

### How it works

Flowtime replaces the fixed 25-minute window with a personal tracking loop:

| Work duration | Break duration |
|---------------|----------------|
| Under 25 min | 5 min |
| 25–50 min | 8 min |
| 50–90 min | 10–15 min |
| Over 90 min | 20+ min (NSDR recommended) |

The break durations are proportional, not arbitrary. Longer focus → longer recovery required.

Critically: breaks must be screen-free and cognitively inert (see [[Ultradian Rhythms cap sustainable deep focus at 90 minutes before cognitive performance drops|Ultradian Rhythms]]).

---

### Research backing

The Flowtime Technique was formalized by Dionatan Moura and aligns with research on Flow states (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990) — the condition of complete absorption in a challenging task that produces peak performance. Flow cannot be scheduled in 25-minute slots; it must be protected once entered.

---

### How to apply it

1. Write your single task for the block (not a list — one thing)
2. Start a stopwatch
3. Work without switching tabs, checking messages, or interrupting
4. When focus fades naturally — stop the stopwatch, note the duration
5. Take a proportional screen-free break
6. Write your [[Hemingway Bridge stops work mid-task to preserve active context and eliminate cold starts|Hemingway Bridge]] note before closing

Track your average focus duration over a week. Most people discover their natural window is 45–70 minutes — not 25, not 4 hours.

---

### When Pomodoro is still better

If you are avoiding starting altogether — Pomodoro wins. The fixed 25-minute commitment removes the activation cost. "Just 25 minutes" is easier to agree to than "work until you're done."

Use Pomodoro to start. Use Flowtime to sustain.
