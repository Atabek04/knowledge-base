Tea contains both caffeine and **L-theanine** — an amino acid that blunts caffeine's jitteriness and extends the focus window. The combination produces a calmer, more sustained alertness than caffeine alone. For long study sessions, tea is often superior to coffee precisely because of this pairing.

### Why it matters

Pure caffeine produces a sharper peak and a sharper crash. The L-theanine in tea modulates both — raising the floor of the curve and smoothing the descent. This matters for multi-hour study blocks where cognitive consistency matters more than peak intensity.

---

### How it works

L-theanine is an amino acid found almost exclusively in tea leaves. It crosses the blood-brain barrier and promotes alpha-wave activity — associated with relaxed alertness, the state often described as "calm focus."

Caffeine raises arousal. L-theanine prevents that arousal from tipping into anxiety or overstimulation. Together they produce a state that is alert without being wired.

Typical tea doses:
- Green tea: ~30–50mg caffeine + ~20–30mg L-theanine per cup
- Black tea: ~40–70mg caffeine + ~15–25mg L-theanine per cup

The ratio in natural tea is already close to the 2:1 caffeine-to-theanine ratio studied in research.

---

### Research backing

Owen et al. (*Biological Psychology*, 2008) found that caffeine + L-theanine improved accuracy on demanding cognitive tasks more than caffeine alone, while reducing self-reported anxiety. The combination is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive nutrition research.

---

### How to apply it

**For long study sessions (2–4 hours):** use tea as the primary driver — the L-theanine sustains focus and prevents the mid-session crash that coffee often produces.

**For high-demand mornings or short intense sessions:** coffee works well — the sharper peak is appropriate for 60–90 minute sprint blocks.

**Practical stack:**
- Morning sprint block → coffee (100mg caffeine)
- Long afternoon session → green/black tea (2 cups over 2 hours)
- Never mix both heavily — cumulative dose climbs quickly
