Sitting still for long periods accelerates adenosine accumulation without the neural reward that normally accompanies productive activity. The result is a foggy, low-energy feeling that has nothing to do with sleep debt — it is a direct consequence of physical stillness. Coffee partially compensates by blocking adenosine receptors, but it does not fix the root cause.

### Why it matters

If your fatigue during study comes from inactivity rather than true sleep deprivation, caffeine is masking a solvable problem. Movement is a more efficient solution — and it makes caffeine work better when you do use it.

---

### How it works

Even brief physical movement increases cerebral blood flow and accelerates adenosine clearance. The brain responds to movement with increased oxygenation and neurotransmitter activity, which reduces the subjective drag of sitting.

This is why a short walk before a study session often produces more clarity than a second cup of coffee — and without the tolerance cost.

---

### Research backing

Raichlen et al. (*PNAS*, 2020) found that even 10 minutes of brisk walking increases cerebral blood flow and produces measurable improvements in cognitive performance. Prolonged sitting degrades executive function even in people with adequate sleep.

---

### How to apply it

Before every deep work block, break the stillness first:

- 10-minute walk outside
- 20 squats or push-ups
- Any movement that raises heart rate briefly

You will find caffeine hits more cleanly after movement, and you may need less of it to reach the same clarity.

IF about to start a study block AND feeling foggy → THEN move first, then make coffee
