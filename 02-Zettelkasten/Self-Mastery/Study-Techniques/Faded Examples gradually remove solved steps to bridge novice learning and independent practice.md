Faded Examples is the technique of gradually reducing the number of steps shown in a worked example as learner competence grows. A fully worked example becomes a partial example, then a problem with only the first step, then a bare problem.

Each fade forces the learner to generate the missing steps — activating the schema being built rather than just reading it.

---

### The three-stage structure

| Stage | What is shown | Cognitive demand |
|---|---|---|
| Full example | All steps solved | Low — observe and understand |
| Completion problem | Some steps blank | Medium — generate missing pieces |
| Independent problem | No steps | High — full schema retrieval |

The transition between stages should happen when the learner can *predict the next step before reading it* — that is the expertise threshold for that level.

---

### Why fading works

Fading triggers **self-explanation** naturally: when a step is missing, the learner must reconstruct the reasoning chain. This is more cognitively demanding than reading a complete solution — and the difficulty is *productive*, unlike blind trial-and-error for novices which produces wasted search.

Renkl et al. (2002): faded worked examples outperform both full worked examples and unguided problem-solving for intermediate learners, precisely because the cognitive demand is calibrated to the learner's current schema.

---

### How to apply it

**New algorithm (e.g. BFS):**
1. Study 2–3 fully solved BFS problems with self-explanation
2. Solve 2–3 problems with only the initial setup given
3. Solve bare problems cold

**New math concept (e.g. gradient descent):**
1. Walk through a fully annotated derivation
2. Fill in missing derivation steps from a partial example
3. Derive from scratch

**LeetCode pattern study:** Full explanation → re-implement with only the function signature → solve new problems of the same pattern cold.
