The Worked Examples method presents a fully solved problem — every step shown, with reasoning — *before* the learner attempts to solve independently. The learner's job is not to solve but to understand: trace each step and ask "why does this step follow from the last?"

This is the default mode of expert teaching. The Prophet ﷺ demonstrated before delegating. The faqih explained the ruling with its 'illa before asking the student to extract rulings. Showing precedes doing — this is the natural order of *tarbiyah*.

---

### Why it works — Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, 1988)

Novice learners have limited working memory. When they attempt an unfamiliar problem independently, most of that capacity goes to *searching* for a valid next step — not to understanding the structure. The search is wasted effort.

Worked examples eliminate the search. Every cognitive resource goes to schema-building instead.

Sweller & Cooper (1985): students who studied worked examples learned **~6x faster** with fewer errors than those solving problems independently from the start. Effect nearly doubles when self-explanation prompts are embedded (Hattie, 2009).

---

### Self-explanation — what stops passive reading

A worked example studied passively produces little benefit. The active component is **self-explanation**: stopping at each step and asking:

- *Why does this step follow?*
- *What principle is being applied here?*
- *Could it have been done differently?*

This is not annotation — it is interrogation of the reasoning chain.

---

### Where it fits and where it doesn't

**High fit:** Math (calculus, linear algebra, probability), algorithms (new patterns), programming syntax, design patterns, ML mechanics (backprop, gradient descent), system design case studies, DevOps config (Terraform, K8s).

**Conditional fit:** LeetCode — use worked examples only for *new patterns*. Once the pattern is recognized, switch immediately to problem-first. A pattern you've seen before is not a novice domain.

**Low fit:** Creative architecture, open-ended design, any domain where you're already intermediate+. Worked examples here cost performance — see [[Expertise Reversal Effect means worked examples hurt performance once domain knowledge grows]].

---

### The fading transition

As competence builds, worked examples should shrink: full solution → partial solution → only the problem. This is **fading** — see [[Faded Examples gradually remove solved steps to bridge novice learning and independent practice]].

The signal to fade: you can *predict the next step before reading it*. That is the expertise threshold.
