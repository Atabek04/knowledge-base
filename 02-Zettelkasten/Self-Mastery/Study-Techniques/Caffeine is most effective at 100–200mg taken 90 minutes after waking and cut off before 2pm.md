Caffeine effectiveness is not just about dose — it is about *when* you take it relative to your cortisol curve and *how much* before tolerance and diminishing returns kick in. Most people mistime and overdose caffeine, which builds tolerance fast and degrades sleep quality invisibly.

### Why it matters

Heavy caffeine users don't drink coffee to feel good — they drink it to feel *baseline*. The brain upregulates adenosine receptors to compensate for chronic blockage, meaning more caffeine is needed just to reach normal. Getting the dose and timing right prevents this trap.

---

### How it works

**Timing:**

Cortisol peaks 20–30 minutes after waking and naturally clears adenosine during the first 90 minutes. Drinking caffeine during this cortisol window competes with a process already clearing the fog — it wastes the dose and accelerates tolerance.

Wait 90 minutes after waking → caffeine then hits receptors already partially cleared → stronger, cleaner effect.

**Dose:**

The dose-response curve for cognition is not linear past 200mg. Beyond that, cortisol spikes, anxiety increases, heart rate rises — cognitive benefits plateau while costs increase.

| Dose | Effect |
|---|---|
| 100–200mg | Adenosine blocked, alertness stable, mood even |
| 300–400mg | Jitteriness, elevated cortisol, diminishing cognitive return |
| 400mg+ consistently | Receptor upregulation → dependency |

**Cut-off:**

Half-life is 5–6 hours. A 3pm coffee still has half its caffeine active at 8–9pm — degrading sleep quality even when you fall asleep easily. Poor sleep increases next-day adenosine load, which increases caffeine need. A cycle that compounds against you.

---

### Research backing

Fredholm et al. (*Pharmacological Reviews*, 2005) established caffeine pharmacokinetics. Andrew Huberman (Stanford, 2021) popularized the 90-minute delay protocol based on cortisol-adenosine interaction research.

---

### How to apply it

**Daily protocol:**
1. Wake → no caffeine for 90 minutes (water, light movement, natural light)
2. First coffee/tea at 90-minute mark
3. Second dose (if needed) no later than 12–1pm
4. Caffeine-free days once or twice per week to prevent receptor upregulation

**One cup of standard coffee = ~80–100mg caffeine.**
**One cup of green tea = ~30–50mg caffeine + L-theanine.**
