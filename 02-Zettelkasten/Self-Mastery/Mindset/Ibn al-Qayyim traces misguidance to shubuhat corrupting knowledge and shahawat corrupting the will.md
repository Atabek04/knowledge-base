When a person goes wrong, the first question is where the failure sits: in what they know, or in what they want. Ibn al-Qayyim, following his teacher Ibn Taymiyyah, sorts every trial into one of two kinds.

---

### The two roots of misguidance

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Shubuhat are doubts that corrupt knowledge, so a person cannot see what is right; shahawat are desires that corrupt the will, so a person sees what is right and does not want it.</mark>

| | Shubuhat (شبهات) | Shahawat (شهوات) |
|---|---|---|
| What breaks | 'ilm, knowledge | irada, the will |
| Symptom | honest confusion about the truth | the truth is known, then bent or ignored |
| Cure | yaqin, certainty | sabr, patience |

Ibn al-Qayyim discusses the two trials in *Ighathat al-Lahfan*. The pairing of cures comes from as-Sajdah 32:24, which ties leadership in religion to patience and certainty; Ibn Taymiyyah's summary is that through sabr and yaqin leadership in the deen is reached.

---

### How desire bends judgment

Hawa, desire following the self, does not only push toward a wrong act. It changes how the act looks.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Hawa makes the self's own case look good to it, so a person judging their own matter sees through desire while judging someone else's matter with clear eyes.</mark>

The Qur'an describes it in Fatir 35:8: "Is one whose evil deed has been made attractive to him, so he sees it as good...?"

A narration in Abu Dawud says the same: hubbuka al-shay'a yu'mi wa yusimm, "your love of a thing makes you blind and deaf." Its authenticity is disputed among hadith scholars, so treat it as a known saying rather than a firm hadith.

---

### Knowledge is not what is missing

Al-Qiyamah 75:14-15: "Man is a clear witness against himself, even if he offers his excuses."

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">A shahwa problem is not solved by more information, because the person already knows; what covers the knowledge is excuse-making.</mark>

This is why [[Solomon's paradox shows people reason more wisely about others' problems than their own|Solomon's paradox]] belongs on the shahawat side: the wise judgment exists and works on other people, and fails only where desire has a stake. It is the same diagnosis as [[Voluntary action requires ilm and irada — information alone cannot produce movement|'ilm without irada]].

---

### Justice against yourself

The command that answers this is an-Nisa 4:135: "Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah, even against yourselves."

Ibn Taymiyyah's recurring point is that the measure must come from revelation, outside the self, because a person following hawa cannot also be a fair judge of their own case.
