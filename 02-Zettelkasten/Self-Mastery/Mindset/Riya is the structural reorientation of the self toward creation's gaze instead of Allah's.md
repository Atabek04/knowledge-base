*Riya* is not just showing off. It is the gradual reorientation of the self — actions, speech, appearance, even worship — around what others will think. Ibn al-Qayyim in *Madarij al-Salikin* calls it *shirk al-khafi* (hidden association): placing creation's opinion alongside Allah's in a position it was never meant to occupy.

The corruption isn't always visible. Riya often feels like motivation, like professionalism, like care for one's reputation. That's what makes it dangerous.

---

### Why it matters

Riya is not a habit problem — it's a heart problem. Treating it with better systems or willpower misses the root. The cure is a specific reorientation of intention, not a scheduling fix.

---

### How it works

Ibn al-Qayyim identifies a spectrum of three grades:

1. Doing good *for* people's praise
2. Doing good *better* when people are watching
3. Abandoning good when no one is watching

Most people live in grade 2 or 3 without recognizing it as riya. The test is internal: *would I do this exactly the same if no one ever knew?*

---

### The modern parallel

Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985) names the same pattern *introjected regulation* — doing things to avoid shame or gain approval rather than from genuine internal values. Research identifies it as the most psychologically costly form of motivation: high effort, high anxiety, low satisfaction. Western psychology rediscovered what Ibn al-Qayyim described in the 14th century.

---

### The cure

Ibn al-Qayyim's prescription is *ikhlas* — active sincerity. Not suppression of the desire to be seen, but deliberate reorientation of the deed toward Allah before, during, and after. Ikhlas is practiced, not waited for.

The du'a the Prophet ﷺ taught specifically for hidden shirk:

> *Allahumma inni a'udhu bika an ushrika bika wa ana a'lam*
> (O Allah, I seek refuge in You from knowingly associating partners with You.)

Recite it when recognition-seeking activates — before the action, not after.

---

### How to apply it

Before any action where recognition is possible, ask: *would I do this if no one ever found out?*

If the answer is no — pause. Don't shame yourself. Redirect. Restate the intention silently, make the du'a, then proceed or delay.

Over time, this practice builds the habit of checking intention *before* the action, not rationalizing it after.
