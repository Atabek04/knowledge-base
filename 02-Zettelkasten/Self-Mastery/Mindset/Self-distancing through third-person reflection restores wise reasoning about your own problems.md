When your own problem shrinks your judgment, you can borrow back the judgment you use for other people by looking at yourself from outside.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Self-distancing is reflecting on your own situation as an observer would: in the third person, by your own name, or as if it were happening to someone else.</mark>

---

### How it works

In the first person ("why am I so angry?") you are inside the problem and replay it. In the third person ("why is Atabek so angry?") you stand beside it and can examine it.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Distance lowers the grip of your personal stake, so the balanced reasoning you normally reserve for others' problems switches on for your own.</mark>

---

### Research backing

Grossmann and Kross (2014): asking people to reflect on their own conflict from a distanced, third-person view removed [[Solomon's paradox shows people reason more wisely about others' problems than their own|Solomon's paradox]]; they reasoned as wisely about themselves as about a friend.

Kross and colleagues (2014, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*): third-person self-talk before a stressful speech reduced anxiety and improved performance compared with first-person self-talk.

Grossmann and colleagues (2021, *Psychological Science*): participants who wrote a daily diary about their conflicts in the third person showed more wise reasoning over the following weeks than those who wrote in the first person.

---

### How to apply it

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Before deciding anything that touches your own interest, write the situation out in the third person using your own name, then write the advice you would give that person.</mark>

- "Atabek's manager criticised his work in front of the team. What should Atabek do?"
- Answer as a friend would, not as the person who was embarrassed.
- Then add the step the method lacks: take the draft to someone you trust, since distance is still your own view.

The last step is where the Islamic tradition goes further: [[Al-Ghazali gives four ways to see your own faults because the nafs hides them from itself|al-Ghazali's four ways]] bring in real outsiders, and the nightly muhasaba of [[Daily Self-Accounting Closes the Gap Between Plans and Execution|daily self-accounting]] is where a third-person diary entry fits.
