Everyone can list a friend's faults, and almost no one can list their own. In the *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din*, in the book on disciplining the soul (Riyadat al-Nafs), al-Ghazali asks how a person can find faults their own nafs keeps hidden.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">The nafs hides its own faults from itself, so a person needs a view from outside the self to see them.</mark>

---

### The four ways

1. <b>A shaykh with insight.</b> Sit with a teacher who sees the hidden faults of the soul, and accept what he points out.
2. <b>A truthful friend.</b> Choose a sincere, perceptive friend of good religion and ask him to watch you and tell you what he sees.
3. <b>Your enemies' words.</b> Listen to what enemies say about you, since hostility exposes the faults that friends hide out of kindness.
4. <b>Other people as a mirror.</b> Mix with people, and whatever you find blameworthy in them, suspect it in yourself.

The first three bring in an outside eye. The fourth turns your own sharp judgment of others back on yourself.

---

### The tradition behind it

- The Prophet ﷺ: al-mu'min mir'at al-mu'min, "the believer is the mirror of the believer" (Abu Dawud, graded hasan).
- Umar ibn al-Khattab: "May Allah have mercy on a man who gifts me my faults."
- Abu Hurayrah, in al-Bukhari's *al-Adab al-Mufrad*: "One of you sees the speck in his brother's eye and forgets the tree trunk in his own."

---

### How it relates to self-distancing

[[Self-distancing through third-person reflection restores wise reasoning about your own problems|Self-distancing]] moves your problem outward so you judge it like a friend's. Al-Ghazali's fourth way runs the other direction: it takes the judgment you already pass on others and applies it inward.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Self-distancing is still your own view from a different angle, while al-Ghazali's first three ways add a real second person, who is exactly what the nafs cannot counterfeit.</mark>

---

### How to apply it

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">When a fault in someone else irritates you, write it down and ask where the same fault shows up in you.</mark>

Pick one trusted friend and ask them directly: "What is one habit of mine you think I don't see?" Receive it as a gift, in Umar's sense, without defending yourself.
