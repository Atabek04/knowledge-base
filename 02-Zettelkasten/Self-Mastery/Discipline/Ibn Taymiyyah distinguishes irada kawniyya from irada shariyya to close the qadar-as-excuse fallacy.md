The rationalization goes: "Allah willed everything that happens. My sin happened. Therefore Allah willed my sin. Therefore I cannot be held responsible."

This argument takes a true premise and draws a false conclusion. Ibn Taymiyyah closes it with a precise theological distinction.

---

### The Two Divine Wills

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">**Irāda kawniyya**</mark> (ontological will / decree): what Allah *permits to occur*. Everything that exists and unfolds — including sins, kufr, evil — does so through Allah's kawnī permission. Without it, nothing could occur at all.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">**Irāda sharī'iyya**</mark> (legislative will / command): what Allah *loves, commands, and is pleased with*. This is the standard against which accountability is measured.

**These are not the same thing.**

Kufr occurs by Allah's kawnī permission — yet Allah explicitly states He does not love kufr (Q. 39:7). Sin occurs through kawnī permission — yet Allah commands against it. The sinner acts with the will Allah gave them, *against* Allah's sharī'ī will. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Accountability attaches to the sharī'ī standard, not the kawnī permission.</mark>

---

### Ibn Taymiyyah's verdict

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">"Whoever affirms Divine Decree but uses it to cancel commandments and prohibitions is **worse** than one who denies qadar altogether." *(al-Tahāwiyya commentary)*</mark>

Why worse? The denier of qadar at least maintains a coherent framework of human accountability. The one who weaponizes qadar has taken a true theological premise — Allah's sovereignty — and used it to neutralize the entire purpose of revelation, which is to command and prohibit.

---

### Ibn al-Qayyim's personal challenge (Madarij al-Sālikīn)

> "If your destiny were an argument for usurping your Lord's rights, O ignorant one — would it not be an excuse for your wife or your son?"

The logic is precise: you never accept qadar as justification from someone who wrongs *you*. You invoke it only to deflect Allah's claim on you. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">This exposes it not as theology but as self-serving rationalization dressed in theological language.</mark>
