The common assumption is that dopamine equals pleasure. It does not.

Berridge and Robinson's **incentive-sensitization theory** draws a precise neurological line between two distinct circuits:

- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">**Wanting**</mark> — encoded by mesolimbic dopamine (nucleus accumbens, VTA). Motivational salience: the urgent pull to *pursue*.
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">**Liking**</mark> — encoded by opioid circuits. Hedonic pleasure: the actual experience of satisfaction.

These systems are separate and can become decoupled.

---

### Why this matters

Drug cues → phasic dopamine spike → compulsive wanting — **independent of whether the drug still produces pleasure**.

An addict can genuinely hate what they are doing. The pleasure may be almost gone. But the wanting circuit does not care about rational evaluation — it fires in response to cues, not to conscious preference.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">"I know I won't enjoy it" does not defeat the craving.</mark> The craving and the enjoyment are measured by different systems. Addressing one does not address the other.

---

### The Islamic parallel

Ibn al-Qayyim observed the same phenomenon in *Ighathat al-Lahfan*. He describes how **shahwa** (the desire-pull) and **ladhdha** (actual pleasure) diverge under repeated sin: the nafs ammārah pushes toward pursuit even when the pleasure is hollow, because the *pull* has been trained and the satisfaction has not.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">What neuroscience calls incentive sensitization, Ibn al-Qayyim calls the trained nafs — a nafs that has learned to want without needing to receive.</mark>

---

### Practical implication

The craving responds to cues, not to willpower at the moment of temptation. This is why:

- <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Removing cues before the session (phone in another room, blockers on a schedule) works</mark> — the wanting circuit never fires
- Reasoning with yourself at the moment of wanting rarely works — that circuit is not listening
- The decision must be made at a moment of clarity, not at the moment of craving
