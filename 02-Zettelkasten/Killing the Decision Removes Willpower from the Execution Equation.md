Every time you must *decide* whether to start a task, you give your brain a veto. The nafs always votes for comfort. Willpower is finite — using it to start tasks depletes it before the task itself.

The solution: remove the decision entirely by pre-committing to when, where, and how.

## Implementation Intentions

Research by Peter Gollwitzer shows that specifying "I will do X at time Y in location Z" increases follow-through by 200–300% compared to "I intend to do X."

**Vague intention:** "I'll study Arabic today."  
**Implementation intention:** "After Asr, I open Anki at my desk for 20 minutes."

## Decision Fatigue

Every decision you make reduces cognitive resources for the next one. Successful people don't have more willpower — they structure their environment so fewer decisions are required.

Eliminate: what to do, when to do it, where to do it. Pre-decide all three.
