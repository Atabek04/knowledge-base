### The method

Don't pick a plan first.
<mark style="background: yellow">Identify the *imbalances* in the position — then let them dictate the plan.</mark>

Imbalances (Jeremy Silman, *How to Reassess Your Chess*): minor-piece quality, pawn structure, space, development, king safety, control of a key file or square.

---
### Why it beats "making a plan"

A plan chosen from intuition floats free of the position.
A plan derived from the imbalances is <mark style="background: cyan">anchored to the position's static facts</mark> — you play *with* the features instead of against them.

If you own the better minor piece, you steer toward a structure that showcases it; if you have more space, you avoid trades.

---
### Companion idea

*The Amateur's Mind* (same author) teaches the flip side: <mark style="background: green">audit your own thinking, because most amateur losses come from flawed reasoning, not missing knowledge</mark> — it transcribes real amateurs thinking aloud and erring.

<mark style="background: pink">Read *The Amateur's Mind* first if under ~1400; *Reassess* is the larger, more advanced continuation.</mark>
