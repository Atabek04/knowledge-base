The dinar (gold coin) comes from the Latin denarius, adopted through Hellenistic and Persian trade networks into Islamic empires.

**Etymology**: Latin denarius ("of ten") → Greek adoption → Arabic dinar (دينار)

## First Islamic Dinar

**Year**: AH 77 (696–697 CE)
**Issued by**: Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan
**Weight**: 1 mithqal = 4.25 grams
**Purity**: 96-98% pure gold (22-24 karats)

Abd al-Malik revolutionized Islamic coinage by removing all images and replacing them with **Arabic text and Quranic verses only**—establishing a distinctly Islamic standard.

## Dirham vs. Dinar

- **Dirham**: Silver, ~3 grams, from Greek drachma
- **Dinar**: Gold, 4.25 grams, from Latin denarius
- **Value Ratio**: 1 dinar = 10 dirhams

The gold dinar remained the Islamic standard from the 7th century through the Ottoman era (until 1918).
