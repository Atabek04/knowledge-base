Barter theory: Money invented because bartering goods directly was inefficient.

**The problem it solves**: When you trade only goods, you hit the "double coincidence of wants"—both parties must have what the other wants *at the same time*.

You have apples, want wheat. But the wheat farmer wants fish. The trade fails.

**Money as the solution**: A medium everyone accepts lets you break any trade into two steps: apples → money → wheat.

**Key assumption**: This theory assumes barter economies existed first, then money was invented to make trading easier.

(Compare this to [[Money originated as a record of debt between people|debt theory]], which argues money came from tracking obligations, not from solving barter problems.)
