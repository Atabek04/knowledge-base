Drew Bent's vision for education by 2030 is that <mark style="background: yellow">the technology becomes invisible but omnipresent</mark>.

The goal is not screens everywhere. It is AI quietly enabling something schools have never achieved at scale: <mark style="background: cyan">deep personalization for every learner</mark>.

---

### Why personalization is the prize

One-to-one tutoring has always produced the best outcomes — and has never been affordable for everyone.

AI makes a tutor-like experience cheap enough to give to all students at once. That is the "at scale" half of the idea.

---

### Educators as builders, not just users

This is already happening. Educators use Claude to build their own custom tools:

- <mark style="background: green">Bespoke flashcard apps</mark>
- <mark style="background: green">Assessments tuned to their class</mark>
- <mark style="background: green">Lesson plans and concept visualizations</mark>

The teacher stops being a passive consumer of edtech products and becomes a maker of them — which connects directly to [[AI agent building is the foundational career skill of the next thirty years|building with AI as a core skill]].

---

### The open risk

Personalization at scale only helps if it builds skill rather than replacing it.

<mark style="background: purple">Does an invisible AI tutor scaffold thinking, or quietly do it for the student?</mark> The [[Using AI to bypass work causes skill atrophy while using it for inquiry builds skill|atrophy research]] says the design choice decides.
