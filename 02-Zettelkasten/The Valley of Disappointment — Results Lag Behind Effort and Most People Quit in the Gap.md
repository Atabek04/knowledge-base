Progress in almost any domain is not linear. Effort compounds invisibly for weeks before results become visible. The gap between effort and visible reward is where most people quit.

James Clear calls this the Valley of Disappointment.

## The Pattern

```
Effort:  ████████████████████████
Results: ............▁▂▄████████
```

You work consistently. Nothing seems to change. You feel like it's not working. You stop — right before the inflection point.

## Why It Happens

- The brain expects proportional feedback (effort in → reward out)
- Compounding is invisible until it isn't — then it's sudden
- Quitting during the plateau feels rational because there's no visible evidence of progress

## The Fix

1. **Expect the plateau** — knowing it exists reduces the shock
2. **Measure leading indicators, not lagging outcomes** — track days completed, not results achieved
3. **Lengthen your time horizon** — ask "what will this look like in 6 months?" not "why hasn't it worked yet?"
