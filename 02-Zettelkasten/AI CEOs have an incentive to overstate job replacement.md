### The conflict of interest

When an AI CEO says *"this will replace the workforce,"* treat it as **marketing, not forecasting**.

<mark style="background: yellow">Claiming your product will remake the economy justifies sky-high valuations and the massive capital spend on compute.</mark>

It also deflects scrutiny of whether that spending is financially sustainable.

---
### Why it's a strange thing to brag about

Newport's analogy: it's like a Pfizer exec announcing a pill that **cures psoriasis but turns half the population into zombies** — and saying it as a *selling point*.

<mark style="background: cyan">A leader cheerfully predicting their own product will gut employment is a signal of hype incentives, not neutral expertise.</mark>

---
### The reporting failure

Doomsday articles quote these CEOs *as authorities*, when they are the **most conflicted possible source**.

<mark style="background: pink">The right epistemic move: weight forecasts by the forecaster's incentive — discount sources who profit from the prediction.</mark>
