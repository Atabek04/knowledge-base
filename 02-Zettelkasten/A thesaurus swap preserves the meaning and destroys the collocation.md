The instinct at band 7 is to raise Lexical Resource by replacing common words with rarer ones. The descriptor does not reward that, and the mechanism explains why.

A thesaurus substitution keeps the denotation and loses the word's partners. *Commit a crime* becomes *make a crime*; *make a decision* becomes *do a decision*; *heavy rain* becomes *strong rain*. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The result is a rarer word scored as an inaccuracy in word choice and collocation</mark> — a fault already listed at band 7, and the criterion explicitly assesses the control and use of collocations.

Nesselhauf's corpus work on advanced learners found collocation is exactly where advanced learners still fail, independently of how large their vocabulary is. Range and collocation are separate axes, and only one of them is fixed by learning more words.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">So the paraphrase target is the whole phrase in context, not the noun.</mark> *Young people commit crimes* becomes *juvenile offending* — a shift of construction, not a swap of a word.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The operational rule: a word you have read but never used stays out of the essay.</mark> Long words are frequently just harder to spell, and spelling errors are counted too.
