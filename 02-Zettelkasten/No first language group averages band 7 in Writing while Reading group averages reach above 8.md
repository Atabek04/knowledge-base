Across the 40 first-language groups in the official 2024-25 Academic data, the four sections behave very differently in spread.

| Section | Lowest group mean | Highest | Range | SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | 5.70 | 7.84 | 2.14 | 0.46 |
| Reading | 5.92 | 8.15 | 2.23 | 0.45 |
| Writing | 5.54 | **6.64** | **1.10** | **0.24** |
| Speaking | 5.45 | 7.52 | 2.07 | 0.45 |

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Writing's spread is half that of every other section and its top is 6.64.</mark> No language group in the world averages band 7 in Writing; thirteen of forty clear 7.0 in Reading, and the best reaches 8.15. Only two groups reach even 6.5 in Writing.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Compressed variance with a hard upper bound is what a ceiling looks like in aggregate data.</mark> Listening and Reading have long upper tails — the skills respond to training far enough that whole populations reach very high averages. Writing does not.

The honest caveat: these are group means, not individual trajectories, and the compression is equally consistent with Writing being marked conservatively by rubric design. The data shows the ceiling exists; it cannot say which of the two causes it.
