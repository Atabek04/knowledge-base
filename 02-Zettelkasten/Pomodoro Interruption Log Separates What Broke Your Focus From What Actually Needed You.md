Francesco Cirillo's Pomodoro Technique is often described as "work 25 minutes, break 5 minutes."

That's the shell. The core is the interruption log.

### Two Types of Interruptions

Cirillo distinguishes internal from external interruptions, and tracks them separately.

**Internal interruptions** — arise from you. The urge to check your phone, look something up, get water, think about something else. Mark each with an apostrophe (`'`) on your tracking sheet when it happens.

**External interruptions** — arrive from others. A colleague, a Slack message, a call. Mark each with a dash (`-`).

You do not act on either during the Pomodoro. You mark them and continue.

---

### Why the Log Changes Everything

Most people think their interruptions come from outside.

The log reveals how many come from inside.

Seeing a string of `'` marks after a session is uncomfortable — and useful. It makes the invisible visible: your own attention is fragmenting your work, not just the environment.

Over days, patterns emerge. Which situations produce more internal urges? Which time slots produce more external interruptions?

You can only fix what you can see.

---

### The External Interruption Protocol

For external interruptions, Cirillo recommends: **Inform, Negotiate, Call Back.**

1. Inform the person you're in a focused session
2. Negotiate a specific time to follow up ("Can we talk at 3pm?")
3. Schedule it — and mark the dash

This is not rudeness. It is a professional response that respects both parties.

This protocol is the Pomodoro's built-in [[If-Then Plans Pre-Decide Your Response to Interruptions Before They Happen|implementation intention]] — a pre-decided script for when someone interrupts you.

---

### What the Log Tells You Over Time

- High `'` days = focus problem, not urgency problem
- High `-` days = environment problem — interruptions are coming from outside
- Both high = no system is protecting your attention

Use the data in your [[Daily Self-Accounting Closes the Gap Between Plans and Execution|daily review]] to adjust the next day's setup.
