Task Response and Coherence and Cohesion are two of the four criteria, so <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">half the Task 2 mark is determined by what you decided to say and in what order</mark> — both settled before the prose starts. Lexical Resource and Grammar are the other half and are the only part the writing itself controls.

Teachers disagree on the minutes: E2 says 3-5, IELTS Liz says at least 5, IELTS Advantage says 10 out of the 40. The disagreement is smaller than it looks, because all three describe the same contents.

The plan holds five things: the question type, and therefore how many tasks the prompt owes; <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">the position written out as one sentence, which becomes the thesis verbatim</mark>; two main ideas, chosen rather than brainstormed; one specific support or example per idea, decided now; and two or three topic-vocabulary items you are certain of.

The fourth item is the one that carries the 7-to-8 move. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Deciding the concrete example under time pressure mid-paragraph is what produces over-generalisation</mark> — the writer reaches for the abstract restatement because nothing specific is to hand.
