When a task requires sustained attention for a prolonged period, performance drops and error rates rise.

This is called the **vigilance decrement** (also: the time-on-task effect).

It is not laziness. It is a predictable physiological and cognitive process.

---

### When It Hits

Degradation typically begins within **20–30 minutes** of monotonous, low-feedback work.

The key factor is not total time worked — it is time spent on **repetitive, low-novelty** tasks that provide little feedback or reward signal.

High-novelty or high-stakes tasks (e.g. a live coding interview, urgent debugging) delay the decrement because dopamine and norepinephrine stay elevated. Routine tasks (reviewing docs, repetitive practice, reading) trigger it sooner.

---

### Three Theories of Why It Happens

**1. Overload (resource depletion)**
Sustained attention draws on a limited pool of cognitive resources. Continuous focused work drains this pool. Performance drops as resources approach zero.

**2. Underload (boredom / monotony)**
Repetitive tasks provide insufficient stimulation for the brain's arousal systems. The [[The Default Mode Network Pulls the Brain Away from Hard Tasks Toward Mental Drift|Default Mode Network]] activates as the task fails to compete for attention. Mind-wandering increases and feels productive even as focus dissolves.

**3. Motivational decay**
Without feedback or visible progress, the brain downweights the value of continuing. The cost-benefit calculation tilts toward doing something else.

Most sessions combine all three — underload causes DMN drift, motivation decays, and the attentional resource pool depletes in parallel.

---

### Boredom Is a Symptom, Not a Cause

Boredom signals that the vigilance decrement is underway.

It means: the task is no longer generating enough novelty or feedback to hold directed attention. The brain is not broken — it is responding rationally to a signal-poor environment.

Reframing the task (inject novelty, change medium, set a sub-goal) can reset the signal temporarily.

---

### What It Looks Like in Practice

- Re-reading the same sentence three times without retaining it
- Making careless errors you would not make fresh
- Checking the clock repeatedly
- Thoughts drifting to unrelated topics between paragraphs
- Feeling vague dissatisfaction without knowing why

---

### The Structural Fix

The vigilance decrement is not optional — it cannot be overcome by willpower alone.

The correct response is structural: **time-box the session and schedule the break before the decrement peaks**.

The [[Pomodoro session cycle alternates four timed focus blocks with short breaks and one long recovery break|Pomodoro cycle's]] 25-minute interval is calibrated to end before the worst degradation sets in.

Pushing past the decrement produces more errors and slower work, not more output.
