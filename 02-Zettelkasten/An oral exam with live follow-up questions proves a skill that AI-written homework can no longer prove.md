When an AI can write the essay or solve the problem set in seconds, a submitted answer no longer shows that the student has the skill. Assessment needs a format where the knowledge must be in the student's head at the moment of testing.

### The viva as the AI-proof format

A **viva** (Latin *viva voce*, "by living voice") is an oral exam: the student explains aloud and the examiner asks follow-up questions.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Follow-up questions defeat copied work because each question is built from the student's last answer, so a memorised or AI-written answer runs out after one or two probes.</mark>

At Dartmouth (SIGCSE 2026), students who had submitted polished code often could not explain their own design choices after two follow-ups.

#### Why vivas never scaled

A 15-minute viva for 300 students costs about 75 hours of one examiner's time per exam, so schools fell back on written tests.

---

### The AI examiner

An AI that asks the follow-ups removes the time cost. Pilots already exist: Socratic Mind at Georgia Tech (Lee et al., 2025), and voice-AI oral exams personalised per student (Ipeirotis and Rizakos, 2026). Barba and Stegner (2026) showed human-run orals can also scale, with 58 students examined in two days.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">An AI examiner is only trustworthy if three things hold: its verdict agrees with a human examiner, the speaker is the student with no hidden AI whispering answers, and it does not mark an accent as a gap in knowledge.</mark>

Whisper tools already exist for job interviews (Cluely, Interview Coder), so identity is the first risk to break. AI speech scoring matches human judges on controlled tasks but less on spontaneous speech (Liu et al., 2025).
