### The simpler explanation

The headline story is "AI replaced these workers." The boring-but-better-supported story is a **hiring bubble bursting**.

During the pandemic, digital demand spiked and tech firms went on a **hiring spree**. The 2024–2025 layoffs are largely the **correction** to that over-hiring — plus investor margin pressure and organizational bloat.

<mark style="background: yellow">IBM's CEO Arvind Krishna attributed the layoff wave to solving over-hiring, not to AI.</mark>

---
### The timing tell

Amazon's cuts began in **2022–2023, before generative AI was deployable at scale**, and aimed to *"trim layers of management"* and *"reduce bureaucracy."*

<mark style="background: cyan">A cause can't post-date its effect — layoffs that predate the AI boom can't be caused by it.</mark>

---
### The honest caveat

The story is evolving. The **2025 wave** looks less like pure over-hiring correction and more like **strategic restructuring** — some of it genuinely to fund or adopt AI.

<mark style="background: pink">"AI" is also a convenient cover story: it sounds more visionary than admitting you over-hired.</mark>
