The dominant distractor mechanism across Listening question types is not a wrong answer placed elsewhere — it is <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the right-sounding answer said first and then cancelled</mark>.

The cancellation markers are a short, learnable set: *sorry*, *actually*, *I meant*, *but*, *however*, and a past tense used against a present state — *this **was** the library, but we moved it*. When one appears, the value after it is the answer.

Type by type, the same idea wears different clothes.

Multiple choice speaks all three options aloud, out of order. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The distractor is the one that word-matches the printed option; the key is the one that is paraphrased.</mark>

Map and plan labelling runs its questions in audio order but scatters them across the map, so the failure is tracking geography instead of question numbers — and the audio plays once, making a lost place unrecoverable.

Matching in Part 3 turns on attributing an opinion to the right speaker after one proposes, the other objects and the first concedes. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">The answer is the final position, not the first or the loudest.</mark>

Part 4 has a structural trap of its own: its mid-part pause is silent and unannounced, and all reading time is given at the start — so all ten questions must be pre-read in one block.
