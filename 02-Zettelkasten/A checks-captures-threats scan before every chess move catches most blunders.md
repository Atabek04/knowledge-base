### The habit

Before committing any move, run a <mark style="background: yellow">Checks–Captures–Threats (CCT) scan from your *opponent's* side: what are their checks, their captures, and their threats after I play this?</mark>

It is the single cheapest safeguard against rating-losing oversights.

---
### Why it works

Most club-level losses are simple oversights, not strategic failures.
A repeatable safety pass filters blunders before your hand commits.

<mark style="background: pink">The most common failure is scanning only your *own* forcing moves and forgetting the opponent's.</mark>

A useful two-question version: *"What does my opponent's last move now do — and what did it stop defending or unblock?"*

---
### The catch nobody admits

The checklist alone fails if you don't actually run it.
Players who *know* CCT still hang pieces because they <mark style="background: cyan">move too fast</mark> — see [[Most chess blunders are impulse errors not knowledge gaps]].

The real fix is behavioral: slow down and make the scan automatic, every move, no exceptions.
