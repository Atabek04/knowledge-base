### The principle

<mark style="background: yellow">Spatial contiguity</mark> (Mayer, 2001): people learn better when related elements are placed adjacent rather than separated.

In bilingual slide design, this applies directly to a target-language word and its L1 translation. Placing them spatially close eliminates visual search — the learner connects word to meaning without any working memory cost beyond the learning itself.

Schroeder & Cenkci (2018) meta-analyzed 58 comparisons (n = 2,426) on spatial split-attention in learning materials and found **g = 0.63** — a medium-to-large effect favoring contiguous placement.

---

### The split-attention trap

Split-attention failure occurs when designers place:
- The target-language word on screen and the translation in a footnote or legend at the bottom
- Two related elements in separate panels with 300+ px between them
- Annotations separated from the diagram they describe

Each visual search move consumes working memory without contributing to learning. Across a 10-minute vocabulary video, this overhead accumulates substantially.

---

### Eye-tracking evidence on position priority

A 2024 eye-tracking study on bilingual subtitles (Cambridge) found learners exhibit a strong **top-line preference** — reflexively fixating on whichever text occupies the top line, regardless of which language it is.

Wang (2022, *Language Learning*) found that only fixation time on the *L2 word form* — not on the translation — predicted vocabulary acquisition.

<mark style="background: cyan">Design tension:</mark> learners naturally look at the top line first. Putting L1 on top = easier comprehension. Putting L2 on top = better acquisition. Resolution: place L2 on top and make it visually heavier (larger, slightly bolder), so the hierarchy reinforces the reading order.

---

### Format recommendation

For a vocabulary slide:
- L2 target word: top, larger font, bold weight
- L1 translation: directly below, smaller, regular weight
- Vertical gap between them: minimal (8–12 px)

For running subtitles: L2 caption line above, L1 gloss immediately below on the same card — never separated to different screen regions.
