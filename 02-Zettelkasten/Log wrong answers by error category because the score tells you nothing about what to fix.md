The most recurrent method in write-ups by candidates who moved into the 8s is not "do more mocks" — it is <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">logging every wrong answer by category and then attacking only the categories</mark>.

One 7.0 → 8.0 account found the whole picture on doing this: the Reading loss was concentrated in False-versus-Not-Given, and the Listening loss was almost entirely word form — *travel* for *travelling*, *close* for *closed*. Two mechanical fixes worth roughly a band between them, and <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">both invisible to anyone tracking their raw score</mark>.

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This makes the test a measuring instrument, not the study session.</mark> A four-hour mock without the review afterwards has produced a number and consumed most of a week's budget at six hours.

The same logic sets the review's priority: sort categories by how many marks each cost, then fix in that order. An error type appearing once is noise; one appearing four times across two tests is the next week's work.
