### The reframing

Narayanan and Kapoor argue AI is best understood as a **general-purpose technology** — more like electricity or the internet than "an alien mind."

Transformative, yes; unprecedented, no.

<mark style="background: yellow">If AI is normal technology, human institutions can shape how it diffuses — it isn't an unstoppable force.</mark>

---
### Why the framing matters for accountability

The doom framing treats disruption as **inevitable**, which conveniently lets companies off the hook for *how* they deploy AI and manage staff.

<mark style="background: cyan">Calling AI "normal" puts agency back: layoffs, deployment choices, and regulation become decisions someone is responsible for.</mark>

It points to standard tools — labor negotiation, regulation — rather than panic.

---
### What the 2026 data supports

Macro-strategists (Citadel, the Fed) see **no imminent AI-triggered collapse**:

- The labor market is in a **"low hire, low fire" freeze**, not a spiral; unemployment ~4.4%, forecast ~4.5% by end of 2026.
- The **NY Fed** found *"little indication of a distinct AI-driven decline in labor demand"* — high-exposure job postings began diverging **before** ChatGPT, and entry-level vs senior roles move **in parallel**.

<mark style="background: pink">That last point directly tempers the Stanford "canaries" reading — the entry-level signal is real but contested, not settled.</mark>
