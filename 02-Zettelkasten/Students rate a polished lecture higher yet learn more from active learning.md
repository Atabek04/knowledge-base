A common argument says that once AI can explain anything, the lecture loses its point. The evidence against the lecture is older than AI, and it points at something deeper than who does the explaining.

### Lecture-only teaching fails more students

A **lecture** here means passive delivery: one person explains, everyone else listens. **Active learning** means students *act* during class: they solve, predict, discuss.

Freeman et al. (2014, PNAS) pooled 225 studies of STEM courses. Failure rates were 33.8% under lecture-only teaching and 21.8% with active learning.

---

### Students judge the lecture as the better one

Deslauriers et al. (2019, PNAS) taught the same physics students twice: once by a fluent, well-rated lecturer (a **polished lecture**), once by active learning.

Students scored higher on the test after the active session. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Students rated the polished lecture as the session they learned more from, while their test scores showed the active session taught them more.</mark>

#### Why the feeling misleads

A smooth explanation asks nothing of the learner, so nothing feels hard. That ease is the [[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency|fluency illusion]]: smooth input feels like understanding because nothing in it was hard.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Active work feels worse because the effort is visible, and the effort is what builds the memory.</mark> The authors traced the lower ratings to that extra cognitive effort.

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Student satisfaction is not evidence of learning; only a delayed test without help measures it.</mark>

---

### What this means for AI explanations

A perfect ChatGPT explanation is a polished lecture with unlimited patience. The learner only listens, so the next day without AI, little remains.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">An AI tutor must make the learner produce something before it explains, never explain first.</mark> This is the design behind a [[An AI tutor that gives hints instead of answers avoids the learning loss a plain chatbot causes|hint-only AI tutor]].
