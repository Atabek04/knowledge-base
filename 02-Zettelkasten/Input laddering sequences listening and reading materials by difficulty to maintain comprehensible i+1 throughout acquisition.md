### What it is

Input laddering is a framework for sequencing language materials from lower to higher difficulty while maintaining the learner at ~98% comprehension (i+1) throughout the acquisition journey.

The governing variable is Nation's vocabulary coverage threshold: as vocabulary size grows, the set of materials that qualify as i+1 expands. The ladder tracks this expansion and selects materials accordingly.

---

### A practical ladder (English)

| Stage | Representative materials | Approx. vocabulary size |
|---|---|---|
| A1–A2 | Graded readers (Starter/Elementary), learner podcasts (e.g., ESL Pod) | 500–2,000 word families |
| A2–B1 | Graded readers (Intermediate), children's books, simplified news | 2,000–4,000 word families |
| B1–B2 | Young adult fiction, VOA Special English, TV with subtitles in L2 | 4,000–6,000 word families |
| B2–C1 | Native fiction, podcasts with transcripts, academic articles in familiar domains | 6,000–8,000 word families |
| C1+ | Authentic input across domains without scaffolding | 8,000+ word families |

---

### Monitoring i+1 while laddering

**Reading:** >2–3 unknown words per page → material is above i+1 → drop one level.

**Listening:** Can't follow the main idea of a segment without replaying → above i+1 → find easier material.

The ladder moves up only when the learner can sustain 98% coverage with the current tier — not when a timer says "you've been at this level long enough."

---

### Common mistakes

- <mark style="background: pink">Jumping to "authentic" content too early</mark> — native TV at A2 is not i+1; it is incomprehensible noise that produces no acquisition signal regardless of volume
- Staying at graded readers too long — comfortable but acquisition-inefficient if unknown words become too rare to expand vocabulary
- Using difficulty labels (Beginner/Intermediate) instead of measuring actual vocabulary coverage — labels are inconsistent across publishers

---

### Relationship to deliberate vocabulary study

The ladder describes material sequencing for [[Comprehensible input at i+1 requires graded exposure sequenced by difficulty to drive acquisition|comprehensible input]] acquisition.

Deliberate vocabulary study (SRS, frequency lists) raises the vocabulary size threshold, which *unlocks the next rung of the ladder* — the two strategies are mutually reinforcing.
