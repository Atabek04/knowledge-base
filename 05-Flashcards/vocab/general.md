English Card
Prisoner's Dilemma
Back: A game theory scenario where two rational individuals, **each acting in self-interest, end up worse off** than if they had cooperated.
- Both silent → both go free; one betrays → betrayer free, silent one gets the maximum; both betray → both punished moderately
- Self-interest drives both to betray, so **individually rational decisions produce a collectively irrational outcome**
Russian: Дилемма заключённого
Example: Price wars between companies are a classic **Prisoner's Dilemma** — both would profit more by keeping prices high, but each fears being undercut.

START
English Card
arbitrary
Back: Chosen at random or by personal whim, with **no reason or system behind the choice**.
- The complaint is about the *absence of justification*, not about being wrong
- Opposite: **principled** — chosen for a stated reason that also explains what is excluded (also *deliberate*, *systematic*, *justified*)
Russian: произвольный, взятый с потолка, ничем не обоснованный
Example: Only three operations may be performed on a system of linear equations. The list looks **arbitrary** until you notice what the three have in common, and why a fourth obvious-looking candidate is missing.
Forms: arbitrary · arbitrarily · arbitrariness
Collocations: an **arbitrary** number · an **arbitrary** decision · **seemingly** arbitrary · **entirely** arbitrary
Synonyms: **random** — no pattern at all; arbitrary implies a chooser who simply had no reason
**capricious** — arbitrary *and* changeable, used of people and authority, never of a number
