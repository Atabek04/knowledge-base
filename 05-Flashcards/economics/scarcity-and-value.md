Coding Questions
What is the **water-diamond paradox**?
Back: Water is **vital but nearly free**; diamonds are **not needed for life but expensive**.
- Stated by Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776)

Coding Questions
What **solves** the water-diamond paradox?
Back: **Marginal utility**: price follows the usefulness of the **next unit**, not the total usefulness of the whole supply.
- Water: plenty already, so the next litre is worth almost nothing
- Diamonds: few exist, so the next one is still worth a lot

Coding Questions
Why is the same glass of water cheap at home but priceless in a desert?
Back: The water is identical; **how much you already have** changed.
- At home the next glass is barely missed
- In the desert the next glass may save your life

What is **diminishing marginal utility**?
Back: Each extra unit of the same good is **worth less than the one before**.
- First glass for a thirsty person > fifth > twentieth

Trap: does "essential" mean "expensive"?
Back: **No.** A thing can be **vital and still cheap** if there is plenty of it.
- Price tracks the **next unit**, not how much we need the good overall
