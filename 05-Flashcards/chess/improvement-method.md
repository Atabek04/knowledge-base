Coding Questions
Of all chess training activities, which gives the most improvement per hour — and why?
Back:
**Analyzing your own games** (especially losses).
- Playing trains instinct; analysis trains understanding
- Your games contain the *recurring pattern of your mistakes* — the exact data telling you what to train next

Coding Questions
Why annotate a chess game yourself *before* turning on the engine?
Back: The **gap between your evaluation and the engine's is the lesson**.
- Hit "analyze" first → you outsource the thinking and learn nothing
- Annotating first exposes where your judgment diverges from the truth

In the engine-review discipline, what do you look for instead of checking every move?
Back: The **largest evaluation swing** — that's where the game was decided. The rest is noise.

What should every engine line be converted into before you move on?

A +1.8 number trains nothing; a human concept transfers to future games.

Below ~1800 ELO, what decides the overwhelming majority of games?
Back: **Tactics** — a missed fork, a hanging piece, a one-move blunder.
- ~95% of sub-2000 games turn on a tactical error, not strategy or opening prep
- So tactics is the dominant lever for rating gain

Coding Questions
Why play rapid/classical rather than bullet when the goal is to improve?
Back: Improvement is **deliberate practice** — it needs time per move to calculate, evaluate, and plan.
- Blitz only rehearses speed/recall you already have
- Bullet rewards premoving and not checking — habits that bleed into slow games
Tags: chess improvement
END

START
Coding Questions
What is Capablanca's argument for studying endgames early?
Back: The endgame is the **one phase you can master in isolation**.
- Openings/middlegames must be understood *in relation to* the endgames they lead to
- Knowing where positions resolve gives a yardstick for judging earlier moves
Tags: chess improvement endgames
END

Coding Questions
What is the CCT blunder-check, and whose forcing moves does it scan?
Back: Before committing a move, scan **Checks–Captures–Threats from the opponent's side**.
- "What are *their* checks, captures, threats after I play this?"
- Most common failure: scanning only your *own* forcing moves

Coding Questions
Players who know the blunder-check still hang pieces — what's the real cause and fix?
Back: It's a **behavioral failure, not a knowledge gap** — they move too fast.
- Fix is the clock: use your time, sit on your hands, don't play your first idea
- The checklist does nothing if you don't actually run it

Coding Questions
Why is deep opening memorization wasted below ~1800?
Back: Games at this level turn on a **middlegame tactic**, not opening theory.
- Memorized lines collapse the moment the opponent deviates (which amateurs do constantly)
- As tactics improve, openings get easier to learn anyway
Tags: chess improvement openings

Coding Questions
Why keep a small opening repertoire for a year instead of collecting many openings?
Back: You learn an opening's **plans and pawn structures by repeatedly reaching the same positions** — depth beats breadth.
- The 100th time you reach your structure, you understand its middlegame
- Systems (London, Colle) only help if you learn their plans, not just move order
Tags: chess improvement openings
END

Coding Questions
What single mechanism unites the Woodpecker Method, de la Maza's Seven Circles, and spaced repetition?
Back: **Re-solving a fixed puzzle set until recognition is automatic** — moving tactics from conscious calculation to instant recall.
- They differ only in *scheduling*, not mechanism
- Mechanism is sound; the dramatic rating claims rest on anecdote
Tags: chess improvement tactics
END

Coding Questions
According to GM Studer, what must happen when you break a chess plateau, and for how long should you commit?
Back: You get **temporarily worse** — new habits are clumsy and your rating dips first.
- Most players quit during the dip and stay stuck
- Commit **1–3 months** before judging a change; decouple identity from rating
Tags: chess improvement plateau
END

Published study-time splits contradict each other — what rule survives the disagreement?
Back: **Weight training toward where your own games are actually lost** — your measured weakness.
- A generic ratio guesses at the average player's weakness
- The diagnostic loop (analyze → find recurring error → train it) overrides any fixed %
