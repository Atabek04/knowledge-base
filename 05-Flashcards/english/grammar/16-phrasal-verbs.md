START
English Card
Where can the object go in *I turned on the light*? *(two positions, one restriction)*
Back: **A noun object goes either side; a pronoun object must go in the middle.**
- *I turned on the light.* or *I turned the light on.* — both fine
- *I turned it on.* (not *I turned on it*)
- Same for *take them off* (not *take off them*), *wake her up* (not *wake up her*), *throw it away* (not *throw away it*)
- Rule of thumb: **it/them/me/him/her never sit after the particle**

START
English Grammar
I want to keep this box, so don't {1:throw it away} (throw / away / it).
Explanation: The object is a pronoun, so it splits the verb and the particle.
- *throw away it* is the error — the particle can never come between the verb and a pronoun object
- With a noun both orders work: *don't throw away this box* / *don't throw this box away*

English Grammar
These shoes are uncomfortable. I'm going to {1:take them off} (take / off / them).
Explanation: Pronoun object → verb + pronoun + particle.
- *take off them* is wrong; *take off my shoes* and *take my shoes off* are both right
- Russian keeps the prefix glued to the verb (*снять*), so learners default to keeping *take off* together

START
English Card
What is a phrasal verb, and how does its meaning relate to its two parts? *(two cases)*
Back: **Sometimes literal movement, sometimes a special meaning you cannot derive.**
- Literal: *get on* the bus, *drive off*, *come back*, *turn round*
- Special: *break down* = stop working · *find out* = discover · *take off* = go into the air · *give up* = stop trying · *get by* = manage
- In Listening the special meanings are the ones that cost marks — the words give you nothing

START
English Card
What do *in* and *out* mean as particles, and what changes when an object follows? *(the in/into and out/out of pair)*
Back: **in = into a room, building or car; out = out of one. Add the noun and they become *into* / *out of*.**
- *I'm moving in on Friday.* → *I'm moving into my new flat on Friday.*
- *She climbed out.* → *She climbed out of the pool.*
- Family: go/come/walk/break **in**, let yourself **in**, check **in** — go/get/move **out**, check **out**, be locked **out**

*drop in* · *join in* · *plug in* · *take somebody in* · *fill in* — what does each mean?
Back: **Visit unannounced · take part · connect to electricity · deceive · complete a form.**
- *I dropped in to see Chris on my way home.*

English Card
*grow up* vs *bring up* — what is the difference?
Back: **You grow up (become an adult); somebody brings you up (raises you).**
- *Amy was born in Hong Kong but grew up in Australia.*
- *She was brought up by her grandparents.* (= raised)
- *grow up* has no object and never a passive; *bring up* almost always appears in the passive here

What does *put up with* mean, and why is its shape unusual?

What do *away* and *back* mean as a pair?
