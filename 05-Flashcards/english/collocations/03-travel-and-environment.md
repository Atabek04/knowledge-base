START
English Card
Rain falling hard, in the ordinary neutral way → ______ rain
Back: **heavy rain** — NOT *strong rain*.
- The book flags this one explicitly as a mistake
- Russian *сильный дождь* is literally "strong rain", which is exactly why it comes out wrong
- Verb form: *it rained heavily all day yesterday*

START
English Card
Rain so extreme it is a disaster-level downpour → ______ rain
Back: **torrential rain** — and *driving rain* for rain falling fast and heavily, usually blown at you.
- *I've never seen such torrential rain!*
- *Road conditions are difficult because of the driving rain.*
- Informal spoken alternative: *it's been pouring with rain* — avoid in Task 2

START
English Card
Wind that is blowing hard → ______ wind (two adjectives)
Back: **strong wind** · **high winds** — two that work; NOT *hard wind*.
- *Quite a strong wind is blowing.* / *High winds are expected tonight.*
- Opposite: **light wind**
- **biting winds** = very cold winds, not merely fast ones

English Card
The wind gets stronger / gets weaker → the wind ______ (two opposite verbs)
Back: **picks up** [gets stronger] ↔ **dies down** [gets weaker].
- *The wind was light this morning but it's picking up now.*
- Sound verb: *the wind was **whistling** through the trees*

English Card
Fog so thick and widespread that it covers everything → a ______ of fog
Back: **a blanket of fog** — literary register, so keep it out of a formal report.
- Neutral everyday equivalents: **thick fog** · **dense fog**
- Smaller, scattered areas are **patches of fog/mist**

START
English Card
Which noun takes *day*, *boat*, *business* and *camping*? → a day ______, a boat ______
Back: **trip** — day trip, boat trip, business trip, camping trip.
- **trip** = a short journey with a purpose, there and back
- **journey** = the travelling itself (long, tiring, overnight, return)
- **travel** = the general activity, uncountable
