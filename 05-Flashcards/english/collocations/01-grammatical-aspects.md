START
English Card
Very improbable, in essay register → ______ unlikely
Back: **highly unlikely** — *It is highly unlikely that I'll finish my work on time.*
- *highly* takes the probability words: **likely** · **unlikely** · **unusual**
- This is the band-8 replacement for *very unlikely* in Task 2 hedging

START
English Card
Which adjectives does *highly* intensify, and what is the one exception to its positive meaning?
Back: **highly** goes with strongly positive adjectives: **successful** · **competitive** · **profitable** · **effective** · **recommended** — plus probability words.
- The exception is **highly controversial**, which is not positive
- *Extremely* works with all the opposites too, except *recommended*

START
English Card
Very tired, at the extreme end → ______ exhausted
Back: **absolutely exhausted**, or the slightly more formal **utterly exhausted**.
- The rule: *very tired* ✓ but *very exhausted* ✗ — extreme adjectives reject *very*
- Same pattern: **absolutely/utterly ridiculous · stupid · impossible · wrong**

START
English Card
Why can you say *very tired* but not *very exhausted*?
Back: Because *exhausted* is already an **extreme adjective** — extremes take **absolutely** or **utterly**, not *very*.
- Gradable → *very*; ungradable/extreme → *absolutely*, *utterly*
- *utterly* is the more formal of the pair and often carries negative connotation

START
English Card
What is the basic meaning split between *make* and *do*?
Back: **make** = producing something; **do** = performing an action.
- *make a decision, a comment, an excuse* — all produce something new
- *do the ironing, do your homework, do some work* — all perform a task
- Russian **делать** covers both, which is why the pair is guessed rather than known

English Card
Losing your hair, your sight, your hearing — which verb?
Back: **go** — people **go bald / grey / blind / deaf / mad**.
- *Go* covers changes in personality, appearance and physical ability
- Not *get bald*; the student error *I was getting crazy* should be *I was going crazy*

START
English Card
The sky changing colour at sunset — which verb, and how does it differ from *go*?
Back: **turn** — *The sky turned gold as the sun set.*
- *Turn* is the neutral verb for colour change: *the tomatoes turn red*, *her hair turned white*
- *go* + colour implies decline (*went yellow*); *turn* + colour is simply descriptive

START
English Card
Which of *get* and *become* belongs in an essay, and why?
Back: **become** — same collocations as *get* (angry, bored, excited, depressed, upset, violent, involved, pregnant) but **more formal**.
- *He became depressed after his wife's death.* / *I would like to become involved in…*
- Swapping *get* for *become* is a free register upgrade in Task 2
