## Solomon's Paradox

START
Coding Questions
What is Solomon's paradox?
Back:
The **self-other asymmetry** in wise reasoning: people reason more wisely about **other people's problems** than about their own.

About a friend's crisis you stay balanced; about your own you narrow to one view and high certainty.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge

END

START
Coding Questions
What did Grossmann & Kross (2014) find in their infidelity study?
Back:
- People imagining a partner's infidelity happening to **a friend** reasoned more wisely than when it happened to **themselves**.
- The gap held for younger (20 to 40) and older (60 to 80) adults alike, so age did not fix it.
- Reflecting on their own case from a **distanced, third-person view** removed the gap.

END

START
Coding Questions
Why does Solomon's paradox show the failure is not a lack of knowledge?
Back:
The wise reasoning **already works** on a friend's case.

What fails is applying it when **your own stake** is involved, so more information does not close the gap.

START
Coding Questions
Why is the name "Solomon's paradox" problematic from an Islamic view?
Back:
It rests on the **biblical story** that Solomon judged wisely for others but fell into idolatry himself.

Islam rejects this: Sulayman is a **prophet**, and the Qur'an clears him (al-Baqarah 2:102, "Sulayman did not disbelieve").

Keep the finding, drop the story.

## Self-Distancing

START
Coding Questions
What is self-distancing, and why does it restore wise reasoning?
Back:
**Self-distancing**: reflecting on your own situation as an observer would, in the third person or by your own name.

Distance loosens the grip of your **personal stake**, so the balanced reasoning you use for others switches on for yourself.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787886-->
END

START
Coding Questions
What did Kross and colleagues (2014) find about third-person self-talk before a stressful speech?
Back:
Third-person self-talk ("Why is Atabek nervous?") **reduced anxiety** and **improved performance** compared with first-person self-talk ("Why am I nervous?").

<!--ID: 1790710787888-->
END

START
Coding Questions
What did Grossmann and colleagues (2021) find with the distanced diary method?
Back:
People who wrote a **daily diary about their conflicts in the third person** showed **more wise reasoning** over the following weeks than those writing in the first person.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787889-->
END

START
Coding Questions
How do you apply self-distancing to a personal decision, and what step does the method lack?
Back:
1. Write the situation in the **third person with your own name**.
2. Write the advice you would give that person as a friend.
3. Missing step: take the draft to **someone you trust**, because distance is still only your own view.

## Shubuhat vs Shahawat

START
Coding Questions
Ibn al-Qayyim: what are shubuhat and shahawat, and what cures each?
Back:
- **Shubuhat** (doubts): corrupt **knowledge**, so you cannot see what is right. Cure: **yaqin** (certainty).
- **Shahawat** (desires): corrupt the **will**, so you see what is right and don't want it. Cure: **sabr** (patience).

Key: the axis is *what breaks*, knowing or wanting.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787891-->
END

START
Coding Questions
Is Solomon's paradox a shubuhat problem or a shahawat problem, and why?
Back:
**Shahawat.** The wise judgment exists and works on other people; it fails only where **desire has a stake** in the outcome.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787892-->
END

START
Coding Questions
How does hawa (desire following the self) distort judgment of your own case, per Fatir 35:8?
Back:
Hawa makes the self's **own deed look good** to it: "one whose evil deed has been made attractive to him, so he sees it as good."

It changes how the act **looks**, not only pushes toward it.

What does al-Qiyamah 75:14-15 teach about self-knowledge?
Back:
"Man is a clear **witness against himself**, even if he offers his **excuses**."

The knowledge of yourself is there; **excuse-making** is what covers it.

Which verse commands justice even when it cuts against your own interest, and what is Ibn Taymiyyah's point about it?

Ibn Taymiyyah: the measure must come from **revelation, outside the self**, because one following hawa cannot fairly judge his own case.

## Al-Ghazali's Four Ways

START
Coding Questions
Why does al-Ghazali say you need outside help to find your own faults?
Back:
The **nafs hides its own faults from itself**, so seeing them requires a view from outside the self.

(*Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din*, book on disciplining the soul, Riyadat al-Nafs)
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787896-->
END

START
Coding Questions
Al-Ghazali gives how many ways to see your own faults, and which people do the first three bring in?
Back:
**Four** ways. The first three each bring in an **outside person**:
1. a **shaykh** with insight
2. a **truthful friend** asked to watch you
3. your **enemies**, through what they say
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787897-->
END

START
Coding Questions
Why does al-Ghazali count your enemies' words as a way to see your faults?
Back:
**Hostility exposes** the faults that friends hide out of kindness.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge
<!--ID: 1790710787898-->
END

START
Coding Questions
What is al-Ghazali's fourth way to see your faults, and how does it reverse self-distancing?
Back:
Mix with people, and **whatever you blame in them, suspect in yourself.**

Self-distancing moves your problem **outward** to judge it like a friend's; the fourth way takes your judgment of others and turns it **inward**.
Tags: self-mastery mindset self-knowledge

END

START
Coding Questions
What does al-Ghazali's approach add that self-distancing cannot?
Back:
A **real second person**. Self-distancing is still your own view from another angle; a shaykh, friend or enemy sees what the nafs cannot counterfeit.
