What are the two highest-rated engagement tools in synchronous online learning?
Back:
From PMC study (N=55 adult learners):

1. **Breakout group discussions** — M = 4.29/5.0
2. **Polls** — M = 4.22/5.0

Both rated significantly higher than passive video watching, chat-only, or camera-on requirements.

Both work because they force **active commitment** — breakout rooms require participation in small groups; polls require individual responses before seeing others' answers.

Why is mandating camera-on a weak engagement lever?
Back:
Byers et al. (2024): camera-on increases **attendance perception** (instructors feel students are present) but does NOT improve emotional or cognitive engagement.

Additionally: mandatory camera adds **self-view burden** — continuous self-monitoring recruits working memory that should go to content.

The peer cascade effect is the right lever: norm-setting in session 1 → a few students on camera → triggers cascade (β = .73) — no mandate needed.

How do breakout rooms function as ZPD scaffolds in online teaching?
Back:
Smaller groups **lower the participation threshold** — students attempt ideas they would not risk in front of the full class.

This mirrors ZPD mechanics:
- Full class = above ZPD for many students (too exposed)
- Breakout room of 4–6 = inside ZPD (enough safety to attempt, enough peers for varied input)

Key requirements:
- Post the discussion prompt **before** sending students in (prompt disappears when slides move)
- Optimal size ~5
- Always debrief: groups report back to main room

What is the reliable attention ceiling for online sessions without interaction?
Back:
**~15 minutes** — after which completion rates and attention drop steeply even for motivated learners.

The "6-minute myth" is an oversimplification — **interactivity extends this**.

Embedded polls, questions, and typed responses can push engagement well past 15 minutes.

But sessions that run continuously without any interaction point will lose the room around the 15-minute mark regardless of content quality.

What is Kounin's "withitness" and how does teacher proximity relate to it?
Back:
**Withitness:** the ability to communicate to students that you know what's happening everywhere in the room at all times.

Demonstrated through:
- Constant scanning of the full room
- Moving toward incipient disruptions before they escalate
- Responding to the right student with the right intervention

Proximity is the physical mechanism: moving closer to a disruptive student stops behavior **without verbal confrontation** and without breaking instructional flow.

Why does teacher proximity amplify both positive AND negative teacher behavior?
Back:
PMC (2021) data:

- Front-row + **high positive feedback:** 89.28% engagement
- Front-row + **low positive feedback:** 14.66% engagement (lower than back-row same condition: 22.55%)

Proximity does not produce engagement — it **amplifies whatever the interaction quality is**.

Moving to the front with cold or critical interaction accelerates disengagement faster than staying at the front of the room.

Rule: proximity is only a positive lever when paired with warm, supportive engagement quality.

Why does lowering your voice draw more attention than raising it?
Back:
Raising voice → students habituate → background noise level rises → you must raise it again → arms race.

Lowering voice → students must **actively attend** to hear → quiets the room → creates contrast that signals importance.

Pause is the sharpest version: sudden silence draws attention faster than any volume change.

The instinct under stress (raise volume, increase pace) produces the opposite of the intended effect.

What are the 4 primary sources of extraneous cognitive load specific to online delivery?
Back:
1. **Self-view** — constant self-monitoring recruits working memory away from content (d = 0.71 effect when removed)
2. **Grid view** — sustained gaze from multiple faces triggers social threat processing; biologically costly
3. **Virtual backgrounds** — distract peripheral attention; natural backgrounds produced 0.68 higher germane load
4. **Multi-app environment** — notifications, browser tabs, messaging apps — no social accountability to counter them

All four add load that teaches nothing.

What effect size does disabling self-view produce and what is the mechanism?
Back:
**Effect size: d = 0.71** (Zoom fatigue study, Scientific Reports 2025, N=39, within-subjects)

This was the **largest significant effect** of any intervention tested.

**Mechanism:** self-view requires continuous self-monitoring — watching yourself while simultaneously trying to process content. Both compete for working memory.

Disabling self-view removes the self-monitoring task entirely → full capacity redirected to content.

Teach students to do this in session 1. Or build it into class setup instructions.

What is the switching heuristic between online and offline delivery?
Back:
**Offline:** presence compensates.
Movement, eye contact, and voice do the cognitive management and accountability work automatically.

**Online:** structure compensates.
Without physical presence, accountability and attention management must be **designed into the session architecture** — via timed interactivity, explicit participation norms, and load reduction by design.

Key difference: offline you *feel* attention loss and respond in real time. Online you must *predict* it in advance and pre-schedule the interventions.
