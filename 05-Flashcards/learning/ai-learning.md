What separates the top 1% of AI users from everyone else, according to Drew Bent?
Back: They treat AI as a **collaborator**, not an assistant.
- Average users: transactional, "chatbot-only" — quick question in, quick answer out
- Top users: a **thinking partner** they argue with, hand hard open-ended problems to, and push back against
- The differentiator is the user's **stance**, not the model

What is the difference between using AI as an **assistant** vs. a **collaborator**?
Back:
- **Assistant** → closed questions ("What is X?"), extracting answers
- **Collaborator** → open hard problems ("here's my half-formed plan, poke holes in it"), building understanding together
The shift is from **extracting answers** to **building understanding**.

What is the single biggest differentiator between elite and average AI users?
Back: How much **rich, specific context** they provide.
- Average users send vague prompts → generic output
- Elite users front-load personal context → output only useful to them
- **Context is the part the human supplies and the model cannot**

How does the way you use AI determine whether your skill grows or decays?
Back:
- Using AI to **bypass the work** → skill **atrophy** ("use it or lose it")
- Using AI for **inquiry and probing** → skill **growth**
The brain weakens any capacity a tool does the work for.

What is the antidote to AI-driven skill atrophy?
Back: Use AI for **inquiry, not bypass** — keep the higher-order thinking yourself.
- A study where students delegated only **lower-order** work to AI showed **greater** critical-thinking gains over 12 weeks
- "Write this for me" → atrophy; "quiz me, challenge my answer" → growth

What is the **friction rule** for effective AI learning?
Back: Place friction deliberately:
- **Low-impact busywork** (reformatting, summarizing, first drafts) → remove friction, let AI do it
- **Skill-building thinking** → add friction, keep it for yourself
"Be lazy where impact is capped; be obsessed where impact compounds."

Coding Questions
What is the **gym-spotter model** for using AI?
Back: A spotter doesn't lift the weight for you — it lets you push harder and safer.
- Study the concept yourself first
- Ask AI to **quiz you**, then escalate difficulty
- Have it **challenge assumptions** and force you to defend answers
The discomfort is the point — learning strengthens at the edge of ability.

Why does Drew Bent call **AI agent building** the foundational career skill of the next 30 years?
Back: Like **spreadsheet proficiency** was for the previous 40 years — a baseline literacy across nearly every job.
- A spreadsheet let anyone model and decide without specialized software
- An AI agent lets anyone **delegate multi-step work to software that reads, decides, and acts** — without being a programmer
- You orchestrate the model, not build it

How do you learn AI's real capabilities, according to Drew Bent?
Back: By doing **reps** — many small, hands-on experiments, not by reading about it.
- The capability frontier moves every few weeks, so fixed mental models go stale
- **Intuition for the limits is itself the skill** — knowing when to trust output and when to step in

Coding Questions
What do **reps** with AI teach you besides where it works?
Back: They teach **trust *and* distrust** — both are the point.
- They surface failure modes: stochastic output, confident wrong answers, degradation on large/complex context
- Push a problem past where you expect it to fail to find the real edge
