Coding Questions
What is the difference between *input* and *intake* in Schmidt's Noticing Hypothesis?
Back:
- **Input** → language the learner hears or reads (environmental exposure)
- **Intake** → language actually processed for acquisition
- Only *noticed* input becomes intake — conscious attention to form is a prerequisite
- Frequent but unnoticed input is not acquired regardless of exposure volume

What three functions does Swain's Output Hypothesis assign to language production?
Back:
1. **Noticing/triggering** — production failures reveal gaps the learner doesn't control
2. **Hypothesis-testing** — output is an experiment; interlocutor feedback confirms or disconfirms a form
3. **Metalinguistic/reflective** — discussing *how* to say something consolidates grammatical knowledge
Tags: language-learning acquisition output swain
END

Coding Questions
Why did French immersion students plateau grammatically despite years of comprehensible input (Swain, 1985)?
Back:
- They received massive input but were rarely *pushed* to produce beyond their comfort zone
- Without **pushed output**, they fossilized at functional comprehension + inaccurate production
- Key insight: input maximizes exposure quantity; output forces precision
- Fix: tasks requiring grammatical accuracy learners cannot avoid
Tags: language-learning acquisition output immersion
END

Coding Questions
What is Long's mechanism for why interaction accelerates acquisition beyond passive input?
Back:
- **Negotiation of meaning** (clarification requests, recasts, confirmation checks) automatically modifies input to be comprehensible *and* draws attention to problematic forms
- **Recasts** deliver correct form implicitly while keeping the conversation moving
- Passive input requires the learner to already notice errors; interaction *creates* the noticing moment
- Meta-analysis d ≈ 0.6–0.8 vs. comprehensible input alone (Mackey & Goo, 2007)
Tags: language-learning acquisition interaction long
END

What vocabulary coverage threshold does Nation's research identify for acquisition from context?
Back:
- **98% coverage** — learner must know ~98% of words in a text
- Below 98%: unknown words interrupt flow too frequently for context-based inference to work
- 95% (1 unknown per 20 words) is the floor for basic comprehension — below acquisition threshold
- This is why graded readers are engineered to hit specific vocabulary bands
Tags: language-learning vocabulary nation coverage
END

When should a language learner use deliberate vocabulary study vs. incidental acquisition?
Back:
- **Deliberate study** → core frequency bands (first 2,000–3,000 word families) — too high-frequency to stand out as "unknown" in reading
- **Incidental acquisition** → long tail (5,000+ families) — too numerous to study explicitly; acquired through extensive reading
- The cycle: deliberate study raises coverage → extensive reading becomes possible → incidental acquisition kicks in
- <mark>Pitfall:</mark> learners who only read never close core-band gaps; learners who only study cards never embed words in natural context
Tags: language-learning vocabulary acquisition deliberate incidental
END

What is the keyword mnemonic and what two stages does it involve?
Back:
- **Keyword Method** (Atkinson & Raugh, 1975) — two-stage encoding for vocabulary
- **Stage 1 — acoustic link:** find a native-language word that sounds like part of the target word (the *keyword*)
- **Stage 2 — imagery link:** form a vivid mental image connecting the keyword to the target meaning
- Example: Spanish *caballo* (horse) → keyword "eye" → image: horse with giant eye
- Works via [[dual coding]]: creates both phonological and visual retrieval routes
Tags: language-learning vocabulary mnemonics keyword
END

Why does teaching vocabulary in semantic sets (e.g., all colors together, all furniture together) hurt retention?
Back:
- **Fan effect / interference:** words sharing semantic features activate overlapping memory networks
- During retrieval, the target word must compete against its category neighbors
- More similar the words → stronger competition → slower, less reliable retrieval
- Tinkham (1993, 1997): semantic-set learners showed significantly lower retention than unrelated-word-list learners
- Exception: **thematic clusters** (beach: sand, wave, towel) are neutral/positive — words play different roles in the same scene
Tags: language-learning vocabulary interference semantic-sets
END

What is sentence mining and what is the critical i+1 card requirement?
Back:
- **Sentence mining** — extract full sentences containing unknown words from authentic input → build Anki cards from those sentences (not isolated words)
- Preserves collocations, register, and contextual meaning that word-list cards lose
- **i+1 card requirement:** each mined sentence must have *exactly one* unknown element
- Two unknowns → ambiguous SRS feedback (failed for the wrong reason)
- Inverts traditional study: start from meaning-in-context, don't arrive at it later
Tags: language-learning vocabulary anki sentence-mining
END

What is spaced repetition and how does it differ from generic flashcard review?
Back:
- **Spaced repetition** — scheduling algorithm that times review just before memory decays below ~90% recall probability
- Re-encoding at near-forgetting produces a steeper, longer-lasting retention curve (Ebbinghaus, 1885)
- **Generic flashcards:** random order, fixed repetition — reviews words you know as often as words you're forgetting
- **SRS (Anki/SM-2):** allocates review time *inversely to recall strength* — maximum efficiency per minute
- Critical: SRS maintains vocabulary, it does not teach it — create cards *after* you understand the word in context
Tags: language-learning vocabulary anki spaced-repetition
END

What does shadowing train and at what proficiency level does it become effective?
Back:
- **Shadowing** — simultaneously vocalizing native-speaker audio while staying ~0.5–1 second behind
- Trains: prosody (rhythm/stress/intonation), listening parsing speed, articulatory automaticity
- *Not* a vocabulary tool — does not encode meaning
- Effective from **B1+** — below that, insufficient phonological knowledge makes native-speed tracking overwhelming
- Best with *familiar* audio (already comprehended); unfamiliar audio shifts attention away from prosody
Tags: language-learning pronunciation fluency shadowing
END

What is the optimal balance between immersion and structured study for adult L2 learners?
Back:
- No study shows pure immersion superiority for adults (Swain's immersion research: comprehension ✓, production accuracy ✗)
- Adult optimal: **3 components**
  1. Explicit instruction → fast pattern acquisition for irregular forms/grammar
  2. Massive comprehensible input → vocabulary, pragmatics
  3. Output with feedback → production accuracy
- Heuristic: *structured study for what to notice; immersion-style input for how much exposure*
- Krashen's counter: explicit learning stays "monitored" (conscious), doesn't convert to automatic — still debated
Tags: language-learning immersion structured-study acquisition
END

Why does speaking from day one benefit acquisition despite Krashen's silent period argument?
Back:
- Krashen: silent period prevents output errors from fossilizing; wait until ready
- Counter-evidence:
  1. Output Hypothesis (Swain): production surfaces gaps input doesn't
  2. Interactional Hypothesis (Long): conversation generates recasts and modified input
  3. Adults have L1 cognitive scaffolding child L1 learners lack
- **Key distinction:** output + feedback = acquisition; output *without feedback* = practicing errors
- Low-stakes speaking with corrective feedback (italki tutors) benefits acquisition at any level
Tags: language-learning speaking output silent-period
END

How does morphological root awareness differ from keyword mnemonics as a vocabulary strategy?
Back:
- **Keyword mnemonics** → temporary encoding bridge; helps get a word *into* memory; bridge fades once the word is learned
- **Morphological awareness** → permanent decoding tool; helps *infer* meaning of words never seen before
- Knowing *port* (carry) decodes: transport, import, export, portable, porter, deportation — 6 words from 1 morpheme
- Analogy: keyword = caching a lookup result; morphological knowledge = owning the hash function
- Caveat: false cognates and semantic drift (terrible ≠ terrific despite shared *terr-*) — verify in context
Tags: language-learning vocabulary morphology etymology mnemonics
END

What is the practical structure of an input ladder from A1 to C1+ in English?
Back:
- **A1–A2** → Graded readers (Starter/Elementary), learner podcasts | vocab: 500–2,000 word families
- **A2–B1** → Graded readers (Intermediate), children's books, simplified news | 2,000–4,000
- **B1–B2** → Young adult fiction, VOA Special English, L2-subtitle TV | 4,000–6,000
- **B2–C1** → Native fiction, podcasts with transcripts, domain-familiar academic articles | 6,000–8,000
- **C1+** → Authentic input across domains | 8,000+
- Monitoring: >2–3 unknown words/page → drop a level
Tags: language-learning input laddering sequencing
