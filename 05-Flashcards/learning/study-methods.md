Coding Questions
What is the **PQRST method** and who created it?
Back: **PQRST** is a 5-stage active reading method by Francis P. Robinson — an educational psychologist who studied how college students learn from textbooks.
- Prevents passive reading by making you seek answers rather than absorb words
- Stages: **Preview → Question → Read → State → Test**

Coding Questions
What is the **Preview** stage in PQRST and what is its purpose?
Back: **Previewing** the material before reading a single word — skim headings, subheadings, bullet points, diagrams, and summaries.
- Goal: build a **mental map** of the content, not comprehension
- You are not reading word by word — just getting the overall structure
Tags: learning study-methods
END

Coding Questions
What is the **Question** stage in PQRST and how do you do it?
Back: **Questioning** converts each heading into a question before reading that section.
- Example: "The Human Respiratory System" → "What is it and how does it function?"
- Sets up **active search mode** — you read to find answers, not just absorb text
- Questions stimulate curiosity and focus attention on key details

END

Coding Questions
What is the **Read** stage in PQRST?
Back: **Reading** the section thoroughly to find answers to the questions you created.
- Don't rush — understand before moving on
- Brain is in **search mode**: looking for specific answers, not scanning

Coding Questions
What is the **State** stage in PQRST and why does it matter?
Back: **Stating** — after finishing a section, explain what you just learned in writing or out loud.
- Restating in your own words **reveals gaps** in understanding
- If you can't explain it, you haven't learned it yet
- Reinforces understanding by forcing paraphrase, not repetition
Tags: learning study-methods
END

Coding Questions
What is the **Test** stage in PQRST?
Back: **Testing** comprehension at the end of the session by answering all the questions from the Question stage — without looking at the material.
- Struggle signals which areas need extra review
- Forces **active recall**, not passive re-reading

END

Why does the **Question** stage make PQRST more effective than plain reading?
Back: Converting headings into questions shifts reading from **passive absorption** to **active search**.
- The brain engages differently when seeking a specific answer vs. scanning text
- Questions stimulate curiosity → increased focus → better retention
- Without questions, reading can feel productive but leave no trace in memory

Coding Questions
What are the three passes in the **3-Pass Rule** for coding practice?
Back: **3-Pass Rule** structures coding practice into three stages:
- **Pass 1 — Attempt**: 30-minute timer, no solutions, no running tests — simulate interview pressure
- **Pass 2 — Study**: read the solution, understand *why* it works (invariant, data structure choice, complexity) — don't memorize steps
- **Pass 3 — Reproduce**: days later, solve from scratch with no help — if stuck, revisit Pass 2
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

START
Coding Questions
Why does Pass 1 of the 3-Pass Rule require no solution lookup?
Back: Pass 1 creates **cognitive struggle** — the brain encodes harder under resistance.
- Skipping Pass 1 turns practice into reading, not problem-solving
- The struggle itself is the learning mechanism, not the solution
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

START
Coding Questions
What role does Pass 3 play in the 3-Pass Rule, and why must it be days later?
Back: Pass 3 **reproduces** the solution from scratch with no help — days after Pass 2.
- Uses **spaced repetition**: returning after a delay forces retrieval from long-term memory, not short-term recall
- If stuck, the concept didn't consolidate — signals a gap, not a failure
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

Coding Questions
What is the core principle behind pattern-based DSA practice?
Back: **Recognizing** recurring algorithmic patterns instead of memorizing individual solutions.
- Solving one-by-one builds familiarity with those specific problems
- Learning patterns builds a mental toolkit that transfers to unseen problems
- Goal: identify the pattern from the problem statement, not after reading the solution
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

Coding Questions
What are the 7 core DSA patterns to internalize?
Back:
- **Sliding window** — optimize subarray/substring problems
- **Two pointers** — reduce O(n²) search to O(n)
- **Fast & slow pointers** — detect cycles
- **Binary search** — eliminate half the search space each step
- **BFS/DFS** — graph and tree traversal
- **Dynamic programming** — overlapping subproblems with optimal substructure
- **Backtracking** — explore decision trees with pruning
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

How should you structure DSA practice sessions when learning by pattern?
Back: Group problems by **pattern**, not by difficulty or topic label.
- Solve **3–5 problems per pattern** before moving to the next
- After each problem, ask: *which pattern did this use, and why?*

Why does switching between DSA resources slow progress?
Back: **Switching** resets momentum and creates shallow coverage of everything.
- Each switch feels like finding a better explanation — it's an illusion of progress
- A single resource builds a **connected mental model** — concepts refer back to each other
- Switching mid-course breaks those connections before they solidify
Tags: learning dsa practice
END

START
Coding Questions
When is switching DSA resources justified?
Back: Only when the current resource has a **fundamental flaw** — wrong explanations or missing critical topics.
- Not justified: another resource looks more appealing or clearer on one point
- Depth within one resource beats breadth across many

END
