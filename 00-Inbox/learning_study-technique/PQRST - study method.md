
This study method created by Francis P. Robinson, 
an educational psychologist 
	who spent his career teaching college students how to learn effectively from textbooks

---
#### Preview

Skip through the entire study material
get a gist of what it contains

> this isn't about reading the text word by word
> previewing heading, subheading, bullet points, diagrams, charts or summaries

---
#### Question

Translate heading and subheadings into question.

Example:
if your heading is “*The Human Respiratory System*” 
your question could be “***What is the human respiratory system*** and ***how does it function***?”

By that you're setting yourself up for active learning
by seeking answers
instead of passively reading the text

> Questions stimulate curiosity
> increases focus & attention on key details as you read

---
#### Reading

Now, it's time to find answers to the questions.
You read material thoroughly.

In this stage, your brain is deeply engaged.
Don't rush, take your time
make sure you understand what you read
only after that move on

---
#### State

State or summarize.
After reading a section, take a moment to **explain what you've just learned** either in writing or verbally.

Restating and paraphrasing the material will reinforce your understanding of the concepts.

---
#### Test

Evaluate your comprehension by recalling of the material.
Answer the questions you created at the beginning of the study session.

> If there are areas you struggle with, it's a signal to give extra attention in reviewing.
