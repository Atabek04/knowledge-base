
**What is Agile?**

Agile is an **iterative, flexible approach** where software is built in small cycles (1-4 weeks called sprints). Each sprint delivers working software, gathers feedback, and adjusts direction.

Requirements evolve continuously. Change is expected and welcomed.

---

**Why it emerged (2001):**

By 2000s, Waterfall was failing in fast-moving industries. Internet companies needed speed. Requirements changed weekly. Six-month development cycles meant shipping obsolete features.

**Catalyst:** The Agile Manifesto (2001) — 17 software developers published principles prioritizing:
- **Individuals and interactions** over processes
- **Working software** over documentation
- **Responding to change** over following plans
- **Customer collaboration** over contracts

**Context then:**
- Startups competing on speed, not budget certainty
- Customer expectations changed constantly (competitors launching fast)
- Internet era — release quickly, learn from users, iterate
- Teams co-located or synchronized (real-time communication possible)

Agile gave them: **speed, flexibility, customer feedback, and learning**.

---

**How it works:**

1. **Sprint Planning:** Define small chunk of work (1-4 weeks)
2. **Daily Standups:** 15-min sync on progress and blockers
3. **Development:** Build incrementally, test continuously
4. **Sprint Review:** Demo working software to customers
5. **Retrospective:** Team reflects on what worked/didn't
6. **Iterate:** Adjust based on feedback, start next sprint

Ship working software every sprint. No 2-year wait.

---

**Issues of Waterfall that Agile solves:**

| Waterfall Problem | Agile Solution |
|---|---|
| Requirements locked upfront, then wrong | Requirements evolve in sprints based on feedback |
| Testing comes too late | Testing happens during sprint (continuous) |
| Customers see working software only at end | Customers see working software every sprint |
| Long delivery cycles (2-3 years) | Working software delivered every 2-4 weeks |
| One mistake in requirements = entire project fails | Small mistakes caught quickly in next sprint |
| No learning or pivoting mid-project | Constant learning and adjustment mid-project |
| Large teams hard to coordinate | Small cross-functional teams (5-9 people) |

---

**Strengths of Agile:**

- Fast feedback loops — customers see progress weekly/biweekly
- Requirements evolve with business needs
- Risk managed incrementally (not all at end)
- Team morale higher — shipping regularly, celebrating wins
- Low ceremony overhead — focus on working software
- Easy to pivot when market changes

**Weaknesses of Agile:**

- Hard to predict final cost/timeline (changes all the time)
- Requires strong customer engagement (can't ignore for weeks)
- Documentation is sparse (harder for compliance/audits)
- Not ideal for hardware-dependent projects (can't iterate fast)
- Scalability issues with large teams (coordination chaos)
- No long-term planning possible

---

**When/where Agile is used today:**

- **Tech startups** — speed to market is critical
- **Web/mobile apps** — requirements constantly evolving
- **Fintech, SaaS platforms** — customer feedback drives features
- **E-commerce, social media** — fast-moving, competitive markets
- **In-house tools/platforms** — internal customers available for feedback
- **Small to medium teams** (5-50 people)

---

**Agile Frameworks:**

- **Scrum** — most popular, structured sprints with roles
- **Kanban** — continuous flow, no sprints
- **Lean** — minimize waste, optimize flow
- **Extreme Programming (XP)** — technical excellence (pair programming, TDD)

---

**Comparison: When to use each**

| Scenario | Use |
|---|---|
| Requirements stable, budget locked, compliance critical | Waterfall |
| Requirements evolving, speed matters, customer engaged | Agile |
| Hybrid: some parts stable, some volatile | Scrumfall (mix both) |

---

**Example Agile:** Spotify building music streaming app. Sprint 1: basic playback. Sprint 2: search. Sprint 3: playlists. Each sprint ships, users give feedback, team adjusts. If users hate playlists, pivot Sprint 4 to recommendations instead.

vs.

**Example Waterfall:** Banking system. Year 1: lock requirements (offline transactions, security specs). Years 2-3: build entire system. Year 4: test. If security requirements were wrong, restart.

---

**Current trend (2020s):** Most tech companies use **Agile at core, Waterfall for infrastructure/compliance**. Hybrid approaches are standard.
