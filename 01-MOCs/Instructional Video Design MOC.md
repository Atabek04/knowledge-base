Evidence-based principles for designing instructional video that maximizes learning, retention, and engagement — applicable across domains and language pairs.

*Rooted in cognitive load theory, multimedia learning research, and large-scale MOOC engagement data.*

---

## Pacing & Video Length

- [[Six-minute video threshold marks a sharp decline in learner engagement and retrieval opportunity]] — 6.9M edX sessions; engagement collapses past 6 min; chunk into 5–6 min segments with practice between them

---

## Retrieval & Active Pause

*Passive viewing produces weak retention. These principles govern how and when to interrupt video for active recall.*

- [[Interpolated retrieval testing after each video segment produces larger retention gains than restudying the same content]] — Szpunar 2013; d = 1.38 tested vs. untested; restudying has no advantage over passive watching
- [[Forward testing effect shows that retrieving prior content frees working memory to encode new incoming material]] — 2025 PMC study; segment retrieval improves encoding of the *next* segment; cross-lesson retrieval primes uptake

---

## Curiosity & Learner Motivation

*How the emotional and neurological state of the learner shapes encoding before content even begins.*

- [[Curiosity gap openings trigger dopaminergic encoding that boosts retention of all subsequent content]] — Loewenstein 1994 + Gruber 2014 fMRI; open questions activate dopamine reward circuits; Zeigarnik pending-task rehearsal
- [[Instructor presence in video increases motivation for declarative content but adds extraneous load during procedural demonstrations]] — 23-study meta-analysis; talking head helps for concepts, hurts for demos; warmth > production quality

---

## Audio & Sensory Design

- [[Background music in instructional video occupies the phonological loop and measurably reduces verbal learning]] — Mayer's Coherence Principle + d = 0.48 empirical; false-fluency trap; music with lyrics especially harmful for language learning

---

## Worked Examples & Scaffolding

*How to design the transition from guided instruction to independent practice.*

- [[Backward fading removes final worked example steps first to preserve context while building independent problem-solving skill]] — Renkl & Atkinson 2002/2004; remove from end first; completion problems as intermediate stage
- [[Expertise reversal effect makes scaffolded instruction counterproductive once learners build sufficient schema]] — Kalyuga et al. 2003; scaffolding reverses from helpful to harmful as schema grows; remove support proactively
- [[Pre-training principle reduces working memory split-attention by teaching component names before the main lesson begins]] — Mayer Pre-Training Principle; 60–90 sec definition segment before complex worked example

---

## Bilingual & Script Design

*Layout principles for video that teaches through two languages simultaneously.*

- [[Spatial contiguity between L2 target and translation eliminates split-attention cost in bilingual slide design]] — g = 0.63 meta-analysis; L2 word on top, L1 gloss directly below; visual weight hierarchy to redirect attention
- [[Sequential L1-then-bilingual subtitle exposure balances comprehension scaffolding with target-language form encoding]] — Yuan et al. 2025 BJEP; L1 first pass builds comprehension scaffold; bilingual second pass encodes L2 form with full working memory
- [[Same-language captions produce larger L2 acquisition gains than translated captions across proficiency levels]] — g = 0.56 meta-analysis; target-language captions force form recognition + speech segmentation; keyword glossing for beginners

---

## Visual Learning in Video Context

- [[Drawing a concept encodes it more reliably than writing it by engaging semantic motor and visual memory simultaneously]] — drawing *after text* = strong benefit; drawing *after visual/animated content* = no benefit; write summaries after video instead
- [[Self-generated diagrams produce better transfer than provided diagrams when used as a generation-then-correction sequence]] — apply to pause activities: ask learner to sketch before revealing the diagram
