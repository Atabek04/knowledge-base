Study methods and learning strategies for effective knowledge acquisition and retention.

## Thinking Methods

- [[First Principles Thinking rebuilds understanding from fundamental truths instead of reasoning by analogy]] — break to bedrock vs. copy by analogy; Aristotle, Feynman, Musk
- [[Playing with a problem's variables before solving reveals the solution through discovered properties]] — discover binary search by manipulating a sorted array; same property in disguise
- [[Building first-principles foundations in fundamentals makes learning frameworks faster]] — master math/algorithms/system design, frameworks follow; ingredients vs. recipes

## Study Methods

- [[PQRST method structures active reading into five sequential stages]] — Preview, Question, Read, State, Test

## DSA Practice

- [[DSA mastery comes from recognizing patterns not memorizing individual solutions]] — recognize sliding window/two pointers/DP patterns, not individual problems
- [[3-Pass Rule builds deep understanding of coding problems through attempt, study, and recall phases]] — attempt → study solution → reproduce from scratch days later
- [[Finishing one DSA resource beats switching between multiple courses]] — switching resets momentum, depth beats breadth of sources

## Skill Building & Confidence

- [[Over-relying on AI coding tools without reading output erodes genuine programming confidence]] — vibe-coding trap, borrowing vs. building confidence
- [[Rebuilding confidence requires starting with small achievable goals before scaling up]] — small wins generate fuel for harder milestones
- [[Daily consistent practice in small doses builds momentum faster than irregular deep sessions]] — start the timer no matter what

## Teaching Methods

## Instructional Video Design

*See [[Instructional Video Design MOC]] for the full treatment. Key principles:*

### Pacing & Retrieval
- [[Six-minute video threshold marks a sharp decline in learner engagement and retrieval opportunity]] — 6.9M edX sessions; engagement collapses past 6 min; chunk into 5–6 min segments
- [[Interpolated retrieval testing after each video segment produces larger retention gains than restudying the same content]] — Szpunar 2013; d = 1.38; restudying has no advantage over passive watching
- [[Forward testing effect shows that retrieving prior content frees working memory to encode new incoming material]] — 2025 study; past-content retrieval primes encoding of the next segment

### Motivation & Emotional State
- [[Curiosity gap openings trigger dopaminergic encoding that boosts retention of all subsequent content]] — Loewenstein + Gruber 2014 fMRI; open questions activate dopamine circuits; Zeigarnik effect
- [[Instructor presence in video increases motivation for declarative content but adds extraneous load during procedural demonstrations]] — 23-study meta-analysis; warmth > production quality; face-on vs. face-off by knowledge type
- [[Background music in instructional video occupies the phonological loop and measurably reduces verbal learning]] — Mayer Coherence + d = 0.48; false-fluency trap; language learning especially vulnerable

### Scaffolding & Worked Examples
- [[Backward fading removes final worked example steps first to preserve context while building independent problem-solving skill]] — Renkl & Atkinson 2002/2004; remove last steps first; completion problems as bridge
- [[Expertise reversal effect makes scaffolded instruction counterproductive once learners build sufficient schema]] — Kalyuga et al. 2003; scaffolding reverses from helpful to harmful as expertise grows
- [[Pre-training principle reduces working memory split-attention by teaching component names before the main lesson begins]] — Mayer Pre-Training; 60–90 sec name-and-define segment before complex worked example

## Visual Learning & Diagram Design

*Why visuals outperform text for long-term retention, how to exploit that advantage, and which diagram type to use for which content.*

### Why Visuals Work

- [[Dual coding theory shows that pairing images with verbal explanation creates stronger memory traces]] — Paivio 1971; verbal + nonverbal systems; picture superiority effect; 2 retrieval routes vs. 1 *(see also Slide Design)*
- [[Visual representations have a larger retention advantage over text at delayed recall than at immediate recall]] — PMC 2020 study; non-significant at immediate; partial η² = 0.26 at 2-hour delay; text trace decays faster
- [[Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience contains no retention percentages — the statistics were fabricated and have no empirical basis]] — Treichler 1967 fabricated the numbers; Dale's actual cone had no data; use Mayer's real effect sizes instead

### Generating Visuals for Learning

- [[Drawing a concept encodes it more reliably than writing it by engaging semantic motor and visual memory simultaneously]] — Wammes 2016; 7 experiments; 2× recall advantage; crude drawings work; drawing after text > writing; drawing after visuals = no benefit
- [[Self-generated diagrams produce better transfer than provided diagrams when used as a generation-then-correction sequence]] — 2025 meta-analysis; generate first → compare → correct; error mismatch is the encoding event
- [[Generative note-taking that forces paraphrase and reorganization outperforms verbatim transcription]] — Peper & Mayer 1978/1986; Cornell mechanism = embedded retrieval cues; sketchnoting components work but specific claims unverified

### Choosing the Right Diagram

- [[Each diagram type offloads a specific cognitive operation — choosing the wrong type adds extraneous load instead of removing it]] — taxonomy: concept map / mind map / flowchart / matrix / CLD / worked example / timeline / hierarchy; spatial contiguity g = 0.63
- [[Concept maps require labeled linking phrases to encode relationships rather than mere co-occurrence]] — Novak 1972; Nesbit & Adesope meta-analysis ES = 0.63; concept map vs. mind map distinction; when concept maps fail

## Active Self-Learning

*Techniques for learning deeply and retaining what you study — for you as a learner, not just as a teacher.*

### Memory & Retention
- [[Retrieval practice strengthens memory by recalling information rather than re-reading it]] — testing effect; Roediger & Karpicke 2006; ~50% better retention at 1-week vs. passive review
- [[Interleaving mixes problem types during practice to build pattern recognition over in-session fluency]] — contextual interference; 61% vs. 37% on delayed tests; application to LeetCode and system design
- [[Desirable difficulties make learning harder in ways that improve long-term retention over short-term fluency]] — *(see Teaching Methods above — applies to both teacher and learner)*

### Understanding Deeply
- [[Elaborative interrogation deepens schema by asking why facts are true during study]] — "why does this work?" forces schema connection; apply during note-writing
- [[Feynman Technique exposes the gap between familiarity and understanding through plain-language explanation]] — 4 steps; the name ≠ the thing; applied to system design, algorithms, backend concepts

### Self-Regulation
- [[Metacognition regulates learning by monitoring whether study methods are actually working]] — Flavell's two components; Dunning-Kruger mechanism; confidence calibration; exam wrappers
- [[Deliberate practice builds expert mental representations through focused effort at the edge of current ability]] — Ericsson's hierarchy; mental representations; 10,000-hour correction; deliberate vs. naive practice

## Language Learning

*How languages are acquired, how vocabulary is built, and what research says about the most effective techniques for learners and teachers.*

### Input & Acquisition Foundations

- [[Comprehensible input at i+1 requires graded exposure sequenced by difficulty to drive acquisition]] — Krashen's i+1 operationalized via Nation's 98% threshold; extensive vs. intensive reading; what i+1 is NOT
- [[High affective filter blocks language acquisition even when input is comprehensible]] — Krashen's affective filter; anxiety as acquisition blocker; pre-teaching vocabulary as prevention *(see Pedagogy section)*
- [[Noticing Hypothesis states that conscious attention to form is a prerequisite for converting input to intake]] — Schmidt 1990; input vs. intake distinction; why passive exposure fails; priming through explicit instruction
- [[Immersion accelerates acquisition only when input remains comprehensible and engagement stays active]] — French immersion evidence; Swain's plateau; structured study + input balance; Krashen's counterpoint
- [[Input laddering sequences listening and reading materials by difficulty to maintain comprehensible i+1 throughout acquisition]] — practical difficulty sequence A1→C1; monitoring i+1; common mistakes (jumping to native content too early)

### Output & Interaction

- [[Output Hypothesis states that producing language forces learners to notice gaps that input alone cannot reveal]] — Swain 1985; three output functions; French immersion evidence; pushed output; silent period challenge
- [[Interactional Hypothesis explains why negotiated conversation accelerates acquisition beyond passive exposure]] — Long 1981; negotiation moves (recasts, clarification requests); interaction vs. passive input; d≈0.6–0.8
- [[Speaking from day one builds acquisition through output pressure and immediate feedback from interaction]] — Wyner/Lewis vs. Krashen; adult L2 vs. child L1 silent period; output + feedback = acquisition; output without feedback = error practice

### Vocabulary Architecture

- [[Nation's vocabulary load research identifies word-frequency thresholds for unaided text comprehension]] — 98% coverage thresholds by text type; Academic Word List; 10–20 encounters for acquisition; vocabulary breadth before depth
- [[Deliberate vocabulary study and incidental acquisition target different frequency bands in the lexicon]] — Nation's tiers; core 2,000–3,000 = deliberate; long tail = incidental; the productive cycle; dangerous traps
- [[Teaching vocabulary in semantic sets creates interference that slows initial acquisition]] — Tinkham 1993; fan effect; semantic vs. thematic clustering; practical grouping rules
- [[Etymology and morphological root awareness multiply vocabulary acquisition by making word families transparent]] — morpheme leverage; Greek/Latin roots; how it differs from keyword mnemonics; false cognate caveat

### Video & Subtitle Design for L2

- [[Same-language captions produce larger L2 acquisition gains than translated captions across proficiency levels]] — g = 0.56 meta-analysis; target-language subtitles force orthographic-phonological mapping; keyword glossing for beginners
- [[Sequential L1-then-bilingual subtitle exposure balances comprehension scaffolding with target-language form encoding]] — Yuan et al. 2025 BJEP; L1 first pass for meaning, bilingual second pass for L2 form encoding
- [[Spatial contiguity between L2 target and translation eliminates split-attention cost in bilingual slide design]] — g = 0.63 meta-analysis; L2 on top, L1 gloss directly below; visual weight directs attention

### Vocabulary Techniques

- [[Keyword mnemonic links a foreign word to a vivid mental image via a sound-alike bridge]] — Atkinson & Raugh 1975; acoustic + imagery link; dual coding mechanism; limitations; best use cases
- [[Method of Loci encodes vocabulary by placing words as objects along a memorized spatial route]] — spatial encoding; hippocampal navigation; ROI at scale; sequential retrieval limitation
- [[Spaced repetition schedules vocabulary review at expanding intervals matched to the forgetting curve]] — Ebbinghaus; SM-2 algorithm; SRS vs. generic flashcards; maintains not teaches; create cards after understanding
- [[Sentence mining builds Anki cards from real input to preserve the contextual meaning that isolated words lose]] — Refold/MIA workflow; i+1 card requirement; inverted acquisition order; tools (Language Reactor, Yomitan)
- [[Shadowing trains prosody and fluency by simultaneously vocalizing native-speaker audio]] — Arguelles/AJATT; prosody + listening speed; articulatory loop; B1+ threshold; complement to CI
