The functional disciplines that make up the core of an MBA — sequenced as a self-study roadmap for an entrepreneur.

This is the "MBA core" both Harvard and Stanford build their first year on: the same spine of **Economics → Accounting → Finance → Marketing → Operations → Strategy → Leadership**, plus Entrepreneurship as the synthesis.

You can self-study the **knowledge** of every analytical course here from books and problem sets — that part transfers cleanly. What a degree adds on top is the **case-method discussion, the network, and the signaling** — none of which a book replaces. For someone building a company, the knowledge is the part that matters.

Walk the disciplines in order: each one assumes the ones above it.

### 0. Economics — the foundation

The logic of scarcity, prices, costs, and competition that every other discipline rests on.

→ Full roadmap in [[Microeconomics MOC]]. Start there.

- Microeconomics — supply/demand, elasticity, costs, market structure, game theory
- Macroeconomics — GDP, inflation, interest rates, business cycles (lighter touch for founders)
- *Canonical: Mankiw, "Principles of Economics" · MIT 14.01 OCW*

### 1. Financial Accounting — the language of business

How economic events become numbers. You can't read a company (or your own) without this.

- The three statements — income statement, balance sheet, statement of cash flows
- The accounting equation — assets = liabilities + equity
- Accrual vs cash accounting
- Recording transactions — debits, credits, the financial-statement-effects template
- Revenue recognition and matching
- Reading and analyzing statements — ratios, common-size analysis
- Forecasting financial statements
- *Canonical: Easton, "Financial Accounting for MBAs" · Ittelson, "Financial Statements"*

### 2. Managerial / Cost Accounting

Accounting turned inward — for decisions, not reporting.

- Cost behavior — fixed, variable, mixed
- Cost-volume-profit and break-even analysis
- Contribution margin
- Budgeting and variance analysis
- Activity-based costing

### 3. Corporate Finance — value and capital

How to value things and decide where money goes. The analytical heart of the MBA.

- Time value of money — present and future value
- Discounted cash flow (DCF)
- Net present value (NPV) and the NPV rule
- Internal rate of return (IRR), payback, profitability index
- Cost of capital and WACC
- Capital structure — debt vs equity
- CAPM, risk and return, portfolio theory
- Valuation methods — DCF, multiples, APV
- Dividend policy and market efficiency
- *Canonical: Berk & DeMarzo, "Corporate Finance" · Brealey, Myers & Allen*

### 4. Marketing — creating and capturing demand

Understanding customers and turning that into revenue.

- Customer and competitor analysis
- Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning (STP)
- The marketing mix — the 4 Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion)
- Branding and brand equity
- Pricing strategy (ties directly to elasticity → [[Microeconomics MOC]])
- Channels and go-to-market
- Customer lifetime value and acquisition cost
- *Canonical: Kotler & Keller, "Marketing Management"*

### 5. Operations & Supply Chain — making and delivering

Turning inputs into product reliably and at scale.

- Process analysis — flow, bottlenecks, throughput
- Capacity and location planning
- Inventory management and the bullwhip effect
- Forecasting and demand planning
- Quality management (Lean, Six Sigma)
- Supply chain design, sourcing, logistics
- *Canonical: Meredith & Shafer, "Operations and Supply Chain Management for MBAs"*

### 6. Strategy — where to compete and how to win

The integrative discipline — it pulls the others together.

- Industry analysis — Porter's Five Forces
- The value chain
- Competitive advantage — cost leadership vs differentiation
- Generic strategies and positioning
- Sustainable advantage and moats
- Corporate strategy — diversification, vertical integration
- *Canonical: Porter, "Competitive Strategy" · Magretta, "Understanding Michael Porter"*

### 7. Organizational Behavior & Leadership — managing people

Businesses are run by people; this is how you lead them.

- Individual differences and motivation
- Team dynamics and group decision-making
- Communication and conflict
- Negotiation
- Leadership theory and influence
- Organizational change and culture
- *Canonical: HBS "Organizational Behavior" cases · open OB texts*

### 8. Entrepreneurship — the synthesis 🟢

Where every discipline above is applied to building something new.

- Opportunity assessment and ideation
- Customer discovery and validation (lean startup)
- Business model design (Business Model Canvas)
- Minimum viable product and lean testing
- Entrepreneurial finance — seed funding, venture capital, valuation of startups
- Building the founding team
- Go-to-market and scaling execution
- *Canonical: Osterwalder, "Business Model Generation" · Ries, "The Lean Startup" · HBS Entrepreneurial Finance*
