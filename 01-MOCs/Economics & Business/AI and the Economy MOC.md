## 1. Scarcity and value

- [[Scarcity forces economies to allocate limited resources among unlimited uses|Scarcity: limited resources, unlimited uses]]
- [[Price follows the usefulness of the next unit, not total usefulness, which solves the water-diamond paradox|Water-diamond paradox: price follows the next unit's usefulness]]

## 2. Labor markets

- [[Supply and demand push a price to the level where the amount offered equals the amount wanted|Supply and demand: price settles where offered equals wanted]]
- [[A wage is the price of labor, set by the supply of workers and employers' demand for their work|Wage: price of labor; technology substitutes or complements]]

## 3. Capital and labor

- [[Income goes either to workers as wages or to owners as a return on capital, and the labor share measures the split|Capital vs labor: automation turns wages into owners' returns]]
- [[Wealth is fundamentally a claim on other people's labor|Wealth is a claim on others' labor]]
- [[Post-AGI power concentrates with the owners of compute, energy, and land|Power concentrates in compute, energy, land owners]]

## 4. Productivity and growth

- [[GDP counts only final goods and services, so each product's value enters once|GDP: value of final goods only, no double counting]]
- [[Total factor productivity is the output growth left over after counting more workers and more machines|TFP: output growth left after counting more inputs]]

## 5. Automation in history

- [[Automation takes over tasks rather than whole jobs, and new tasks can bring labor back|Task model: machines take tasks; new tasks bring labor back]]

- [[Farm jobs fell from 41 to 2 percent of US work while total employment kept growing|Agriculture: 41% to 2% of jobs, spending moved elsewhere]]

- [[A technology that destroys jobs in one industry creates new jobs in the industries that grow around it|New work: jobs grow in industries around a technology]]
