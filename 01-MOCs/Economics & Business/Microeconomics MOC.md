Microeconomics studies how individual actors — consumers, firms, single markets — make decisions under scarcity, and how their interactions set prices and quantities.

This MOC is a **learning roadmap**: topics are ordered in prerequisite sequence. Walk it top to bottom. Each bullet becomes an atomic note as it's studied.

For an entrepreneur, the high-leverage sections are marked 🟢 — pricing, costs, unit economics, and competitive structure are the day-to-day decision engine of running a business.

### Teaching Progress

> Not started — next: Foundations.

---

### 1. Foundations

- Scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost
- Tradeoffs and the production possibilities frontier (PPF)
- Marginal thinking — deciding by the next unit, not the total 🟢
- Positive vs normative economics
- How markets coordinate decisions (the price signal)

### 2. Supply and Demand 🟢

- The demand curve — willingness to pay
- The supply curve — willingness to sell
- Market equilibrium — where supply meets demand
- Shifts vs movements along a curve
- Price elasticity of demand — how quantity responds to price 🟢
- Elasticity of supply, income and cross-price elasticity
- Consumer surplus, producer surplus, and total welfare

### 3. Consumer Theory

- Utility and rational choice
- Budget constraints
- Indifference curves and marginal rate of substitution
- Utility maximization — the optimal consumption bundle
- Deriving demand from preferences
- Income and substitution effects

### 4. Producer Theory and Costs 🟢

- Production functions and the factors of production
- Short run vs long run
- Diminishing marginal returns
- Fixed, variable, marginal, and average costs 🟢
- Break-even and the shutdown decision 🟢
- Economies and diseconomies of scale
- Profit maximization — marginal cost meets marginal revenue 🟢

### 5. Market Structures 🟢

- Perfect competition — the price taker
- Monopoly — the price maker and pricing power 🟢
- Price discrimination 🟢
- Monopolistic competition — differentiation 🟢
- Oligopoly and strategic interdependence
- Comparing efficiency and deadweight loss across structures

### 6. Factor Markets

- Labor markets — wages and the demand for labor
- Marginal revenue product
- Capital and land markets
- Income distribution

### 7. Market Failure and Welfare

- Externalities — positive and negative
- Public goods and the free-rider problem
- Common resources and the tragedy of the commons
- Information asymmetry — adverse selection and moral hazard
- Government intervention — taxes, subsidies, price controls

### 8. Game Theory and Strategic Behavior 🟢

- Dominant strategies and the prisoner's dilemma
- Nash equilibrium
- Sequential games and credible commitment
- Application to pricing and competition 🟢
