## Phase 1 — Foundations: board, pieces, rules

*Non-negotiable before anything else. The language and legal moves of the game.*

### Board & notation
- Piece names and relative values (P1 N3 B3 R5 Q9, K = priceless)
- Board setup; white square on the right
- Files, ranks, diagonals; coordinates and square colour
- Algebraic notation; descriptive notation; FEN

### How pieces move & capture
- Pawn: forward move, diagonal capture, two-square first move
- Rook, bishop, queen — line movement
- Knight — the jump, captures, central vs cornered
- King — movement and capturing
- En passant (special pawn capture)
- Pawn promotion and underpromotion

### Check, mate, and game-end states
- Check; the king's options when in check
- Double check (king must move); discovered check
- Checkmate
- Castling — kingside/queenside, philosophy (safety before attack)
- Castling restrictions: not through/into check, not after king/rook moved, not obstructed
- Ways to win: checkmate · resignation · win on time
- Ways to draw: stalemate · perpetual check · insufficient material · 3-fold repetition · 50-move rule · mutual agreement
- Tournament rules: touch-move, one hand, clock etiquette, FIDE handbook

## Phase 2 — Opening principles

*Not memorizing lines — understanding why the first moves are good. Same four ideas appear in every course; learn them once, deeply.*

- Guiding philosophy: put yourself beyond defeat before attacking
- Control the central squares (e4/d4/e5/d5)
- Develop your pieces (minor pieces first)
- Castle early — king safety
- Don't bring the queen out too early
- Don't move the same piece twice without reason
- Don't be greedy at the cost of development
- Don't leave pieces unprotected
- Connect the rooks
- Worked failures: Morphy's Opera Game, Anderssen–Kieseritzky, Steinitz–von Bardeleben (development & king-safety punished)

## Phase 3 — Tactics: the engine of improvement

*The single biggest rating driver to 1600. Built bottom-up: capture logic → calculation method → named patterns. Merges the Beginner course's tactics with the full Kingscrusher + GM calculation method.*

### Capture logic (first tactics)
- Making wise captures — is the target defended?
- Counting attackers vs defenders on a square
- Removing a defender to win the capture
- The exchange: when to trade pieces vs pawns; trade when cramped, trade off the attacker under a king-attack

### Calculation method
- Forcing moves first — check all Checks, Captures, and Threats (CCT)
- What calculation is; variation vs main line; pattern vs process
- The 3 golden rules: weakness of the last move · killer common squares · the "in effect" clause
- Loose pieces drop off (LPDO); hunting opponent downsides
- Quiet moves can beat forcing moves; calculate further before committing

### Core named tactics (Tier 1)
- Fork / double attack
- Pin — absolute vs relative; rebelling against a relative pin
- Skewer (the reverse pin)
- Discovered attack; discovered check; double check
- Removing the guard / defender; undermining
- Deflection; decoy; overloading
- Hanging / unprotected piece exploitation

### Further tactics (Tier 2–3)
- Interference (disconnection); clearance (line/square vacation)
- X-ray; battery; desperado
- Zwischenzug (in-between move); zugzwang (compulsion to move)
- Sacrifices: calculated, Greek Gift (Bxh7+), queen sacrifice, f7/f2 soft-spot
- Simplification; trapped piece; windmill (see-saw); rook lift; king hunt
- Prophylaxis (threat prevention)

### Tactical training method
- Solve puzzles without tags; see the whole solution including defenses before moving
- Train patterns daily; don't race for speed, abstract the principle

## Phase 4 — Checkmate patterns

*A vocabulary of finishing positions. Recognize them instantly so calculation finds the mate. Catalogued in both the Beginner and Tactics courses.*

- Elements of mate: support mate, removing escape squares, the killer check
- Constructing your own mating net
- **Essential:** Back-rank, Smothered, Scholar's, Fool's, Légal's
- **Common named:** Anastasia's, Arabian, Boden's, Morphy's, Opera, Greco's, Réti's, Epaulette, Hook, Dovetail/Cozio's, Swallow's tail
- **Endgame mates:** Lawnmower/ladder, Box (K+R), Queen mate, two-bishop mate

## Phase 5 — Middlegame strategy & pawn structure

*What to do when there's no immediate tactic. "Pawns are the soul of chess" (Philidor).*

### Position evaluation
- The elements: material, time (tempo), space, king safety, pawn structure
- Evaluation: spot the downsides — unprotected pieces, exposed king, back-rank weakness
- Chess is a single-turn game: overwhelm with multiple threats
- Material balance isn't everything

### Pawn structure
- What a pawn structure is; pawns can't go backwards (irreversibility)
- What makes a pawn weak (dependencies, no pawn defender)
- Pawn breaks / levers; structural holes; the minority attack
- Passed pawns; doubled pawns (up/downside); isolated queen's pawn (IQP)
- Backward pawns; hanging pawns; thorn/wedge pawns
- Piece–pawn harmony

## Phase 6 — Endgames

*Where games are decided and the most teachable phase. The GM "Understanding Endgames" trilogy goes deepest here — follow its order.*

### Roles & basic mates
- King activity in the endgame; the rook on the 7th rank
- Basic checkmates: K+Q vs K · K+R vs K · two rooks · two bishops
- Back-rank checkmate technique

### King & pawn endgames (the foundation)
- The opposition; key squares
- King & pawn vs king (white-to-move vs black-to-move)
- Shouldering (vertical/horizontal); the king doing two jobs
- The rule of the square (can the king catch the pawn?)
- The Réti maneuver / Réti idea; triangulation
- Two-pawn endgames: doubled, adjacent files, same file, mined squares, pawn races
- The passed-pawn breakthrough every beginner must know

### Queen & rook endgames
- Queen vs pawn (king near vs far); queen vs queen mating ideas
- Rook & pawn vs rook: the Lucena position (building a bridge); the Philidor position; the Vancura defence

## Phase 7 — Openings in depth (after tactics are solid)

*Deliberately late. Beginners gain almost nothing from opening theory before tactics. Then build a small repertoire — don't over-study.*

- Imbalances; main lines vs variations; using opening databases without bias
- Why know traps; beginner traps: Fool's mate, Scholar's mate, Légal/Blackburne, Fried Liver, Blackburne Shilling, Stafford
- Systems/setups: London, Colle, King's Indian Attack, English/Botvinnik, Torre, Nimzo-Larsen
- Major openings: Ruy Lopez, Italian, Sicilian, French, Caro-Kann, Scandinavian, Pirc, Slav, Nimzo/King's Indian, Grünfeld
- Gambits: Queen's, King's, Evans, Danish, Smith-Morra, Benko, Albin
- Build a repertoire: openings for white, openings for black

## Phase 8 — Improvement method (the meta-layer)

*How to actually get better — synthesized from coaches, expert blogs, r/chess consensus, and the canonical books. Applies from day one, revisited as you climb. These bullets ARE written up as atomic notes.*

### Core habits (highest leverage)
- [[Analyzing your own games is the highest-ROI chess training activity]] — playing trains instinct, analysis trains understanding
- [[Annotate a chess game yourself before turning on the engine]] — the gap between your eval and Stockfish's is the lesson
- [[Below roughly 1800 ELO chess games are decided by tactics not strategy or openings]] — so tactics is the dominant lever
- [[Long time controls build calculation skill that blitz cannot]] — play rapid/classical, not bullet
- [[Studying endgames early gives a foundation for judging earlier moves]] — Capablanca's argument

### Thinking process & blunders
- [[A checks-captures-threats scan before every chess move catches most blunders]] — scan the opponent's CCT, not just your own
- [[Most chess blunders are impulse errors not knowledge gaps]] — the fix is the clock, not more theory

### Openings discipline
- [[Deep opening memorization is wasted below 1800 ELO so learn principles not lines]]
- [[A small chess opening repertoire kept for a year beats collecting many openings]]

### Tactics training mechanism
- [[Re-solving the same chess puzzle set until automatic is the core mechanism of tactics training]] — Woodpecker / Seven Circles / SR, demystified

### Plateaus & study planning
- [[Breaking a chess plateau requires getting temporarily worse]] — commit 1–3 months through the dip
- [[Training your measured weakness beats following a generic chess study ratio]] — diagnose, don't follow fixed percentages

## Phase 8b — Tools & platforms

*The modern improvement stack: free analysis, guided "why", spaced-repetition memory, and engine discipline.*

- [[Lichess provides unlimited free engine analysis and tactics training]] — the free backbone
