Booking, format, test day and what happens afterwards, for IELTS Academic computer-delivered in Astana, February 2027.

All figures captured 4-5 September 2026 from the providers' own Kazakhstan pages. Tenge-to-dollar conversions use the National Bank rate of 456.56₸/$ on 5 September 2026 and are indicative only. Anything I could not verify is listed at the bottom rather than guessed at.

---

## Booking

### British Council or IDP

Both operate in Astana, the test is identical, and <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the fee is identical — 110,250₸</mark>. So the choice is made on four things that are not the price.

**Preparation.** British Council's booking confirmation includes access to IELTS Ready Premium — around 40 practice tests with model answers, an adaptive study plan, AI feedback across all four skills. IDP's materials are good but publicly free and not booking-gated, so booking with IDP unlocks nothing. Since IELTS Progress Check closed there is no paid equivalent to Premium at any price ([[Booking the test unlocks the best free IELTS resource so booking comes before diagnosing|why this decides it]]).

**Speaking scheduling.** IDP gives same-day certainty on computer-delivered. British Council Kazakhstan schedules Speaking same day, up to 2 days before or 1 day after — but lets you move the slot yourself in the Test Taker Portal. *(Note: the British Council's own test-day page repeats the global "up to a week either side"; the Kazakhstan FAQ is the local rule.)*

**Price transparency.** British Council publishes its remark and One Skill Retake fees. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">IDP Kazakhstan publishes neither</mark> — you would be committing without knowing what a remark costs.

**Rescheduling.** British Council is better documented and marginally more generous, and allows several transfers on one booking.

**Recommendation: British Council, computer-delivered, booked as early as the February window opens.** Premium is worth more than IDP's same-day speaking certainty.

### Format — the choice is already made

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">British Council Kazakhstan no longer offers paper IELTS at all.</mark> Their FAQ states it flatly. IDP still runs both nationally, but its only Astana centre is computer-delivered.

That suits the plan anyway: results in days, an on-screen word count for Writing, and — decisively — <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">One Skill Retake requires a computer-delivered original</mark>. The one cost is that computer-delivered Listening has no transfer window ([[Computer-delivered Listening removes the transfer time so typing accuracy becomes part of the score|detail]]).

**Payment:** tenge only. Card, the Kaspi app, Halyk e-invoice, or bank transfer (allow five working days). No cash, though you can pay by card at the Astana office. Payments from UK-sanctioned banks are frozen and the service is not delivered.

### Venues

- **IDP / InterPress, Astana** — 12A Kazhymukan St, 4th floor, 010000. Open 09:00-19:15. The only IDP centre in Astana.
- **British Council, Astana office** — 6 Saryarka Ave, Business Centre "Arman", 8th floor, room 840. Mon-Fri 09:00-17:30. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This is the office, not necessarily the exam room</mark> — the British Council does not publish its testing-point addresses, and the venue arrives in the booking confirmation email.

### Rescheduling and cancellation

| | British Council | IDP |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer, 15+ days out | Free | Free |
| Transfer, 5-14 days out | 33,000₸ admin fee | up to 25% of the fee |
| Transfer, under 5 days | Not allowed — treated as a cancellation | under 4 days: same |
| Cancel, 15+ days out | Refund minus 33,000₸ | 75% refund |
| Cancel, 14 days or less | **No refund** | **No refund** |
| No-show | Full fee lost | Full fee lost |

Refunds take 4-6 weeks. Both providers allow special consideration for illness or bereavement, claimable before the test or up to two calendar days after, never once results are out. A transferred date must fall within three months of the original.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Book far enough ahead that the free-transfer window is still open</mark> — six to eight weeks out keeps the 15-day threshold comfortable.

### Identity documents — strict, and Kazakhstan-specific

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">A Kazakhstan national ID card is accepted; Kazakh citizens do not need a passport.</mark> Non-citizens must use an international passport.

The binding rule is not which document but which one you named: you may only sit the test with <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">the exact document stated at registration</mark>. Work permits, driving licences, student cards and birth certificates are rejected. So is an expired document — no test, no refund. A scan is uploaded at booking; if you replace the document, email a copy no later than 72 hours before test day.

## Test day

**Order is fixed and there are no breaks between sections:** Listening (~30 min) → Reading (60) → Writing (60), one sitting. Official pages quote the total variously as 2h40 and 2h45; the computer-delivered arithmetic is about 2h32. Budget 2h45 seated.

**Arrive 60 minutes before the written test, 20 minutes before a Speaking slot.** Arrive late and you cannot sit, with no refund and no transfer. There is no fixed public start time — yours is emailed at least three days ahead.

**Bring in: your ID, and a transparent bottle of water with no label.** That is the whole list.

**Leave outside:** everything else. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">All watches of any kind</mark> — there is a clock in the room. Phones and electronics must be surrendered switched off; keeping one on you is disqualification. No food. No personal stationery, pencils included — the centre supplies a pen.

**Provided:** pen and paper, plus a login-details sheet you may write notes on. On screen: note-taking, highlight and underline, adjustable font size and background colour, volume control, question navigator, review flags, and a timer that flashes at ten and five minutes.

**Listening.** No transfer time — roughly two minutes to review at the end. Part 4's mid-recording pause is silent and unannounced and all its reading time comes at the start, so all ten questions are pre-read in one block. The audio plays once.

**Reading.** No transfer time in either format. Split screen, free navigation, auto-save.

**Breaks.** None scheduled. Toilet breaks are permitted by raising your hand, and on computer you can hide the question and resume. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">No official page says the clock stops — assume it does not.</mark>

**Speaking.** British Council Kazakhstan offers two modes: face to face, or by Zoom videocall **at the test centre**. Never from home, in either case. 11-14 minutes, three parts, digitally recorded, with a live certified examiner in both ([[IELTS Speaking stays face-to-face with a live examiner in both computer-delivered and paper formats|detail]]).

## Results

**Timing.** The providers contradict themselves — the British Council results page says 1-2 days, its FAQ says 3-5, IDP says 1-5. Globally about 80% of scores land within two days. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Plan on five days, expect two.</mark>

**The Test Report Form is electronic.** British Council Kazakhstan stopped printing paper TRFs on 1 June 2026 — the e-TRF appears in your Test Taker Portal.

**Validity is two years.** IELTS recommends two years and cannot confirm results older than that; guidance to institutions is stricter still, suggesting an older TRF be accepted only with evidence that English has been maintained.

**Sending results:** five copies free within two years. List the institutions in the portal; those on the verification service view results electronically, the rest are posted. Beyond five, 5,000₸ per copy per organisation, about seven working days.

## If the result is short

Three instruments — and importantly, <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">a remark and a One Skill Retake are sequential, not parallel</mark>. You must wait for a remark outcome before booking a retake of that skill, and IDP requires the remark to be filed within two weeks to keep the retake open. Choose a lane early.

### One Skill Retake

Both providers offer it in Astana. *(The British Council markets it as "exclusively" theirs; IDP's own Astana session listings all carry the One Skill Retake badge, so that claim is marketing copy.)*

- The original must have been **computer-delivered**.
- **One skill, once per full test**, with the same provider as the original.
- <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Must be sat — not merely booked — within 60 days of the original.</mark>
- 69,500₸ at the British Council, about 63% of a full retake. IDP does not publish a price.
- Results in 3-5 days. You get a new Test Report Form with the retaken skill plus the three original scores; **both forms stay valid and you choose which to use**.
- Validity still runs from the **original** test date. The retake does not reset the clock.

**The real risk is acceptance.** One Skill Retake is opt-in for institutions — they must enable it, and the Test Report Form flags it explicitly, so a non-accepting institution will see it. The downside is bounded because the original form survives, but the upside only exists if your target institutions are registered. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Check that before choosing it over a full retake.</mark>

### Enquiry on Results

Apply within six weeks of the test. 53,000₸ at the British Council, self-service through the portal; IDP routes it through the centre and publishes no fee. You nominate any subset of the four skills. Turnaround is quoted from a few hours up to 21 days. A score can only rise or hold, and <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">the fee is refunded in full if any band changes</mark>.

Only ever nominate Writing or Speaking. Listening and Reading answers are right or wrong.

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">No success rate is published by anyone.</mark> IELTS publishes examiner reliability — 0.92 for Writing, 0.90 for Speaking — and no remark outcome data at all. Circulating figures run from 10% to 50%, none traceable to an IELTS partner. Treat every one of them as folklore. The reliability figures cut both ways: a remark is unlikely to overturn a band, which also means your Writing score is probably a real measurement rather than noise.

### A full retake

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">There is no waiting period and no limit on attempts.</mark> The British Council Kazakhstan page says so directly. The old 90-day rule could not be verified on any official or archived page and should be treated as folklore.

## What a February sitting buys you

Assume Saturday 6 February 2027, illustratively:

- Results by roughly 8-11 February.
- **One Skill Retake window closes about 7 April** — 60 days, and it must be *sat* by then. Booking it in late February returns results in early March.
- **Remark deadline about 20 March.** But to keep the retake open, file it inside two weeks — around 20 February.
- **A full retake has no waiting period**, and with near-daily computer sessions in Astana a second sitting in late February returns results well before the end of March.

So February leaves room for one remark, or one One Skill Retake, or one full retake with time to spare — and, moving fast, a remark resolved in early March followed by a full retake mid-month. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Booking early February rather than late February widens every one of those windows by two weeks.</mark>

## Not verified

Stated plainly rather than guessed: the British Council's actual Astana testing-point address (published only in the booking email; a third-party listing gives 33 Kunaev St, Astanalyk Business Centre, 14th floor); February 2027 seat availability for either provider; IDP's remark and One Skill Retake fees; whether headphones are officially provided and whether the note paper is paper or a whiteboard; rules on tissues and medication; whether toilet breaks are restricted during Listening; whether the clock pauses during a break; whether fingerprint scanning is used in Astana; IDP Astana's Speaking delivery mode; and whether you may sit a new full test while a remark is pending.

Seasonality is genuinely unknown — no official source publishes fill rates or booking windows for Kazakhstan, so any claim that February is busy would be invention. What is observable is that IDP Astana runs sessions on most weekdays and the British Council advertises 50+ national dates a month, so a *specific date* is far likelier to be constrained than availability in general.
