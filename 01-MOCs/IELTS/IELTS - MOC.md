## The scoring system

The single most consequential fact about IELTS is that it is four independent scores averaged, not one score. That arithmetic, not motivation, decides what to study first.

The second fact is that within a criterion the rubric is a floor rather than an average — which is why a band is held down by one recurring habit rather than raised by occasional brilliance.

- [[The IELTS overall band is the mean of four sections so one weak skill can be carried by the other three|Overall band is a mean — 8.0 needs the four bands to sum to 31, not four 8s]]
- [[A criterion band is awarded only when the performance fully fits that band's positive features|Full fit rule — your worst recurring habit sets the criterion, not your best sentence]]
- [[Mastering the band descriptors moves a score more than adding practice-test volume|The descriptors are the unlock; practice volume is the measuring instrument]]

## Writing Task 1 — Academic

Task 1 is worth a third of the Writing band and is the most mechanical part of the exam: a fixed input, a predictable output, and one hard gate written into the descriptor.

- [[A Task 1 answer that recounts detail with no clear overview cannot pass band 5 for Task Achievement|No overview caps Task Achievement at band 5 — and Task 1 takes no conclusion]]
- [[Explaining why data changed is off-task in Academic Writing Task 1|Task 1 is information transfer; speculation outside the data earns nothing]]
- [[Grouping the data before writing is what separates a Task 1 report from a list|Decide the grouping axis before writing — past ~210 words you are listing]]
- [[Band 8 Task Achievement adds illustrating each feature with chosen data not adding more language|Band 8 illustrates each feature with chosen figures; both extremes are punished]]

## Writing Task 2

Task 2 carries twice the weight of Task 1 and is where the 7-to-8 decision is made. Two of its four criteria are settled before the first sentence is written.

- [[Task 2 is worth twice Task 1 in the Writing band so the time split is not fifty-fifty|Task 2 is double-weighted — 20 minutes and 40 minutes, not 30 and 30]]
- [[Half the Task 2 marks are decided in the plan before a sentence is written|TR and CC are half the mark and both are settled in the plan]]
- [[Band 7 Task Response is capped by over-generalisation rather than by missing parts of the question|The band 7 TR ceiling is over-generalisation, which is why templates plateau]]
- [[Discuss both views and give your own opinion is two tasks and the opinion must run throughout|Discussion essays fail on a missing, late, or merely reported opinion]]
- [[Task 2 coherence is scored on paragraph progression not on the density of linking words|CC is paragraph topics and reference, not connective density]]
- [[Band 8 grammar means the majority of sentences are error-free while band 7 needs only frequent ones|Band 8 GRA is subtraction — remove recurring errors, don't add structures]]
- [[Band 8 lexis tolerates occasional collocation errors but not a narrow range|Band 8 lexis forgives the odd wrong word; it does not forgive a narrow range]]
- [[IELTS Writing errors are assessed by density and communicative effect rather than by count|Errors are scored by density, so one repeated fault beats several one-offs]]
- [[The template that carries a writer from 5.5 to 7 is the same thing that caps them at 7|The template that got you to 7 is the thing now holding you at 7]]
- [[A thesaurus swap preserves the meaning and destroys the collocation|Rarer synonyms cost collocation marks; paraphrase the phrase, not the noun]]
- [[Articles are the largest error category for writers whose first language has no article system|Articles are the biggest error category for an article-less L1 — and the cheapest fix]]
- [[Most IELTS writing style rules are folklore with no basis in the descriptors|"Never use I", "300+ words", "use the passive" — all folklore]]

## Speaking

Speaking is rated on average performance across all three parts, and the criterion most candidates worry about — pronunciation — turns out to reward the opposite of what they train.

- [[Band 8 speaking allows hesitation to find content but not to find words or grammar|Band 8 fluency is about where the pause falls, not how long it is]]
- [[The Part 2 preparation minute is for choosing one real example not for brainstorming|The prep minute picks one real example; the bullets are a guide, not a checklist]]
- [[Part 3 is scored on abstraction and qualification while Part 1 length answers cap the fluency band|Part 3 rewards hedging and abstraction; Part 1-sized answers cap fluency]]
- [[Memorised speaking answers lower the pronunciation score and provoke harder questions|Memorised answers cost pronunciation marks and get you a harder test]]
- [[Speaking band 7 pronunciation is defined only as sitting between band 6 and band 8|There is no band 7 pronunciation descriptor — you train the band 8 list]]
- [[IELTS penalises the effect of an accent on intelligibility not the accent itself|Accent is not penalised; listener effort is]]
- [[Prosodic pronunciation training transfers to spontaneous speech while segmental training does not|Prosody transfers to spontaneous speech; segmental drilling does not]]
- [[Final obstruent devoicing is the one segmental fix worth study time for a Russian first language|Final devoicing turns bed into bet — the one sound fix worth doing]]
- [[Speaking topic prediction lists are a coverage map rather than a leak|Forecast lists are a coverage map, never a script bank]]
- [[IELTS Speaking stays face-to-face with a live examiner in both computer-delivered and paper formats|Speaking is face-to-face in both formats; video-call scores differ negligibly]]

## Listening and Reading

The two objectively-marked sections. These are the cheapest bands in the exam and the reason an overall 8.0 is reachable without a Writing 8.0 — see the strategy note in the scoring section above. At 30/40 the losses are comprehension; at 34/40 a disproportionate share of what remains is mechanical, and mechanical marks cost the same as real ones.

- [[Listening and Reading bands come from a raw score out of 40 so they are trainable by technique alone|Band 8 is 35/40 in both — bought in single countable marks]]
- [[Listening distractors work by stating an answer and then withdrawing it|Listening distractors state an answer then cancel it — take the second value]]
- [[An over-limit Listening answer scores zero even when the content heard was correct|Over the word limit scores zero; so does giving two answers]]
- [[False requires a contradiction while partial coverage of the statement is Not Given|False needs contradiction; partial coverage is Not Given]]
- [[A matching heading must summarise the whole paragraph which is why keyword matching fails|A heading covers the whole paragraph — do this type last]]
- [[Matching features is the one Reading type whose answers do not follow passage order|Matching features runs out of order and is the only type where scanning works]]
- [[Locating the answer then reading carefully beats the skim-then-scan ritual|Locate fast then read carefully; the skim-first ritual is unsupported]]
- [[Computer-delivered Listening removes the transfer time so typing accuracy becomes part of the score|No transfer time on computer — typing accuracy becomes a scoring variable]]

### Listening technique, ranked by marks per hour

Ordered by what a mark actually costs to recover, not by how much is written about it. The top three are mechanical — the word was heard correctly and the mark was lost anyway — and mechanical marks are the cheapest in the paper because the fix is a rule rather than a skill.

Most of what circulates about Listening technique is practitioner heuristic with no evidence behind it. The exceptions are marked: cascade loss and the attention-switching cost come from Field's IELTS-commissioned protocol study, and the preview finding from a controlled comparison of preview conditions. Where a widely-repeated tip has no traceable source, the note says so rather than dropping it silently.

- [[The question stem's grammar settles singular or plural before the audio does|Plural is decided by the printed stem, not by a /s/ you cannot hear]]
- [[Dictation with immediate comparison is the spelling remediation that matches the Listening task|Spelling is fixed by audio-front dictation on your own error log, not by word lists]]
- [[A missed Listening answer becomes a cascade when you keep listening for information already gone|A missed answer costs four marks unless you close it and re-anchor]]
- [[Listening preview time helps mainly through the question stems while the answer options add little|Preview the stems first — the options add almost nothing when time is short]]
- [[Predict the answer slot rather than the answer word because a predicted word gets confirmed instead of heard|Predict word class and number; predicting the word makes you hear it]]
- [[Keyword training backfires when attention shifts from the audio to the question paper|One or two keywords per question — reading during the audio costs audio]]
- [[Part 3 matching often tests what the speakers jointly settle on rather than what one speaker said|Part 3 asks what they agreed, so first mention is the distractor]]
- [[Part 4 is a lecture with the redundancy stripped out so nothing gets restated|Part 4 never repeats itself and gives you one preview block for ten questions]]

## How to prepare

The method matters more than the hours, because the hours are few and the evidence says undirected volume is the input that fails to convert. Two loops run in parallel: an objective one for Listening and Reading, driven by error categories, and a subjective one for Writing and Speaking, which needs an external rater the candidate cannot supply.

- [[Log wrong answers by error category because the score tells you nothing about what to fix|Log errors by category — the score never tells you what to fix]]
- [[A marked essay only changes a score when it is rewritten after the feedback|The unit of work is essay plus rewrite, and one marked essay is noise]]
- [[Booking the test unlocks the best free IELTS resource so booking comes before diagnosing|Book first, then diagnose — Premium unlocks on booking, not payment]]
- [[A real sitting is the only trustworthy band measurement available before the exam|Since Progress Check closed, only a real sitting gives a calibrated Writing band]]

### Using AI without being misled by it

- [[LLM essay scoring matches examiners on average but varies by over a band on any single essay|AI scoring has no mean bias but ±1.3-band spread — and compresses at the top]]
- [[Ask an LLM what rule a sentence breaks rather than what band the essay is|Ask for the located, named error; never ask for the band]]
- [[Anchor an LLM to your own marked scripts because its absolute scale drifts with every model version|Anchor it to your own marked scripts and ask comparatively]]
- [[A text-based LLM cannot assess pronunciation because the signal is not in the transcript|Transcript-only pronunciation scoring is 0.404 accurate — pause markup changes nothing]]

## What the evidence says about the ceiling

The official 2024-25 test-taker data settles the strategic question, and it settles it against the intuitive answer. This section exists because the whole prep plan is built on it.

- [[Writing is the lowest-scoring IELTS section for almost every first language including native English speakers|Writing is the lowest section for 33 of 40 languages — natives average 6.49]]
- [[No first language group averages band 7 in Writing while Reading group averages reach above 8|No language group averages 7 in Writing; Reading reaches 8.15]]
- [[Study hours convert into band gains worse as the starting band rises|Hours per band rise with starting level — "200 hours per band" is retired folklore]]
