> Track 4 of the [[Interview-Prep-Master-Plan|Senior Backend interview-prep system]]
> The behavioral round is the **leveling round** — it decides senior vs mid, not just pass/fail. Most engineers underprepare here. Don't.

---

### Why this track matters most for *you*

At ~1.5 years experience targeting a **senior** title, the behavioral round is where the level gets set.

A polished STAR answer about a routine task still fails the senior bar — down-levels are almost always caused by **weak story content**, not weak delivery.

So the work here is not "practice talking." It's **surfacing and framing real moments of ownership, influence, and ambiguity** from your experience, then rehearsing until you can re-angle any of them on the fly.

---

### The method: STAR-L (not plain STAR)

Use **Situation → Task → Action → Result → Learning**.

The **Learning** suffix is what signals seniority — it shows self-awareness and growth, not just task completion.

Proportions per answer:
- **Situation + Task** → 2-3 sentences total. Set context fast.
- **Action** → roughly half the answer. This is *your* contribution — say **"I"**, not "we".
- **Result** → quantify it. Attach a number wherever possible.
- **Learning** → one line. What you'd do differently / what it taught you.

Target delivery: **2-3 minutes per answer**, spoken, not recited.

---

### The core insight: select stories, don't memorize answers

Do **not** prepare one canned answer per question — questions are too varied, and reciting is the most common failure mode.

Instead build a **story bank of 5-7 strong stories**, each tagged by the competencies it demonstrates. In the interview you *decode the signal* behind the question, then *select* the highest-scope relevant story and angle it to fit.

One strong project can cover ownership, ambiguity, conflict, and technical depth — different angles of the same story.

---

### Competency map

Every behavioral question probes one of these signals. Your 5-7 stories must cover all of them between them:

| Competency | What it probes | Typical prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Drove something end-to-end, beyond your remit | "Tell me about a time you took initiative." |
| **Conflict** | Disagreement with a peer/manager, resolved well | "A time you disagreed with a colleague." |
| **Failure** | Owned a mistake, learned from it | "Tell me about a time you failed." |
| **Leadership / influence** | Moved others without authority, mentored | "A time you influenced a decision." |
| **Ambiguity** | Made progress without clear requirements | "A time you worked with unclear scope." |
| **Technical decision** | Made + defended a non-obvious tradeoff | "A hard technical decision you made." |
| **Communication** | Explained complex things, handled stakeholders | "Explaining something technical to non-engineers." |
| **Growth** | Sought feedback, leveled up deliberately | "How have you grown in the last year?" |

⚠️ **Your gap:** at 1.5 years you may lack true cross-team leadership stories. Mine your real experience hard for any moment of mentoring, incident ownership, or scope-stretch — and frame the impact with metrics.

---

### The story bank (build 5-7)

Each story lives as an atomic note in `02-Zettelkasten/` and is tracked in [[Behavioral-Interview - Tracker]].

Recommended core set:
1. **Biggest ownership win** — a project you drove end-to-end (covers ownership, technical decision, result).
2. **Conflict resolved** — disagreement with a peer/lead that you turned productive.
3. **Genuine failure** — a real mistake you owned, with concrete learning. Non-negotiable; everyone gets asked.
4. **Influence without authority** — convinced others / changed a decision.
5. **Ambiguity navigated** — unclear requirements, you created clarity.
6. **Technical tradeoff** — a hard call you made and defended (ties into system-design thinking).
7. **Growth / feedback** — a time you actively leveled yourself up.

---

### Company-research routine (per application)

Before each interview:
1. Pull the company's stated **values / leadership principles** (careers page, eng blog).
2. Pre-map **which of your 7 stories hits each value**.
3. Tailor scope examples to the role's seniority.
4. Prepare 3-4 **questions to ask them** (signals seriousness + evaluates fit).

### Salary negotiation basics

Covered in [[Salary negotiation starts after the offer, never anchor first]] and related notes.

- **Never share current comp or a number first.** Deflect: *"I'm flexible — what's the range for this role?"*
- Wait for their offer, then **counter using market data** (level + remote, not location-adjusted to your local salary).
- **Competing offers are the strongest leverage** — apply to 3+ simultaneously.
- A counter will **not** get an offer rescinded. Negotiating is expected.
- Resist exploding-offer pressure ("you have 24 hours"). Ask for time.

Your numbers (from [[Remote-Job-Prep]]): floor **$2,000/mo**, ask **$4,500-6,000/mo**. Never anchor to local ~$1,280/mo.

---

### Common mistakes to avoid

- ❌ Reciting STAR robotically / one canned story per question type.
- ❌ Generic "we did X" stories with vague results ("things went well").
- ❌ No failure story, or a fake-failure ("I work too hard").
- ❌ No metrics — impact must be quantified.
- ❌ Skipping company/values research.
- ❌ Winging the salary conversation (sharing a number first, accepting the first offer, caving to pressure).
- ❌ Assuming coding skill or charisma carries the round. Social storytelling ≠ behavioral signal.

### Workflow per story

1. Draft the story as an atomic note in `02-Zettelkasten/` (STAR-L structure, in your own words).
2. Tag the competencies it covers.
3. Create flashcards in `05-Flashcards/behavioral/` — prompt → which story + key beats (recall the *structure*, not a script).
4. Rehearse **out loud**, recorded, until you deliver in 2-3 min and can re-angle it.
5. Mark progress in [[Behavioral-Interview - Tracker]].
