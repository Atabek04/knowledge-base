How people acquire a second language, and how Arabic changes the answer because every learner meets a standard form and a spoken dialect. An Arabic learning platform needs this to separate theory that is tested from theory that is only quoted.

Parent: none. Read in order; each section assumes the ones above. Teaching progress: not started.

## 1. Input and the contested hypotheses (Core)

- [[Comprehensible input at i+1 requires graded exposure sequenced by difficulty to drive acquisition|Comprehensible input at i+1: graded exposure drives acquisition]]
- [[Input laddering sequences listening and reading materials by difficulty to maintain comprehensible i+1 throughout acquisition|Input laddering: sequence materials by difficulty]]
- [[High affective filter blocks language acquisition even when input is comprehensible|Affective filter: anxiety blocks comprehensible input]]
- [[Immersion accelerates acquisition only when input remains comprehensible and engagement stays active|Immersion: works only while input stays comprehensible]]
- [[Noticing Hypothesis states that conscious attention to form is a prerequisite for converting input to intake|Noticing: attention to form turns input into intake]]
- [ ] Krashen's input hypothesis is hard to test because i and i+1 are never operationally defined (Gass, Behney and Plonsky 2025)
- [ ] Comprehensible input is necessary but not sufficient, since immersion learners show persistent grammar gaps (Swain 1985)
- [ ] The acquisition-learning distinction fails because explicit instruction produces durable gains that beat implicit instruction (Norris and Ortega 2000)
- [ ] Noticing is necessary for intake, but the strong claim that no unnoticed form is ever learned is contested (Schmidt 1990, Truscott 1998)
- [ ] L2 anxiety measurably impairs processing, which is the empirical core that survives of the affective filter (MacIntyre and Gardner 1994)
- [ ] A platform should operationalise i+1 as known-word coverage of a text, not as a hidden grammar level (Nation 2006)

## 2. Interaction, output and feedback (Core)

- [[Interactional Hypothesis explains why negotiated conversation accelerates acquisition beyond passive exposure|Interaction hypothesis: negotiation speeds acquisition]]
- [[Output Hypothesis states that producing language forces learners to notice gaps that input alone cannot reveal|Output hypothesis: producing language reveals gaps]]
- [[Speaking from day one builds acquisition through output pressure and immediate feedback from interaction|Speak from day one: output plus feedback]]
- [[Shadowing trains prosody and fluency by simultaneously vocalizing native-speaker audio|Shadowing: trains prosody by vocalizing audio]]
- [ ] Interaction with feedback improves L2 development, and effects are larger in lab studies than in classrooms (Mackey and Goo 2007)
- [ ] Classroom corrective feedback has durable effects, and prompts that make the learner retrieve the form beat recasts (Lyster and Saito 2010)
- [ ] Output practice forces syntactic processing that listening does not, but only when pushed and followed by feedback (Swain 1995)
- [ ] Fluency development needs repeated practice on known material, as in the 4/3/2 task, which trains speaking fluency and not vocabulary (Nation 1989)

## 3. Vocabulary (Core)

- [[Nation's vocabulary load research identifies word-frequency thresholds for unaided text comprehension|Nation: frequency thresholds for unaided comprehension]]
- [[Deliberate vocabulary study and incidental acquisition target different frequency bands in the lexicon|Deliberate vs incidental vocabulary: different frequency bands]]
- [[Teaching vocabulary in semantic sets creates interference that slows initial acquisition|Semantic sets: related words interfere at first learning]]
- [[Etymology and morphological root awareness multiply vocabulary acquisition by making word families transparent|Roots and etymology: make word families transparent]]
- [[Spaced repetition schedules vocabulary review at expanding intervals matched to the forgetting curve|Spaced repetition: expanding intervals fit forgetting]]
- [[Sentence mining builds Anki cards from real input to preserve the contextual meaning that isolated words lose|Sentence mining: cards from real input keep context]]
- [[Keyword mnemonic links a foreign word to a vivid mental image via a sound-alike bridge|Keyword mnemonic: a sound-alike image bridge]]
- [[Method of Loci encodes vocabulary by placing words as objects along a memorized spatial route|Method of Loci: words along a spatial route]]
- [[Same-language captions produce larger L2 acquisition gains than translated captions across proficiency levels|Same-language captions: beat translated captions]]
- [[Sequential L1-then-bilingual subtitle exposure balances comprehension scaffolding with target-language form encoding|Sequential subtitles: L1 first, then bilingual]]
- [ ] Incidental vocabulary learning works but gains are small and variable, so a platform cannot rely on it alone (Webb, Uchihara and Yanagisawa 2023)
- [ ] Word frequency decides which words repay deliberate study first, and the first 2000 to 3000 families cover most running text (Nation 2013)
- [ ] A word needs about ten meaningful encounters to stick incidentally, so spaced retrieval beats waiting for encounters (Webb 2007, Nakata 2015)

## 4. Course design and effect sizes (Working)

- [ ] The four strands give a balanced course with equal time on input, output, language-focused study and fluency (Nation 2007)
- [ ] SLA effect sizes need field-specific benchmarks, because Cohen's 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 underestimate typical L2 effects (Plonsky and Oswald 2014)
- [ ] Sleep after learning supports L2 word retention, but the L2 sleep literature is small and mixed (Gaskell and Dumay 2003)

## 5. Arabic diglossia and curriculum shape (Core)

- [ ] Diglossia means two related varieties with divided functions, and no Arabic speaker is a native speaker of the high form (Ferguson 1959)
- [ ] Arab states show a continuum rather than two clean varieties, so learners meet mixed registers in real speech (Badawi 1973, Holes 2004)
- [ ] The integrated approach teaches Modern Standard Arabic and one dialect together, while the traditional approach teaches the standard first (Al-Batal 2018, Ryding 2013)
- [ ] The gap between spoken and standard Arabic delays early literacy, since phonological awareness is trained on the dialect (Saiegh-Haddad 2003)
- [ ] A Quran-focused learner and a communication-focused learner need different goals and different curricula (Husseinali 2006)
- [ ] The root and pattern system makes new words partly predictable, but root awareness helps only after morphological analysis is taught (Boudelaa and Marslen-Wilson 2005)
- [ ] Unvocalised Arabic text is ambiguous for learners, so short vowel marks are scaffolding to fade (Abu-Rabia 2001, Ryding 2013)
- [ ] Arabic reading is harder for beginners than English or Spanish, so time-to-fluency estimates must be Arabic-specific (US Defense Language Institute ranking)

## 6. Arabic and AI tutors (Working)

- [ ] Large language models are weaker on Arabic dialects than on the standard form, so AI tutors need evaluation per variety (Abdelali 2024, Khondaker 2023)
- [ ] Automatic speech recognition for Arabic learner speech and tajwid rules is an open area with scarce learner data
- [ ] AI chatbots in L2 education show promise, but efficacy evidence is uneven across designs (Wiboolyasarin 2025)

## Contested claims to avoid
- Acquisition and learning are separate systems and learning never becomes acquisition: untestable as stated; explicit instruction yields durable gains (Norris and Ortega 2000).
- Comprehensible input alone is enough: immersion students keep grammar gaps (Swain 1985).
- i+1 is a measurable level: no operational definition, so near-unfalsifiable (Gass and colleagues).
- Learning styles in language learning: no support for matching.
- Nation's 4/3/2 builds vocabulary: it is a fluency task; no vocabulary test of it was found.
- Arabic is the hardest language at 2200 hours: a difficulty ranking for English speakers, not an effect size, and not about Central Asian learners (US Defense Language Institute).
- Starting in a dialect and then moving to the standard works for everyone: the integrated-approach evidence is mostly classroom reports and surveys.
- Cohen's 0.2, 0.5, 0.8 as the benchmark for L2 effects: too lenient for the field (Plonsky and Oswald 2014).
