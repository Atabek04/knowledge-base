How learners develop, what moves them to start and keep going, and what gets in their way: motivation theory, emotion, reading, development, special needs and accessibility.
A learning platform for K-12 and university learners needs this layer, because content that is well designed still fails when the learner does not engage.

Parent: none. Read in order; each section assumes the ones above. Teaching progress: not started.

## 1. Self-determination theory (Core)

- [ ] Self-determination theory says sustained motivation needs three basic needs: autonomy, competence and relatedness (Ryan and Deci 2000)
- [ ] Motivation is a continuum from amotivation through external, introjected, identified and integrated regulation to intrinsic (Deci and Ryan 2020)
- [ ] Autonomy support means choice, rationale and acknowledged feelings, and is not the same as permissiveness (Niemiec and Ryan 2009)
- [ ] Rewards perceived as controlling reduce intrinsic motivation while informational feedback raises it (Deci and Ryan 1985)
- [ ] Children paid for drawing later drew less on their own, the original overjustification study (Lepper, Greene and Nisbett 1973)
- [ ] In 128 studies, expected tangible rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation and verbal praise raised it (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999)
- [ ] Cameron and Pierce found a smaller harm confined to specific conditions, so the reward debate is still live (Cameron and Pierce 1994)
- [ ] Intrinsic motivation declines across school grades, especially at transitions, partly from poor stage-environment fit (Lepper 2005, Eccles 1993)
- [ ] Gamification raises autonomy and relatedness but barely competence, so points and badges are not a motivation fix (Huang and Hew 2024)
- [ ] SDT rests on correlational and classroom studies, and universality of the three needs across cultures is debated (Ryan and Deci 2017)

## 2. Expectancy-value theory (Core)

- [ ] Expectancy-value theory says effort and choice follow from "can I succeed" and "is it worth it" (Wigfield and Eccles 2000)
- [ ] Task value splits into attainment, intrinsic, utility and cost components (Eccles and Wigfield 2002)
- [ ] Cost is the forgotten component and predicts disengagement beyond expectancy and value (Flake 2015)
- [ ] Utility-value interventions raise interest and grades, most for students with low expectancy (Hulleman and Harackiewicz 2009)
- [ ] Parents' beliefs and teacher feedback shape a child's expectancy before ability beliefs stabilise (Eccles)
- [ ] Motivation interventions overall show small to moderate gains (Lazowski and Hulleman 2016)

## 3. Self-efficacy and attribution (Core)

- [[Rebuilding confidence requires starting with small achievable goals before scaling up|Small wins first: confidence is rebuilt through achievable goals before scaling up]]
- [[Over-relying on AI coding tools without reading output erodes genuine programming confidence|AI over-reliance: skipping the reading of output erodes confidence in your own skill]]
- [ ] Self-efficacy is belief in one's ability to do a specific task, and differs from global self-esteem (Bandura 1997)
- [ ] Mastery experience is the strongest source of self-efficacy, ahead of modelling, persuasion and arousal (Bandura 1997)
- [ ] Self-efficacy correlates with academic performance at a moderate level (Honicke and Broadbent 2016)
- [ ] Attribution to effort or strategy sustains persistence while attribution to fixed ability invites helplessness (Weiner 1985, Dweck 1975)
- [ ] Self-handicapping protects self-worth at the cost of performance (Covington 1984)

## 4. Emotions in learning and anxiety (Core)

- [[High affective filter blocks language acquisition even when input is comprehensible|Affective filter: anxiety blocks acquisition even when input is comprehensible]]
- [ ] Control-value theory says achievement emotions arise from perceived control over and value of an activity (Pekrun 2006)
- [ ] Boredom arises from low control and low value, or from over-easy tasks, and lowers performance (Pekrun 2010)
- [ ] Test anxiety correlates negatively with achievement across a thirty-year literature (von der Embse 2018)
- [ ] Foreign language anxiety is a distinct situation-specific anxiety that burdens cognitive processing (Horwitz 1986, MacIntyre and Gardner 1994)
- [ ] Positive emotions broaden attention but can reduce effort when learners are over-relaxed (Pekrun)
- [ ] AI chatbots may lower speaking anxiety through low-stakes practice, but the evidence strength is unassessed (Wiboolyasarin 2025)

## 5. Reading development (Core)

- [ ] The Simple View of Reading says comprehension is decoding multiplied by language comprehension, so a zero in either gives zero (Gough and Tunmer 1986)
- [ ] Scarborough's rope shows skilled reading as word recognition strands becoming automatic while language strands widen (Scarborough 2001)
- [ ] Explicit systematic phonics instruction beats unsystematic approaches for early decoding (National Reading Panel 2000, Castles 2018)
- [ ] Children pass through phases of word reading from logographic cues to full alphabetic mapping to consolidated units (Ehri 2005)
- [ ] Early reading gaps widen because good readers read more and gain vocabulary, the Matthew effect (Stanovich 1986)
- [ ] Reading fluency bridges decoding and comprehension and is trained by guided repeated reading (National Reading Panel 2000)
- [ ] Reading comprehension depends heavily on background knowledge, not only on strategies (Recht and Leslie 1988)
- [ ] Orthographic depth changes how long decoding takes, so shallow orthographies like Kazakh Cyrillic are easier than deep ones (Seymour 2003)
- [ ] Declining PISA 2025 reading in the OECD and Kazakhstan motivates reading-focused platform design (OECD 2026)

## 6. Child and adolescent development (Working)

- [[Zone of Proximal Development defines the teachable gap between independent and guided performance|Zone of proximal development: the gap between solo and guided performance that scaffolding targets]]
- [[Expertise reversal effect makes scaffolded instruction counterproductive once learners build sufficient schema|Expertise reversal: scaffolding that helps novices hurts once schema is built]]
- [ ] Piaget described stages of reasoning, but children show earlier competence than stages predict, so stages are descriptions not limits (Flavell 1979)
- [ ] Working memory capacity and processing speed grow through childhood, so one task loads a ten-year-old far more than an adult (Gathercole)
- [ ] Adolescents reach adult-level reasoning before adult-level self-control, because prefrontal regulation matures after reward sensitivity (Steinberg 2005)
- [ ] Platforms must be designed per age band, because motivation, reading level and autonomy needs change between grades (Eccles 1993)

## 7. Interest and curiosity (Working)

- [[Curiosity gap openings trigger dopaminergic encoding that boosts retention of all subsequent content|Curiosity gap: opening a knowledge gap raises retention, with dopamine claims read cautiously]]
- [ ] Interest develops through four phases from triggered situational interest to well-developed individual interest (Hidi and Renninger 2006)
- [ ] Situational interest can be triggered by design, but sustaining it needs meaning and personal value (Harackiewicz 2016)
- [ ] Curiosity arises from a perceived gap between what one knows and wants to know (Loewenstein 1994)
- [ ] Seductive details, interesting but irrelevant additions, hurt learning (Harp and Mayer 1998)
- [ ] Curiosity peaks at moderate prior knowledge and mid-level uncertainty, an inverted U (Kang 2009)

## 8. Achievement goals (Working)

- [[Rebuilding confidence requires starting with small achievable goals before scaling up|Small wins first: confidence is rebuilt through achievable goals before scaling up]]
- [[Telling Your Goals Prematurely Can Drain the Motivation to Pursue Them|Premature goal sharing: announcing a goal can drain the drive to pursue it]]
- [ ] Mastery goals aim at learning and performance goals aim at showing ability (Dweck and Leggett 1988, Ames 1992)
- [ ] The 2 by 2 model splits performance goals into approach and avoidance, and avoidance goals are the harmful ones (Elliot and McGregor 2001)
- [ ] Classroom goal structure shapes student goals and help-seeking more than personality does (Ryan and Gheen 1998)
- [ ] Mastery goals predict interest and deep strategies more than grades (Hulleman 2010)

## 9. Flow and engagement (Working)

- [ ] Flow is full absorption when challenge matches skill with clear goals and feedback (Csikszentmihalyi 1990)
- [ ] High school students report flow in active, challenging classroom work and rarely in lectures (Shernoff 2003)
- [ ] Engagement has behavioural, emotional and cognitive facets that are measured separately (Fredricks 2004)
- [ ] Active, constructive and interactive engagement produces deeper learning than passive engagement (Chi and Wylie 2014)
- [ ] Flow is measured by self-report, so it is hard to separate from enjoyment (Jackson and Marsh 1996)

## 10. Academic help-seeking (Working)

- [ ] Adaptive help-seeking means asking for the right help at the right time and is a learned self-regulation skill (Nelson-Le Gall 1981)
- [ ] Students avoid help to protect self-worth, especially in performance-goal classrooms (Ryan and Pintrich 2001)
- [ ] Tutoring systems see gaming and hint abuse, and metacognitive feedback improves help use (Aleven 2003, Roll 2011)
- [ ] Instant AI help lowers the cost of asking but may substitute for thinking (Pardos and Bhandari 2024, Fischer 2025)

## 11. Executive function (Working)

- [[Prolonged sitting without movement breaks reduces cerebral blood flow and degrades executive function|Prolonged sitting: weak evidence that movement breaks protect executive function]]
- [ ] Executive function has three core parts: working memory, inhibition and cognitive flexibility (Miyake 2000, Diamond 2013)
- [ ] Childhood executive function predicts later school success and health beyond IQ (Moffitt 2011)
- [ ] Computerized brain training improves the trained task but shows near-zero far transfer to schoolwork (Sala and Gobet 2017)
- [ ] Reducing distraction in a platform helps weaker executive function more than motivation prompts do (Diamond 2013)

## 12. Behavioural economics and nudges (Working)

- [[Present Bias makes immediate rewards defeat future goals by default unless the environment is designed against it|Present bias: immediate rewards beat future goals unless the environment is designed against it]]
- [ ] Defaults, commitment devices and reminders raise task completion in online courses, but effects are small and fade (Thaler and Sunstein 2008, Damgaard and Nielsen 2018)
- [ ] Present bias explains why learners plan to study and then do not, which makes commitment devices a design option (Laibson 1997)
- [ ] Nudges built on social comparison can backfire on low performers, so they need testing in the pilot (Allcott 2011)
- [ ] Reward systems that undermine intrinsic motivation are the central caution for gamification (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999)
- [ ] Implementation intentions that fix when and where to act raise goal attainment (Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006)

## 13. Adolescent well-being and screen time (Working)

- [[Online delivery increases extraneous cognitive load through self-view split-attention and screen fatigue|Online delivery: self-view and screen fatigue add extraneous load]]
- [ ] Digital technology use and adolescent well-being are associated, but the link is tiny (Orben and Przybylski 2019)
- [ ] Haidt argues social media caused the teen mental-health decline while critics call the evidence correlational (Haidt 2024, Odgers 2024)
- [ ] School phone bans show mixed, small effects on wellbeing and attainment (Goodyear 2025, Abrahamsson 2026)
- [ ] Mobile multitasking during learning impairs performance (Chen 2025)
- [ ] Screen time as one number is a poor measure, because content, context and displaced activities matter (Madigan 2019)

## 14. Special educational needs and inclusion (Working)

- [ ] Dyslexia is a persistent difficulty with accurate word reading and spelling rooted in phonological processing (Snowling 2019)
- [ ] Structured phonics interventions help dyslexic readers moderately and were the only approach with confirmed efficacy among 22 trials (Galuschka 2014)
- [ ] Coloured overlays and special fonts for dyslexia lack good evidence (Galuschka 2014)
- [ ] ADHD affects executive function and sustained attention, and immediate-feedback designs may help engagement (Barkley)
- [ ] Autistic learners often benefit from predictable structure, explicit instruction and reduced sensory load (NCAEP 2020)
- [ ] Inclusion works when supports are built in rather than bolted on, but outcome evidence is mixed (Hehir 2016)

## 15. Accessibility and assistive technology (Working)

- [ ] WCAG 2.2 organises accessibility around perceivable, operable, understandable and robust content (W3C 2023)
- [ ] Captions, alt text, keyboard navigation and sufficient contrast are baseline requirements for an education platform (W3C WAI)
- [ ] Screen readers and text-to-speech are core assistive technology, and platform markup must support them (W3C WAI)
- [ ] Right-to-left and Arabic support affect accessibility on an Arabic learning platform (W3C i18n)

## 16. Universal Design for Learning (Aware)

- [ ] UDL proposes multiple means of engagement, representation, and action and expression, and CAST released Guidelines 3.0 in 2024 (CAST 2024)
- [ ] Boysen argues the evidence for UDL resembles learning styles, so treat UDL as a design heuristic (Boysen 2021)

## 17. Growth mindset (Aware)

- [ ] Mindset meta-analyses found weak overall effects, with some benefit for low-SES or at-risk students (Sisk 2018)
- [ ] A national experiment found grade gains in lower achievers only where peer norms supported the message (Yeager 2019)
- [ ] Macnamara and Burgoyne argue apparent benefits reflect weak design and bias, and Tipton disputes their method (Macnamara and Burgoyne 2023)

## Contested claims to avoid

- Learning styles matching: no support for matching instruction to preferred style (Pashler 2008).
- Growth mindset as a general fix: weak overall effects, but not debunked, since Yeager 2019 shows context-specific gains (Sisk 2018; Macnamara and Burgoyne 2023; Tipton 2023).
- Grit as a strong predictor of success: overlaps conscientiousness, perseverance facet about .18 with performance (Credé 2017).
- Overjustification as universal: depends on reward type, expectation and task (Cameron and Pierce 1994 against Deci 1999, 2001).
- Smartphone presence "brain drain": replication failures reported, status unverified (Ward 2017).
- Social media as the cause of the teen mental-health crisis: correlational, small effects, contested causality (Orben and Przybylski 2019; Odgers 2024).
- Brain training for general cognition: near-zero far transfer (Sala and Gobet 2017).
- Coloured overlays or special fonts for dyslexia: only phonics was confirmed effective (Galuschka 2014).
- UDL as evidence-based: evidence behind the CAST guidelines is weak and learning-styles-like (Boysen 2021).
- Dopamine encoding explanations of curiosity: mechanistic claims outrun the evidence (Gruber 2014).
- Flow as a classroom-quality indicator: self-report, hard to separate from enjoyment (Jackson and Marsh 1996).
- Telling goals prematurely drains motivation: mixed replications, unverified (Gollwitzer against later studies).
- Nudges as a durable fix: effects are small and fade, and social-comparison nudges can backfire (Damgaard and Nielsen 2018).
