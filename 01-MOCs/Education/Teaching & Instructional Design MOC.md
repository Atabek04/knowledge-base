The teacher's side: how to sequence, deliver and check instruction for other people, in a classroom, on slides, on video, or online. The platform needs it to turn learning science into lessons, feedback and tests that can be defended in a thesis.

## 1. Cognitive Load & Worked Examples (Core)

- [[Cognitive load theory explains why working memory bottlenecks determine learning speed|Cognitive load theory: working memory is the bottleneck]]
- [[Worked Examples method reduces cognitive load by showing solved problems before independent practice|Worked examples: study solutions before solving]]
- [[Backward fading removes final worked example steps first to preserve context while building independent problem-solving skill|Backward fading: remove the final steps first]]
- [[Expertise reversal effect makes scaffolded instruction counterproductive once learners build sufficient schema|Expertise reversal: scaffolding hurts once schema forms]]
- [ ] Intrinsic, extraneous and germane load are three roles of working memory demand, and only extraneous load is cut by design (Sweller 2019)
- [ ] The split-attention effect arises when learners must integrate separated sources, so integrated layouts help (Sweller 1994)
- [ ] Worked examples in mathematics give a medium average effect, larger for correct-only examples (Barbieri et al. 2023)
- [ ] Self-explanation prompts help in general, yet in maths worked-example studies they were linked to lower effects (Bisra 2018; Barbieri et al. 2023)
- [ ] Completion problems bridge worked examples and full problems by leaving the last steps to the learner (van Merrienboer 1990)
- [ ] Minimal guidance fails novices because discovery burdens working memory, and the claim covers novices only (Kirschner, Sweller and Clark 2006)
- [ ] Problem-based learning is not minimal guidance, which is the core rebuttal to Kirschner (Hmelo-Silver, Duncan and Chinn 2007)

## 2. Curriculum & Lesson Planning (Core)

- [[Backward Design starts from desired outcomes before planning instruction or activities|Backward design: outcomes before activities]]
- [[Bloom's Taxonomy sequences learning objectives from recall to original creation|Bloom's taxonomy: recall to create]]
- [[Spiral curriculum revisits concepts at increasing depth across multiple encounters|Spiral curriculum: revisit concepts at growing depth]]
- [[Concept prerequisite mapping sequences instruction along the dependency graph|Prerequisite mapping: follow the dependency graph]]
- [[Advance organizers give learners a cognitive hook before new content arrives|Advance organizers: a hook before new content]]
- [ ] Backward design has three stages, results then evidence then plan, and needs assessment defined before activities (Wiggins and McTighe 2005)

## 3. Direct & Explicit Instruction (Core)

- [[Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction describe ten behaviors common to the most effective teachers|Rosenshine's 10 principles of instruction]]
- [ ] Rosenshine's principles came from process-product studies of top teachers, cognitive science and master-teacher programs (Rosenshine 2012)
- [ ] Explicit instruction is modelling, guided practice and independent practice with high success rates (Archer and Hughes 2011)
- [ ] Project Follow Through found Direct Instruction ahead on basic skills, with caveats about design (Stebbins 1977; Watkins 1997)
- [ ] Capitalised Direct Instruction is a scripted curriculum and differs from explicit teaching in general (Stockard et al. 2018)
- [ ] Checking understanding with all-pupil responses beats asking volunteers (Rosenshine 2012; Wiliam 2011)
- [ ] Guided inquiry can match explicit instruction when scaffolded, so the real contrast is guidance level (Alfieri et al. 2011)

## 4. Instructional Design Models (Core)

- [ ] Merrill's first principles are task-centred, activation, demonstration, application and integration (Merrill 2002)
- [ ] Merrill's principles synthesise earlier models and are not a tested package (Frick et al. 2010)
- [ ] The 4C/ID model builds training from whole tasks with learning tasks, supportive information, procedural information and part-task practice (van Merrienboer and Kirschner 2018)
- [ ] 4C/ID learning tasks vary in surface features and shed support, so transfer is designed in (van Merrienboer and Kirschner 2018)
- [ ] ADDIE is a process frame, not a theory of learning (Molenda 2003)
- [ ] Gagne's nine events work as a checklist whose elements have uneven evidence (Gagne 1985)
- [ ] The Knowledge-Learning-Instruction framework links observable instruction to learning processes (Koedinger, Corbett and Perfetti 2012)

## 5. Active Learning & Discussion (Core)

- [[Students rate a polished lecture higher yet learn more from active learning|Polished lecture: feels better, teaches less than active learning]]
- [[Peer Instruction uses ConcepTests and peer discussion to deepen conceptual understanding|Peer instruction: ConcepTest vote, discuss, re-vote]]
- [[Think-Pair-Share gives every learner think time before committing to whole-class discussion|Think-Pair-Share: think time before discussion]]
- [[Socratic questioning uses six question types to probe thinking rather than deliver content|Socratic questioning: six probing question types]]

## 6. Slides, Visuals & Multimedia (Core)

- [[Mayer's Multimedia Learning Principles reduce cognitive load through twelve evidence-based design rules|Mayer's 12 multimedia principles]]
- [[Assertion-Evidence slide structure replaces topic-label titles with claims supported by visual evidence|Assertion-Evidence slides: claim plus visual, not bullets]]
- [[Presentation Zen applies restraint and simplicity to remove noise from slide design|Presentation Zen: restraint and simplicity]]
- [[Each diagram type offloads a specific cognitive operation — choosing the wrong type adds extraneous load instead of removing it|Match the diagram type to the cognitive operation]]
- [[Concept maps require labeled linking phrases to encode relationships rather than mere co-occurrence|Concept maps need labeled linking phrases]]
- [ ] Mayer's third edition describes fifteen principles, so the vault's twelve-principle note needs reconciling (Mayer 2021)
- [ ] The coherence principle removes interesting but irrelevant material because it competes for attention (Mayer 2021)
- [ ] The signaling principle adds organisational cues and has a smaller effect than headline principles (Schneider et al. 2018)
- [ ] The modality principle weakens for text-heavy or slow material and depends on pacing and learner control (Ginns 2005; Reinwein 2012)
- [ ] The redundancy principle reverses for novices facing difficult technical or L2 audio (Sweller 2005; Mayer 2021)
- [ ] The animation advantage over static pictures is small and shrinks when pictures are well designed (Hoffler and Leutner 2007)
- [ ] Many multimedia effects come from short lab sessions with university students, so school transfer is unproven (Mayer 2021; Noetel et al. 2022)

## 8. Mastery Learning & Pace (Core)

- [[One-to-one tutoring beats classroom teaching but Bloom's two sigma gain shrinks to under one sigma in later evidence|Two sigma: tutoring gain shrinks in later evidence]]
- [ ] Mastery learning holds the criterion fixed and lets time vary, reversing the traditional classroom (Bloom 1968)
- [ ] Mastery programs lift class exam scores by about half a standard deviation (Kulik, Kulik and Bangert-Drowns 1990)
- [ ] On standardized tests the mastery gain shrinks to near zero in best-evidence synthesis (Slavin 1987)
- [ ] Mastery gains rise when corrective feedback is paired with enrichment and not just retesting (Guskey 2010)
- [ ] Keller's Personalized System of Instruction is the self-paced ancestor and shows high dropout from procrastination (Keller 1968; Steel 2007)
- [ ] Software mastery criteria, such as n consecutive correct or a knowledge tracing threshold, change when learners count as done (Kelly et al. 2015; Pelanek and Rihak 2017)

## 9. Formative Assessment & Feedback (Core)

- [[Formative assessment generates real-time evidence that adjusts instruction before learning ends|Formative assessment: adjusts instruction in real time]]
- [ ] Formative assessment gains of 0.4 to 0.7 come from mixed designs and are cited too uncritically (Black and Wiliam 1998; Bennett 2011)
- [ ] Wiliam's five strategies are intended learning, eliciting evidence, feedback, peers as resources and ownership (Wiliam 2011)
- [ ] Feedback works at four levels, task, process, self-regulation and self, and praise at the self level helps least (Hattie and Timperley 2007)
- [ ] About one third of feedback interventions lower performance, mainly when attention shifts to the self (Kluger and DeNisi 1996)
- [ ] The 2020 feedback meta-analysis puts the overall effect near 0.48, well below earlier Visible Learning figures (Wisniewski, Zierer and Hattie 2020)
- [ ] Elaborated feedback beats correctness-only feedback on complex tasks, while simple tasks gain little (Van der Kleij et al. 2015)
- [ ] Immediate versus delayed feedback depends on task difficulty and learner expertise (Shute 2008)
- [ ] Feedback only counts when tasks give learners a chance to act on it (Sadler 1989; Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick 2006)
- [ ] Teacher dashboards equalise feedback only if teachers can act on the signal (Knoop-van Campen 2023; Molenaar 2019)

## 10. Psychometrics: Reliability & Validity (Core)

- [ ] Observed score equals true score plus error, so every test score is an estimate, not a fact (Crocker and Algina 1986)
- [ ] Reliability is the share of score variance that is not error, and a reliable test can still measure the wrong thing (AERA, APA, NCME 2014)
- [ ] Cronbach's alpha measures internal consistency and does not prove a single dimension (Sijtsma 2009)
- [ ] Validity is an argument about score interpretation and use, not a property of the test (Kane 2013; Messick 1995)
- [ ] Construct underrepresentation and construct-irrelevant variance are the two main threats to validity (Messick 1995)
- [ ] Standard error of measurement turns a score into a band, so small gaps between pupils are often noise (Crocker and Algina 1986)
- [ ] Item difficulty and discrimination give a first quality screen on any quiz (Haladyna and Rodriguez 2013)
- [ ] Fairness requires checking differential item functioning across groups such as language background (AERA, APA, NCME 2014)

## 11. Item Response Theory & Adaptive Testing (Core)

- [ ] Item response theory models the chance of a correct answer from learner ability and item parameters (Embretson and Reise 2000)
- [ ] The Rasch model fixes discrimination and gives equal-interval ability scores when it fits (de Ayala 2022)
- [ ] The two and three parameter models add discrimination and guessing at the cost of sample size (de Ayala 2022)
- [ ] Item information shows where on the ability scale an item measures best, which drives adaptive selection (Embretson and Reise 2000)
- [ ] Adaptive testing picks each next item for maximum information and reaches equal precision in about half the length (Wainer et al. 2000)
- [ ] Adaptive tests need a calibrated item bank, which is the real cost for a small platform (van der Linden and Glas 2010)
- [ ] Elo-style rating of learners and items is a lightweight stand-in for item response theory (Pelanek 2016)
- [ ] Knowledge tracing models skill state over time while item response theory models one ability snapshot (Corbett and Anderson 1994; Pelanek 2017)

## 12. Item Writing, Rubrics & Grading (Working)

- [[Curve grading ranks students against each other while criterion grading measures them against a fixed standard|Curve grading ranks; criterion grading measures]]
- [ ] Multiple choice items work best with plausible distractors drawn from real misconceptions (Haladyna, Downing and Rodriguez 2002)
- [ ] Three options are usually enough since the fourth distractor is rarely chosen (Rodriguez 2005)
- [ ] A rubric raises scoring reliability when criteria and levels are specific and anchored in examples (Jonsson and Svingby 2007)
- [ ] A rubric alone does not improve learning unless students use it for self-assessment (Panadero and Jonsson 2013)
- [ ] Automated LLM scoring of open answers needs human agreement checks before any high-stakes use (Liu et al. 2025)

## 13. Assessment Security in the AI Era (Working)

- [[An oral exam with live follow-up questions proves a skill that AI-written homework can no longer prove|Oral exam with follow-ups: proves skill AI homework cannot]]
- [ ] Conversational and oral exams restore validity when written work can be AI-generated (Barba and Stegner 2026; Ipeirotis and Rizakos 2026)
- [ ] AI-text detectors have high false-positive rates on non-native writers and are poor evidence for discipline (Liang et al. 2023)
- [ ] Proctoring technology raises privacy and fairness concerns that matter most for minors (Swauger 2020, unverified)
- [ ] Process evidence such as drafts, keystroke logs and oral defence is stronger than product-only evidence (unverified)

## 14. Online vs Offline Teaching (Working)

- [[Synchronous online classes require structured interaction patterns to compensate for absent physical presence|Online classes: need structured interaction]]
- [[Teacher proximity and movement signal accountability and direct attention without verbal instruction|Teacher proximity: directs attention nonverbally]]
- [[Online delivery increases extraneous cognitive load through self-view split-attention and screen fatigue|Online delivery: adds load from self-view and screen fatigue]]

## 15. Curriculum Design & Standards (Working)

- [ ] A curriculum has intended, implemented and attained layers, and gaps between them explain many failed reforms (IEA TIMSS framework)
- [ ] Learning progressions order skills by empirical difficulty and not only by logic (Heritage 2008, unverified)
- [ ] Mapping a national standard to an atomic skill graph is the entry point for Kazakhstan alignment (design task, no source)
- [ ] Knowledge-rich curricula build the vocabulary that later reading depends on, though the claim is contested (Willingham 2006, unverified)

## 16. Teacher Expertise & Classroom Practice (Working)

- [ ] Great teaching rests on content knowledge, quality of instruction, climate, expectations and professional behaviours (Coe et al. 2014)
- [ ] Teacher quality moves lifetime earnings, with sizable long-run value-added effects (Chetty, Friedman and Rockoff 2014)
- [ ] Teachers differ more within than between schools in their effects on achievement (Rivkin, Hanushek and Kain 2005)
- [ ] Teacher expectations affect pupils modestly, mostly through accurate perceptions (Jussim and Harber 2005)
- [ ] Expert teachers differ from novices in noticing, routines and pedagogical content knowledge (Shulman 1986; Berliner 2001)

## 17. Visible Learning & Its Critique (Working)

- [ ] Hattie's synthesis ranks influences by average effect size with 0.40 as a hinge point (Hattie 2009)
- [ ] A statistician's critique argues Cohen's d cannot serve as a universal impact measure (Bergeron and Rivard 2017)
- [ ] Averaging effect sizes mixes outcomes, designs and ages, so rankings are not causal guidance (Slavin 2018; Simpson 2017)
- [ ] Causal studies on standardized tests have a median effect near 0.1, so Cohen's benchmarks mislabel real gains (Kraft 2020)
- [ ] The Education Endowment Foundation reports effects as months of progress with security-of-evidence ratings (EEF Toolkit)

## 18. Mathematics Education Research (Working)

- [ ] Mastery of fractions in primary school predicts later algebra and overall maths achievement (Siegler et al. 2012)
- [ ] Productive failure shows transfer benefits in some designs, so struggle versus direct instruction is contested (Kapur 2016; Sinha and Kapur 2021)
- [ ] Maths anxiety is associated with lower achievement and the relationship runs both ways (Ashcraft 2002; Barroso et al. 2021, unverified)

## Contested claims to avoid
- Learning styles matching: no evidence for a matching benefit (Pashler et al. 2008).
- Hattie's 0.40 hinge and league tables as causal rank: Cohen's d is not a universal impact measure (Bergeron and Rivard 2017; Kraft 2020).
- Bloom's two sigma as a normal expectation: small early samples; modern tutoring effects are about 0.3 to 0.4 (Nickow 2024).
- Mastery learning gives half a standard deviation everywhere: it shrinks on standardized tests (Slavin 1987).
- Black and Wiliam 1998 "0.4 to 0.7": mixed designs, weak control (Bennett 2011).
- "Feedback always helps": a third of effects are negative (Kluger and DeNisi 1996).
- Mayer's median effect sizes as universal: mostly short lab studies; modality and redundancy are conditional.
- Kirschner et al. 2006 as proof against all inquiry: it covers novices and minimal guidance only.
- Bloom's taxonomy as a strict hierarchy of difficulty: weak empirical basis.
- Sweller 2019 evolutionary framing: use the load mechanism only, framed through fitrah and Allah's creation.
- AI-text detectors as proof of cheating: high false-positive rates on non-native writers (Liang et al. 2023).
