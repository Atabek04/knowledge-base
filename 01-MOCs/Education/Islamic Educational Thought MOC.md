The classical Islamic account of knowledge, teacher, student and memorisation, and what the evidence says about hifz. An Arabic and Quran learning platform draws its educational aims from this tradition and its empirical claims from experiment, and the two must stay separate.

## 1. Classical educational thought (Working)

Each note cites a translated primary text plus one academic secondary source.

- [ ] Al-Ghazali's Book of Knowledge divides knowledge into individual and communal obligation, giving a values-based curriculum filter (Ihya Ulum al-Din, Kitab al-Ilm)
- [ ] Al-Ghazali requires the teacher to be a sincere guide who treats students as his own children and corrects gently (Ihya, Book 1)
- [ ] Al-Ghazali teaches that learning must match the learner's capacity, so a teacher withholds what the learner cannot yet grasp (Ihya, Book 1)
- [ ] Ibn Jama'ah's Tadhkirat al-Sami' fixes reciprocal duties of teacher, student and study environment in one manual (Ibn Jama'ah)
- [ ] Al-Zarnuji's Ta'lim al-Muta'allim lists intention, choice of subject and teacher, respect, diligence, review and avoiding idle time as the student's method (von Grunebaum and Abel 1947)
- [ ] Al-Zarnuji advises writing, repetition and mutual review (mudhakara), which matches retrieval and spaced practice without the vocabulary for them (Makdisi 1981)
- [ ] Ibn Khaldun argues that knowledge should be taught in graduated stages with repeated passes, which prefigures spiral curricula (Muqaddimah, Rosenthal translation)
- [ ] Ibn Khaldun warns that severe punishment harms students (Muqaddimah, chapter 6)
- [ ] Classical adab names the ethics of the teacher-student relationship, the Islamic analogue of a conduct framework for a platform (Halstead 2004, al-Attas 1979)
- [ ] Ijaza and personal presence certified transmission, so a platform cannot replace the teacher, only extend practice (Makdisi 1981, Berkey 1992)
- [ ] Islamic educational thought is a source of values for aims, not of effect sizes, and must stay separate from experimental claims in the thesis (Halstead 2004)

## 2. Hifz and spaced review (Working)

- [[Retrieval practice strengthens memory by recalling information rather than re-reading it|Retrieval practice: recalling beats re-reading]]
- [[Spaced repetition schedules vocabulary review at expanding intervals matched to the forgetting curve|Spaced repetition: expanding intervals fit forgetting]]
- [ ] Traditional hifz combines new lesson (sabaq), recent review (sabqi) and distant review (manzil), a tiered spaced review schedule (practitioner descriptions)
- [ ] The spacing effect supports muraja'a in general, but no controlled trial of hifz review schedules was found (Cepeda 2006)
- [ ] Quranic preschooling in Morocco improved serial recall but did not transfer to other memory tasks, so hifz skill is largely domain-specific
- [ ] Memorising to understand through repetition challenges the rote versus understanding dichotomy, applied to hifz only as an analogy (Marton, Dall'Alba and Tse 1996)
- [ ] Memorising text in a language the learner does not understand decouples recall from meaning, and literacy outcomes of Quranic schooling are mixed (Wagner 1993, Boyle 2004)
- [ ] A digital hifz product should treat each ayah as a card with an interval, without replacing a qualified reciter who corrects tajwid (design inference)

## Contested claims to avoid
- Spaced repetition is proven better for hifz: no controlled hifz trial found; support is general spacing research.
- Quran memorisation improves general memory or intelligence: the one Moroccan study shows gains on serial memory only (authors unverified).
- Hifz app retention percentages: marketing claims with no study (commercial blogs).
- Rote memorisation excludes understanding: challenged by Marton, Dall'Alba and Tse (1996), though only analogous to hifz.
- Classical manuals as evidence for effect sizes: they supply aims and values, not experimental results (Halstead 2004).
- Ibn Khaldun on abridgments (mukhtasarat) harming beginners: not found in any source search; check the Muqaddimah text before attributing.
