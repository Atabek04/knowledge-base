September 2026 → February 2027. ~6 h/week. IELTS Academic, computer-delivered, Astana.

This plan is subordinate to higher-priority commitments and does not expand past 6 h/week. Where it competes with the interview-prep track in [[Interview-Prep-Master-Plan]], the interview track wins and this plan absorbs the loss by dropping practice volume, never by dropping the marked-essay loop.

---

## The end-state, stated as a binary

**Done means: the Test Report Form reads Overall 8.0.** Nothing else counts — not a mock score, not an AI estimate, not feeling ready.

That end-state decomposes into an arithmetic condition, not four conditions. The overall band is the mean of the four sections rounded up at .25 and .75, so <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">the four bands must sum to 31.0</mark> ([[The IELTS overall band is the mean of four sections so one weak skill can be carried by the other three|why]]).

**Primary route — L 8.5 · R 8.5 · S 7.5 · W 7.0 = 31.5 → 8.0**, with half a band of slack.

| Route | L | R | S | W | Sum | Reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | 8.5 | 8.5 | 7.5 | 7.0 | 31.5 | **8.0** |
| Minimum viable | 8.5 | 8.5 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 31.0 | **8.0** |
| If Speaking lags | 9.0 | 9.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 32.0 | **8.0** |
| If Writing surprises | 8.0 | 8.0 | 7.5 | 7.5 | 31.0 | **8.0** |
| Naive plan | 8.0 | 8.0 | 8.0 | 8.0 | 32.0 | 8.0 |

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The naive plan is the expensive one.</mark> Every route above reaches 8.0 with Writing at 7.0–7.5, and the official data says Writing 8.0 is the hardest sub-score in the exam — no first-language group on earth averages band 7 in it, and native English speakers average 6.49 ([[Writing is the lowest-scoring IELTS section for almost every first language including native English speakers|data]]). Buying certainty in Listening and Reading is cheaper than fighting Writing from 7.0 to 8.0.

**So the plan's real objective is: get Listening and Reading to 8.5–9.0 and hold them there, get Speaking to 7.5, and stop Writing from falling below 7.0.**

## Deriving the weekly rate

22 working weeks (W1 = Mon 7 Sept 2026; W23 = exam week, ~8 Feb 2027) × 6 h = **~132 hours total**.

| Track | Work to be done | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Close the raw-mark gap from the diagnostic; error-category drilling | ~1 full section + 1 review per week |
| Reading | Same, concentrated on the one or two failing question types | ~1 full passage set + 1 review per week |
| Writing | **8–12 essays marked externally and rewritten** | 1 marked-and-rewritten essay per fortnight |
| Speaking | Recorded practice with a rotating single target | 3 × 20 min per week |
| Review | Error log, descriptor re-reads, mock post-mortems | ~1 h per week, non-negotiable |

The marked-essay count is the rate that actually binds. Below about six the pattern is unreadable; above twelve it repeats ([[A marked essay only changes a score when it is rewritten after the feedback|why]]). Twelve essays across 22 weeks is one per fortnight with two spare slots — that is the schedule's backbone, and everything else flexes around it.

## Week 1 — book, then diagnose

In this order, and the order matters.

**Step 1: book the February sitting with the British Council.** IELTS Ready Premium — roughly 40 full scored practice tests, per-skill courses, AI feedback across all four skills — unlocks on booking, not on payment, and stays open until seven days after the test date. Booking late means preparing without the best free resource available ([[Booking the test unlocks the best free IELTS resource so booking comes before diagnosing|why]]). Booking details in [[IELTS - Exam Day]].

**Step 2: sit a full diagnostic.** Two sittings, ~4.5 hours total — this is week 1's entire budget and it should be.

*Sitting A (~2h40, unbroken, morning):* Listening and Reading from a single Cambridge IELTS 20 or 21 test, on screen if possible. Mark against the book's own answer key and convert with **that book's** raw-score table. Then, immediately, Writing Task 1 and Task 2 from the same test, typed, 60 minutes, no spellcheck.

*Sitting B (~30 min):* a full three-part Speaking test from the same volume, recorded. Transcribe it.

**Step 3: produce the four numbers.**
- Listening and Reading — real bands, from the answer key. Trust these.
- Writing — submit Task 2 to two different AI graders. **Read the per-criterion comments, discount the number.** AI scoring compresses at the top and will tend to call a 7.5 a 7 ([[LLM essay scoring matches examiners on average but varies by over a band on any single essay|why]]). Then compare your essay side by side against an examiner-commented band 8 sample on ielts.org.
- Speaking — self-mark the transcript against the official descriptors, and treat any AI speaking band as noise ([[A text-based LLM cannot assess pronunciation because the signal is not in the transcript|why]]).

**Step 4: one page of output.** Four bands, and for Listening and Reading a list of every wrong answer tagged by question type. That tag list, not the score, is what weeks 2 onwards act on ([[Log wrong answers by error category because the score tells you nothing about what to fix|why]]).

> **Note on raw-score targets.** Secondary sources disagree on the Listening raw score needed for band 8 — some report 30/40, others 35/40. They cannot all be right because **the conversion table varies slightly between test versions**, which is exactly why Cambridge prints one inside each test. Use the table in the volume you sat. Do not use a table you found online.

## Weeks 2–22, conditional on the diagnostic

Every branch below is triggered by the week-1 numbers. Read the branch that matches, ignore the rest.

### Listening

**If Listening ≥ 8.0** — it is close to done. Maintain with one section per week plus review; move the freed hour to Writing. Re-test in W10 and W18 only.

**If Listening 7.0–7.5** — this is the cheapest band in the exam to buy back and the fix is usually clerical, not comprehension. Two-hour weekly block: one full section, then a full review tagging each error as *word form* (travel/travelling), *spelling*, *word-limit breach*, *distractor followed*, or *lost place*. Expect the tags to cluster in one or two buckets; drill only those. Target: back at 8.0+ by W10.

**If Listening < 7.0** — treat it as the highest-priority track for six weeks, at 2 h/week, before Writing gets its second hour. A low Listening score at this level is almost never vocabulary.

### Reading

**If Reading ≥ 8.0** — maintain, one passage set per week, timed. Re-test W10 and W18.

**If Reading 7.0–7.5** — almost certainly one or two question types, most commonly True/False/**Not Given** and matching headings. Do not do more full tests; do 10 questions of the failing type, then review each against the passage text that decides it. Return to full timed sets in W12.

**If Reading < 7.0** — check whether the problem is accuracy or the clock by re-doing the diagnostic passages untimed. Untimed accuracy that is fine means it is a pacing problem and the fix is a per-passage time budget; untimed accuracy that is also poor means it is question-type technique.

### Writing — runs every week regardless of the diagnostic

This track does not branch on the score, because at any starting point between 6.0 and 7.5 the work is the same and the schedule is set by the marking cadence.

**Every fortnight: one Task 2 essay, written to time, marked externally, then rewritten.** The rewrite is not optional and is the part that moves the band. Alternate markers so systematic bias shows up as disagreement rather than as a number.

**In between: one Task 1 under 20 minutes, self-reviewed against three questions only.** Is there an overview? Is the data grouped, or listed? Is every highlighted feature carrying a figure?

**Standing focus, in order of expected return for this profile:**
1. **Articles and plurals.** The largest single error category for a writer without an article system, and distributional — so it contaminates sentences at a rate that makes "majority error-free" arithmetically impossible ([[Articles are the largest error category for writers whose first language has no article system|why]]). Reserve the last five minutes of every timed essay for an articles-only proofread pass.
2. **One concrete example per body paragraph, decided in the plan.** This is the literal band 7→8 Task Response wording ([[Band 7 Task Response is capped by over-generalisation rather than by missing parts of the question|why]]).
3. **Halve the sentence-initial connectors.** Carry cohesion with reference instead ([[Task 2 coherence is scored on paragraph progression not on the density of linking words|why]]).

**If diagnostic Writing < 6.5** — add the second weekly hour here by taking it from whichever of Listening or Reading is already ≥ 8.0, and shorten the marking cadence to one essay every 10 days for W2–W10.

### Speaking

**If Speaking ≥ 7.5** — maintain at 2 × 20 min per week; the risk is regression, not the ceiling.

**If Speaking 6.5–7.0** — 3 × 20 min per week, each session with **one** rotating target rather than "everything":
- Monday: articles and tense, in Part 3 abstract answers — where they concentrate.
- Wednesday: chunking and sentence stress. Shadow a short clip, then re-record your own answer.
- Friday: hesitation *type*. Listen back and mark every pause as content-related or language-related; only the second kind is a problem ([[Band 8 speaking allows hesitation to find content but not to find words or grammar|why]]).

Reviewing everything at once is why recorded practice usually stalls.

**If Speaking < 6.5** — the fluency gap is real and slow-moving; buy live practice rather than more solo recording. See the speaking options in [[IELTS - Resources]].

**Applies to every branch:** no scripted answers. A bank of memorised cue-card responses produces the strong-Part-2/weak-Parts-1-and-3 profile examiners are trained to detect ([[Memorised speaking answers lower the pronunciation score and provoke harder questions|why]]). And do not spend time on accent reduction — accent is explicitly permitted at band 8 ([[IELTS penalises the effect of an accent on intelligibility not the accent itself|why]]).

## Phase structure and the checkpoints

| Phase | Weeks | Shape | Hours/week |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Book and diagnose** | W1 | Book the sitting, sit the full diagnostic, produce the error log | 6 |
| **1 — Technique** | W2–W9 | Branch work above. L/R by error category; Writing marking loop starts W2; Speaking targets rotate | 6 |
| **2 — Production** | W10–W17 | Same loop, but every L/R session is now timed and full-length. Writing cadence unchanged | 6 |
| **3 — Rehearsal** | W18–W21 | Two full four-section mocks under exam conditions (W18, W21), each followed by a full post-mortem. CD interface rehearsal | 6 |
| **4 — Taper** | W22–W23 | No new material. Re-read the four descriptor pages, re-read your own error log, one light section per day. Sleep | 3 |

**Checkpoint W10 — the go/no-go.** Re-test Listening and Reading. If both are ≥ 8.0 the plan is on track. If either is below 7.5, move an hour into it from Writing for W11–W17 and accept Writing at 7.0.

**Checkpoint W18 — the honest one.** After the first full mock, compare the section bands against the primary route. If the sum is below 31, the remaining four weeks go entirely to whichever section has the largest gap to *its own route target* — not to whichever section is lowest.

## Risks, and what each one costs

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Interview-prep track crowds this out | Two consecutive weeks under 4 h | Drop L/R volume, protect the marked-essay fortnight. The essay loop is the only thing with a long feedback delay |
| Writing stalls at 6.5 | Three consecutive markers agree at 6.5 by W14 | Accept it. Re-target L 9.0 / R 9.0 / S 7.5 / W 6.5 = 32.0 → 8.0. Do not spend W15–W21 on Writing |
| AI feedback misleads upward or downward | Any single AI band used as a decision input | Anchor the model to your own marked scripts and ask comparatively only ([[Anchor an LLM to your own marked scripts because its absolute scale drifts with every model version|why]]) |
| Diagnostic is far below the self-estimate | Overall below 6.5 in W1 | Do not re-plan for 8.0 in five months at 6 h/week. Re-scope the target or move the sitting |
| February sitting fills up | Dates unavailable when booking in W1 | Book whatever February date exists immediately; the exact date matters far less than unlocking Premium in W1 |

**The retake question.** Sitting twice is normal strategy, not a failure plan, and a real sitting is the only calibrated Writing mark that exists ([[A real sitting is the only trustworthy band measurement available before the exam|why]]). A sitting costs 110,250₸ and a One Skill Retake 69,500₸; there is no waiting period between full attempts. If the budget allows a second sitting, an earlier one used as a diagnostic is worth more than any course. If it does not, February is the only shot and W18 is where the plan gets honest.

**Book early February, not late.** Every recovery window — the 60-day One Skill Retake deadline, the six-week remark deadline, room for a second full sitting — is measured from the test date, so a sitting in the first week of February is worth two extra weeks of options over one in the last. The full deadline arithmetic is in [[IELTS - Exam Day]].
