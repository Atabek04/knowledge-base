# Segment: Inactive Learners Wanting to Restart

Conditional survey questions for respondents who previously used an Arabic learning method but stopped and want to restart.

**Segment Goal:** Understand churn reasons deeply, validate if product improvements would reactivate them, identify quick-win fixes vs fundamental mismatches.

**Critical Insight:** These respondents are **pre-qualified buyers** — they've already invested in Arabic learning and proven willingness to spend time/money. Churn analysis reveals our biggest product risks.

---

## Q10: Confirming Your Previous Method
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "You mentioned you were using [Telegram/Arabicle/Self-taught] to learn Arabic. Is that still correct?"

**Options:**
- [ ] Yes, that's what I was using
- [ ] No, I was using something else (please specify: _______)

**Purpose:** Confirm segment fit; capture if they've switched methods multiple times

**Red flag:** If they say "something else," they may belong in a different segment pattern. Note and ask for clarification.

---

## Q11: Timeline of Learning & Stopping
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "How long ago did you stop using [method]?"

**Options:**
- Less than 3 months ago
- 3-6 months ago
- 6-12 months ago
- 1-2 years ago
- More than 2 years ago

**Purpose:** Recency matters — recent churn shows current pain; old churn shows if problem still exists

**Analysis:**
- "Less than 3 months" = hot churn; memory fresh; can ask specific details
- "6-12 months" = cooling period; may try again if conditions change
- "2+ years" = cold churn; something fundamental didn't work

---

## Q12: Duration of Engagement (Past Tense)
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "How long did you use [method] before you stopped?"

**Options:**
- Less than 1 week
- 1-2 weeks
- 2-4 weeks
- 1-3 months
- 3-6 months
- 6+ months

**Purpose:** Short engagement (< 2 weeks) = onboarding/expectation problem; Long engagement (6+ months) = burnout/boredom/plateau

**Analysis:**
- Immediate quit (< 1 week) = wrong fit, wrong expectation, or life crisis
- 2-4 weeks = got past onboarding but hit wall
- 3-6 months = gave it real chance, ran out of steam
- 6+ months = stickiness possible; major life event likely caused churn

---

## Q13: Specific Moment of Quitting
**Type:** Paragraph (open-ended)

**Question:** "What specifically made you decide to stop? Tell me about what happened."

**Purpose:** Most valuable churn insight — the exact breaking point

**Example answers we're looking for:**
- "The teacher started charging more and I couldn't afford it."
- "I realized I wasn't improving after 2 months. It felt pointless."
- "Work got busy and I missed a few classes, then I felt too behind to catch up."
- "The repetition exercises in Arabicle were so boring I just stopped opening the app."
- "I hit a plateau and didn't know how to break through it."

**Interview follow-up:** "You mentioned [reason]. Walk me through that day. What did you do immediately after deciding to stop?"

---

## Q14: Post-Churn Attempts
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "After you stopped [method], did you try any other ways to learn Arabic?"

**Options:**
- [ ] Yes, I switched to a different method → GO TO Q15
- [ ] No, I stopped trying altogether → SKIP TO Q16
- [ ] Yes, I tried briefly but stopped that too → GO TO Q15

**Purpose:** Understand churn patterns — single failure vs systemic Arabic learning burnout

**Analysis:**
- "Switched methods" = solution-focused; problem was specific to that method
- "Stopped altogether" = burned out on Arabic learning; deeper motivation issue
- "Tried briefly" = struggling to find right fit; may need guidance

---

## Q15: Alternative Methods Tried (Conditional)
**Type:** Checkboxes (multiple select)

**Question:** "After stopping [method], which other ways did you try to learn Arabic?"

**Options:**
- [ ] Different live class group (Telegram/Zoom/etc.)
- [ ] Arabicle.ru (if not original method)
- [ ] Duolingo or gamified apps
- [ ] YouTube videos
- [ ] ChatGPT or AI tools
- [ ] Textbooks or structured study
- [ ] Private tutor
- [ ] Language exchange partner
- [ ] Other (please specify)

**Purpose:** Understand what they think is "better" or "different"

**Analysis:**
- If they tried multiple methods = actively seeking solution
- If one method stuck = validation for that approach
- Pattern of failures = may need structured guidance or accountability

---

## Q16: Core Reason for Wanting to Restart
**Type:** Paragraph (open-ended)

**Question:** "What's making you want to try learning Arabic again now?"

**Purpose:** Reactivation trigger identification — what's changed in their life or motivation?

**Example answers:**
- "My kids are asking me to teach them Quran, so I need to know it myself."
- "A friend told me about a new method that might work better."
- "I realized I regret stopping. Life is calmer now and I have time again."
- "I started seeing Arabic in movies and it sparked my interest again."

**Interview follow-up:** "You mentioned [reason]. That's significant. How long have you felt this way?"

---

## Q17: What Would Make It Work This Time
**Type:** Checkboxes (multiple select)

**Question:** "What would need to be different for you to succeed this time? (Select all that apply)"

**Options:**
- [ ] Better structure/clearer learning path
- [ ] More affordability
- [ ] Better engagement/less boring
- [ ] Grammar explanations (not just memorization)
- [ ] More flexible schedule
- [ ] Community/accountability to keep going
- [ ] Speaking practice (confidence building)
- [ ] Progress visibility/tracking
- [ ] Different learning style entirely
- [ ] Something else (please specify)

**Purpose:** Direct path to product-market fit — what features would reactivate them?

**Critical insight:** If many say "structure" or "affordability" or "engagement," that's product validation for prioritization.

---

## Q18: Previous Payment Experience
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "When you were using [method], did you pay for it?"

**Options:**
- [ ] Yes, it was subscription-based ($X/month) → GO TO Q19
- [ ] Yes, one-time payment ($X) → GO TO Q19
- [ ] No, it was free → SKIP TO Q20
- [ ] It was free with optional paid features → GO TO Q19

**Purpose:** Understand pricing acceptance — did cost drive churn or was it other factors?

---

## Q19: Pricing Reflection (Conditional)
**Type:** Paragraph (open-ended)

**Question:** "You paid for [method]. Looking back, did you feel you got good value for your money?"

**Purpose:** Churn due to price vs. churn due to product mismatch

**Analysis:**
- "No, it wasn't worth it" = value mismatch; product didn't deliver
- "Yes, but couldn't afford it anymore" = economic churn; solve with payment plan?
- "Yes, but for some reason I stopped anyway" = willingness to pay exists; something else broke engagement

---

## Q20: Confidence in Reactivation
**Type:** Linear scale (1-5)

**Question:** "On a scale of 1-5, how confident are you that you'll actually restart learning Arabic?"

**Scale:**
- 1 = Very unlikely (I'm just exploring options)
- 2 = Somewhat unlikely
- 3 = Neutral (might happen, might not)
- 4 = Somewhat likely
- 5 = Very likely (I'm committed to trying again)

**Purpose:** Filter for serious restart intent vs wishful thinking

**Analysis:**
- 4-5 = High reactivation probability; good interview candidate
- 1-2 = Low commitment; may respond to incentives but unlikely to stick

---

## Q21: Reactivation Timeline
**Type:** Multiple choice (single select)

**Question:** "When are you planning to restart learning Arabic?"

**Options:**
- This week/next week
- Within the next month
- Within the next 3 months
- Not sure yet / depends on circumstances
- Just thinking about it

**Purpose:** Intent timing — who's ready to start NOW vs who's dreaming

**Analysis:**
- "This/next week" = hot intent; interview ASAP before motivation cools
- "Within month" = serious; good interview candidate
- "Not sure yet" = low priority; they may flake

---

## Q22: On a scale of 1-5, how satisfied were you with [method] before stopping?
**Type:** Linear scale (1-5)

**Scale:**
- 1 = Very unsatisfied
- 2 = Somewhat unsatisfied
- 3 = Neutral
- 4 = Somewhat satisfied
- 5 = Very satisfied

**Purpose:** Baseline satisfaction — did they love it and life got in the way, or did they hate it?

**Analysis:**
- Satisfied (4-5) but stopped = life circumstance churn; reactivatable with better timing/flexibility
- Unsatisfied (1-2) and stopped = product mismatch; solve with features or different approach
- Neutral (3) and stopped = never engaged deeply; hard to reactivate

## Interview Script (After Survey Match)

When interviewing Segment-Inactive respondents, prioritize these areas:

### 1. The Churn Story (10-15 min) — *DEEPEST DIVE*

**Opening:**
- "You mentioned stopping about [timeframe] ago. Walk me through that period."
- "What was happening in your life at that time?"

**Dig into the moment:**
- "Tell me about the specific day or moment you decided to stop."
- "What emotion did you feel? Frustration? Relief? Regret?"
- "Did you tell anyone you were stopping? What did you say to them?"

**Understand if it was product or life:**
- "If your life circumstances were different, would you have kept going?"
- "Was it the [method] itself that didn't work, or just timing?"
- "What frustrated you most about [method]?"

### 2. What They Tried After (5 min)

**If they tried other methods:**
- "You tried [method 2]. How was that different from [original]?"
- "Why did that one not stick either?"
- "What did [method 2] do better? What did it do worse?"

**If they stopped trying:**
- "When did you realize you weren't going to try anything else?"
- "What would have made you try something different?"

### 3. Reactivation Readiness (5-7 min)

- "You said you want to restart. What's changed since you stopped?"
- "Is this different from past motivation, or similar?"
- "What scares you about trying again?"
- "What would make you stick this time?"

### 4. Problem Deep Dive (Based on Q17 answers) (5-10 min)

**If they said "better structure":**
- "Tell me about the last time you felt lost or confused learning Arabic."
- "Who helps you decide what to study next?"

**If they said "affordability":**
- "What's your budget now? Has it changed since then?"
- "What would be a fair price for you?"

**If they said "engagement/boring":**
- "What part was boring? Be specific."
- "What would make it exciting for you?"

### 5. Quick-Win Testing (3-5 min)

- "If [method you quit] had [solution to your main problem], would you go back to it?"
- "Or would you prefer to start fresh with something new?"
- "What's more important: familiar but improved, or completely new?"

## Common Segment-Inactive Patterns

| Survey Response | Interpretation | Interview Priority |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Stopped < 3 months ago + clear reason | Hot churn; memory fresh | **PRIORITY** |
| 6+ month engagement + still satisfied | Life churn, not product | **PRIORITY** — likely reactivatable |
| < 2 week engagement + unsatisfied | Wrong fit; needs different approach | MEDIUM |
| Tried multiple methods after | Solution-seeking; hard to convert | MEDIUM |
| Very likely to restart (Q20: 5) | High intent | **PRIORITY** |
| Just exploring (Q20: 1-2) | Low commitment | LOW |

## Why This Segment Matters

**Pre-qualified validation:**
- Removed money objection (they've already paid)
- Validated pain (problem was real enough to try)
- Eliminated "would never try" doubt
- Identified specific failure modes to avoid

**Product-market fit signal:**
- If 70%+ say "I'd come back if [feature]" → that's your MVP
- If 50%+ still want to learn → market demand validated
- Churn pattern (life vs product) reveals customer type

**Reactivation is easier than acquisition:**
- Cost to reactivate lapsed customer < cost to acquire new customer
- They've already proven behavioral intent
- May just need one key fix or better timing

---

## Red Flags in This Segment

⚠️ **They quit and never wanted to try again** — Not part of this segment, move to "not learning" analysis

⚠️ **Tried 5+ different methods, all failed** — May indicate unrealistic expectations or misalignment with actual learning capacity

⚠️ **High satisfaction (5/5) but won't commit to restart** — Confidence is low; something beyond product is blocking them

⚠️ **Can't articulate why they quit** — Either too much time has passed or they're not being honest; interview may be challenging
