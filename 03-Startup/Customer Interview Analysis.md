# Customer Interview Analysis — Arabic Learning Platform

Deep analysis of customer interview questions aligned with **The Mom Test framework** and validation of critical platform assumptions.

**Purpose:** This document serves as the definitive reference for validating assumptions about your Arabic platform through behavioral customer discovery.

---

## Critical Context: Platform Assumptions

Your platform validates three core hypotheses:

1. **Willingness to pay:** Target audience will pay $20/month (when they currently pay $25-40/month for live classes)
2. **Hybrid model value:** Pre-recorded lessons + live practice combination is more valuable than either alone
3. **Differentiation:** Grammar teaching + engagement + AI tutor addresses real pain points competitors don't solve

**Your customer segments:**
- **Telegram live class students** (community-based, $25-40/month)
- **Arabicle.ru users** ($48.97 sunk cost, one-time payment model)
- **Self-taught learners** (YouTube + textbooks + AI, entirely free)

Each segment has different switching costs, pain points, and value drivers.

## Phase 1: Analysis of Existing Interview Questions

### Strengths ✓

**Excellent Framing (Lines 10-23)**
- Non-pitching approach sets professional tone
- Clear time boundary manages expectations
- Sets expectation for listening, not validation

**Strong Behavioral Questions**

| Question | Why It Works |
|----------|-------------|
| "What did you pay for your last Arabic learning method?" | Tests real currency (money), not hypothetical |
| "How long have you been learning?" | Establishes behavioral timeline |
| "Which ones? How long did you use each?" | Digs into specific past behavior with concrete timeframes |

**Competitor Discovery**
- Arabicle.ru specific question validates awareness
- Alternative consideration reveals competitive landscape awareness

**Jobs-to-be-Done Foundation**
- "What's your ultimate goal with Arabic?" begins outcome understanding
- Starting point for deeper "why" digging

### Weaknesses & Critical Gaps ⚠️

**Category 1: Hypothetical Questions (Unreliable)**

| Question | Problem | Fix |
|----------|---------|-----|
| "What would it take for you to start learning Arabic?" | **Future hypothetical** — imaginary barrier | Ask: "Tell me about the last time you tried to learn Arabic. What stopped you?" |
| "What would convince you to switch to something new?" | **Hypothetical + leading** — they guess what you want to hear | Ask: "Tell me about the last time you switched learning platforms. What triggered that decision?" |

**Category 2: Too Generic (Missing Specificity)**

| Question | Problem | Fix |
|----------|---------|-----|
| "What frustrates you most about it?" | **Too broad** — too many frustrations possible | Ask: "Tell me about the last time you got frustrated studying Arabic. What happened exactly?" |

**Category 3: Missing Observable Behavior (No "Show Me")**

No questions request demonstrations:
- "Show me the last lesson you completed"
- "Walk me through your typical study session"
- "Open your current learning app — show me your progress"

**Observable behavior** is stronger evidence than reported behavior.

**Category 4: Missing Segment-Specific Probing**

**No Telegram-specific questions:**
- How do they interact with the group?
- What keeps them in this community?
- What would they lose switching away?

**No Arabicle.ru-specific questions:**
- What specifically triggered the purchase?
- What's their satisfaction level now?
- What pain points are unmet?

**No self-taught-specific questions:**
- Why did they choose free over paid?
- What specific barrier stops them from paying?
- What evidence that they'll pay?

**Category 5: Missing Hybrid Model Validation**

No questions test preference for self-paced vs live instruction:
- "Tell me about the last time you wanted to review something from a previous class. What did you do?"
- "If you could study recorded lessons anytime AND practice live once weekly, how would that change your learning?"
- "Do you prefer self-paced flexibility or live accountability?"

**Category 6: Missing Retention Driver Questions**

No exploration of what keeps users studying past week 2:
- "What's the longest you've stuck with any learning method? What kept you going?"
- "Tell me about a week where you studied every day. What was different?"
- "How do you celebrate or track progress?"

**Category 7: Missing Social/Community Value**

- No questions about learning with others
- No referral request (Mom Test core technique)
- No community motivation exploration

**Category 8: Weak Pricing Validation**

- No specific $20/month validation
- No comparison to current spending breakdown
- No switching cost calculation

**Category 9: Weak AI Tutor Validation (Lines 82-86)**

Current AI questions are surface-level.

**Missing anxeity/shyness probing:**
- "Tell me about a time you felt too shy or anxious to ask a question in a live class. What happened?"
- "What would it feel like to practice speaking with an AI instead?"

## Phase 4: Missing Questions to Add

These questions target gaps in current interview script:

### Retention Drivers

1. **"What's the longest you've stuck with any Arabic learning method? What kept you going?"**
   - Reveals retention factors that worked
   - Identifies what sustained motivation looks like

2. **"Tell me about a week where you studied every day. What was different that week?"**
   - Positive behavior drivers
   - What environment, mindset, or circumstance enables consistent behavior?

3. **"What do you do on days when you don't feel like studying?"**
   - Motivation sustainability
   - How do they overcome resistance?

4. **"How do you celebrate or track progress?"**
   - Progress visualization value
   - Do they use apps? Spreadsheets? Just feeling it?

### Competition Deep Dive

5. **"What other Arabic learning platforms have you heard of but not tried? Why not?"**
   - Competitive awareness
   - What barriers exist for other platforms?

6. **"If you could take one feature from each platform you've tried and combine them, what would you build?"**
   - Feature prioritization signal
   - Reveals their ideal product

### Platform Features Validation

7. **"Tell me about a time you wanted to practice but felt too shy or anxious."**
   - AI tutor + anxiety-free practice validation
   - Is shyness/anxiety real or assumed?

8. **"If you could ask a teacher a question anytime, how often would you use that?"**
   - Async Q&A feature validation
   - Is on-demand help valuable?

9. **"Do you prefer learning new material at your own pace or in a scheduled class?"**
   - Self-paced vs live preference
   - This affects feature priority

10. **"Tell me about a time you reviewed something you learned weeks ago. What triggered that review?"**
    - Spaced repetition value
    - Do they naturally practice review? Or does it need to be built in?

### Mobile/Accessibility

11. **"Where do you usually study? Show me your setup."**
    - Device and environment context
    - Desktop? Mobile? Tablet? Commute? Home?

12. **"Do you study on your phone, tablet, or computer?"**
    - Platform priority
    - Should platform be mobile-first?

### Budget Reality Check

13. **"What's your monthly budget for self-improvement or education?"**
    - Total addressable budget
    - What's their typical spending on learning?

14. **"What other subscriptions do you pay for? How much?"**
    - Spending pattern context
    - Are they subscription-averse? Subscription-savvy?

### Social Learning

15. **"Do you ever study with a friend or family member?"**
    - Social learning preference
    - Is accountability/community valuable?

16. **"Would you want to see how other learners are progressing?"**
    - Leaderboard/community feature validation
    - Does competition or comparison motivate them?

## Critical Warnings: Questions to AVOID

**These questions produce unreliable data:**

### ❌ Never Ask These

| Question | Problem | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| "Would you use this feature?" | Hypothetical, unreliable | Imaginary scenario, not real behavior |
| "Do you like our idea?" | Seeking validation | They'll be encouraging, not honest |
| "How much would you pay?" | Imaginary number | They'll guess or be influenced |
| "What features do you want?" | People don't know what they want | Features aren't the question, jobs are |
| "Is this a good idea?" | Opinion, not evidence | Politeness, not truth |
| "Would this solve your problem?" | Leading + hypothetical | Suggests you already have the answer |

### ✓ Instead Ask These

| Better Alternative | Why It Works |
|------------------|--------------|
| "Show me how you currently do this." | Observable behavior, not reported |
| "Tell me about the last time you had this problem." | Recent past is most reliable |
| "What did you pay for your last solution?" | Real currency, real commitment |
| "Walk me through what you did." | Specific behavior beats generalization |
| "What are you using now? How much does it cost?" | Concrete facts, not opinions |
| "When did you last try to solve this? What happened?" | Past action beats hypothetical |

## Critical Assumptions Summary

Your platform must validate these before building heavily:

### Financial Assumptions

| Assumption | Validation Method | Key Question |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **$20/month is affordable** | Compare to current spending + education budget | "What are you paying now? What's your monthly education budget?" |
| **Willing to pay recurring subscription** | Past subscription behavior, not just one-time purchases | "What subscriptions do you currently pay for?" |
| **$20 is positioned correctly** | Not too cheap (looks fake) but not expensive | "How does $20/month compare to what you're spending now?" |

### Product Assumptions

| Assumption | Validation Method | Key Question |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Hybrid model (pre-recorded + live) is valuable** | Ask about past experience with both formats | "Tell me about a time you wanted to review a lesson. What did you do?" |
| **Grammar teaching is differentiator** | Probe Arabicle.ru users specifically | "Have you ever wanted to understand why something works, not just memorize it?" |
| **AI tutor addresses anxiety** | Find evidence of shyness/anxiety in live settings | "Tell me about a time you felt shy asking a question in class." |
| **Gamification drives retention** | Ask about past gamified app experience | "Tell me about a gamified app you used. What made you keep going or stop?" |
| **Self-paced is preferred** | Ask about schedule flexibility pain | "What happens when you miss a scheduled class?" |
| **Progress tracking matters** | Ask how they measure progress now | "How do you know you're improving?" |

### Segment Assumptions

**Telegram students:**
- Will pay $20/month despite free/cheap groups → **Test with pricing questions**
- Value community but want flexibility → **Ask about missed classes, review needs**

**Arabicle users:**
- Will switch despite $48.97 sunk cost → **Ask satisfaction, pain severity, switching threshold**
- Grammar gap is real pain → **Ask for specific frustration examples**

**Self-taught:**
- Will pay when YouTube is free → **Understand what would justify $20/month**
- Want structure despite choosing free → **Ask about choice paralysis, progress uncertainty**

## Success Criteria: When You've Validated Enough

You've collected sufficient customer evidence when:

✓ Interviewed **15-30 people in specific target segment** (not just "people interested in languages")
✓ Hearing **same problems repeatedly** — can predict what next person will say
✓ Interviewees have **already paid money or invested significant time** solving problem
✓ You understand **the job they're trying to accomplish** deeply
✓ You know **what existing solution they'd abandon** to use yours
✓ You can **articulate the problem in their words**, not yours
✓ Discovered **unexpected findings** that contradicted assumptions
✓ Identified **common currency** — specific amounts of money/time they're investing
✓ Validated at least **8 of your 10 critical assumptions**
