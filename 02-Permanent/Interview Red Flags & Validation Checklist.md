# Interview Red Flags & Validation Checklist

Quick reference for quality control during and after customer interviews.

---

## Red Flags During Interview (Stop & Recalibrate)

If you observe these, your data quality is at risk:

### 🚩 You're doing most of the talking
**Signal:** You've explained your idea, answered questions about your platform, or talked for >30 seconds without interruption
**What's wrong:** You're pitching, not learning
**What to do:** Stop. Go silent. Ask an open question and listen for 2 minutes
**Example:** Instead of "so that's why we built the AI tutor feature," ask "Tell me about the last time you practiced speaking Arabic."

---

### 🚩 Getting excessive compliments
**Signal:** "That's brilliant!" "Great idea!" "I would definitely use that!"
**What's wrong:** They're being encouraging, not giving evidence
**What to do:** Politely deflect. "Thanks! Now tell me about your experience with X..."
**Example:** After hearing "Your platform sounds amazing," follow with "Have you tried anything like this before? How did it go?"

---

### 🚩 Hearing hypothetical futures
**Signal:** "I would probably..." "I might..." "I think I would want..."
**What's wrong:** Imaginary behavior, not actual behavior
**What to do:** Dig into past behavior instead
**Example:** If they say "I would pay $20/month," ask "What did you pay for your last learning solution?"

---

### 🚩 No mention of currency
**Signal:** They've described problems but haven't mentioned $, time, or effort invested
**What's wrong:** Low skin in the game = low reliability
**What to do:** Ask directly about money/time spent
**Example:** "You mentioned frustration with Arabicle. How much did you pay for it? How long do you spend on it weekly?"

---

### 🚩 You feel validated
**Signal:** You're excited, feel like they confirmed your idea, thinking "this is going well"
**What's wrong:** Dangerous! Means you're not learning new things, just confirming existing beliefs
**What to do:** Identify what you assumed and ask the opposite
**Example:** If excited they liked AI tutor, ask "Have you ever had a bad experience with AI tutors?"

---

### 🚩 Nothing surprised you
**Signal:** Everything they say matches what you expected them to say
**What's wrong:** You asked only leading questions or already knew the answers
**What to do:** Look back at script. Did you ask about areas where you're least confident?
**Example:** If you're confident $20/month is acceptable, ask "What's the most expensive subscription you currently pay for? How much?"

---

### 🚩 Can't recall specific behaviors
**Signal:** Interview is done, but when you try to write down what they did/said, it's all vague
**What's wrong:** You weren't listening closely. You were thinking about next question
**What to do:** Take more detailed notes. Slow down. Ask for specific examples more
**Example:** Don't write "They don't like repetition" → write "In Week 2 of Arabicle, they said vocabulary review felt 'like mindless clicking' and they wanted to understand grammar patterns instead"

---

### 🚩 They didn't ask clarifying questions
**Signal:** You ask a question, they answer, but never ask "What do you mean by X?" or "Can you give me an example?"
**What's wrong:** They weren't genuinely curious or engaged
**What to do:** Note this as LOW interest signal. Be skeptical of their answers
**Example:** Compare: (Low interest) You: "Why did you stop using Duolingo?" Them: "It got boring." End. VS (High interest) You: "Why did you stop?" Them: "It got boring." Them: "Are you building something like that?"

## Questions to NEVER Ask (They Produce Garbage Data)

### ❌ "Would you use this feature?"
**Problem:** Hypothetical, unreliable
**Why:** They're imagining their ideal version, not reality
**Replacement:** "Tell me about the last time you looked for a feature like this. What was it?"

---

### ❌ "Do you like our idea?"
**Problem:** Seeking validation
**Why:** They'll be encouraging; not evidence-based
**Replacement:** "Have you heard of similar solutions? How did they work for you?"

---

### ❌ "How much would you pay?"
**Problem:** Imaginary number
**Why:** People guess or anchor to your hints
**Replacement:** "What did you pay for your last Arabic course? How long did you use it?"

---

### ❌ "What features do you want?"
**Problem:** People don't know what they want
**Why:** Features ≠ solutions. They want outcomes, not features
**Replacement:** "Tell me about the last time you got frustrated learning Arabic. What were you trying to do?"

---

### ❌ "Is this a good idea?"
**Problem:** Opinion, not evidence
**Why:** Politeness ≠ truth
**Replacement:** "What would need to be true for you to switch from your current method?"

---

### ❌ "Would this solve your problem?"
**Problem:** Leading + hypothetical
**Why:** Suggests you already have the answer
**Replacement:** "How are you currently solving this? What's not working about your approach?"

---

## Questions to ALWAYS Ask (They Produce Gold Data)

### ✓ "Show me how you currently do this."
**Why it works:** Observable behavior > reported behavior
**Captures:** Real workflow, pain points they don't even notice
**Example:** "Show me your typical study session" → see they struggle with certain lesson types, spend time on admin vs learning, etc.

---

### ✓ "Tell me about the last time you had this problem."
**Why it works:** Recent past is most reliable
**Captures:** Real emotion, specific context, actual behavior
**Example:** "Tell me about the last time you felt frustrated studying Arabic" → get specific incident, what triggered it, how they felt, what they did

---

### ✓ "What did you pay for your last solution?"
**Why it works:** Real currency, real commitment
**Captures:** Willingness to pay, switching cost, problem severity
**Example:** "What did you pay for your last Arabic learning method?" → know if they're price-sensitive, what budget they allocate, how long they stuck with it

---

### ✓ "Walk me through what you did."
**Why it works:** Specific behavior beats generalization
**Captures:** Exact steps, where friction occurs, how committed they were
**Example:** "Walk me through the last time you used Arabicle" → discover they abandoned it after 2 weeks, see exact lesson where they got stuck

---

### ✓ "What are you using now? How much does it cost?"
**Why it works:** Concrete facts, not opinions
**Captures:** Current solution landscape, actual spending, competitive alternatives
**Example:** Get specific app names, exact monthly costs, how long they've been using each

---

### ✓ "When did you last try to solve this? What happened?"
**Why it works:** Past action beats hypothetical
**Captures:** Real urgency, real barriers, switching thresholds
**Example:** "When did you last try to improve your Arabic? What stopped you?" → learn what barriers are real vs imagined

## Post-Interview Validation Checklist

After each interview, check if you have:

### Data Quality Signals ✓

- [ ] **Captured specific amounts:** Exact $ spent, hours per week, how many weeks they used it
- [ ] **Documented real behaviors:** What they actually do, not what they think they do
- [ ] **Identified currency:** Money, time, effort they're investing
- [ ] **Found emotional indicators:** When did they get animated? Frustrated? What triggered emotion?
- [ ] **Documented workarounds:** How they work around current pain (external resource, DIY solution, just accept it)
- [ ] **Recorded exact quotes:** Key phrases in their words, not your interpretation
- [ ] **Identified assumptions tested:** What did this interview validate or challenge?

### Red Flag Checks 🚩

- [ ] Did they mention specific past behavior (not hypothetical future)?
- [ ] Did they describe problems with specificity (not generalizations)?
- [ ] Did they reveal currency they're already invested (money/time)?
- [ ] Were there any surprising findings (not just confirmations)?
- [ ] Did you listen >80% of the conversation?
- [ ] Can you write down 3 specific behaviors they described?

### Assumption Testing ✓

For each critical assumption, did you learn whether it's:
- **Validated:** Evidence supports it
- **Challenged:** Evidence contradicts it
- **Unknown:** Need more interviews

Critical assumptions to track:
- [ ] $20/month willingness to pay
- [ ] Hybrid model (self-paced + live) appeal
- [ ] Grammar teaching differentiator
- [ ] Retention challenge after week 2
- [ ] Switching behavior from current solution
- [ ] Community value
- [ ] AI tutor anxiety relief
- [ ] Gamification effectiveness
- [ ] Self-paced preference
- [ ] Progress tracking importance

## How to Know You've Collected Enough Data

You can stop interviewing when:

✓ **You've interviewed 15-30 people in each target segment** (not just "interested in languages")

✓ **You hear the same problems repeatedly** — Can predict what next person will say

✓ **People have already paid money or invested significant time** solving the problem

✓ **You understand the job they're trying to accomplish** deeply (not just the features you want to build)

✓ **You know what existing solution they'll abandon** to use yours

✓ **You can articulate the problem in their words**, not yours

✓ **You've validated 8+ of your 10 critical assumptions** with behavioral evidence

✓ **You discovered unexpected findings** that contradicted your initial beliefs

✓ **You stopped being surprised** (last 5 interviews taught you nothing new)

## Validation Success Criteria

Your assumptions are validated when:

### Segment Assumption: Telegram Students Will Pay $20/month

**Strong evidence:**
- 70%+ of interviewed Telegram users currently pay for Arabic learning
- All mention specific pain with free/cheap Telegram groups
- Show frustration with schedule inflexibility (can't review past classes)
- Interested in hybrid model when described with specifics
- Ask clarifying questions about pricing and features

**Weak evidence:**
- They say "I might pay" (hypothetical)
- They compliment the idea (seeking validation)
- They can't articulate specific pain point
- No mention of what they currently spend
- No questions about features or access

### Segment Assumption: Arabicle Users Will Switch Despite Sunk Cost

**Strong evidence:**
- Satisfaction rating <6/10 with Arabicle
- Can point to specific frustration (not just "it's boring")
- Mention trying to solve grammar gap externally
- Ask about retention/engagement mechanics
- Show interest in grammar teaching validation
- Already switched learning methods before

**Weak evidence:**
- Say "I would probably try it"
- Mention sunk cost as barrier (signals it matters)
- Only compliment Arabicle, no criticisms
- Can't articulate what's missing

### Segment Assumption: Self-Taught Will Pay When Value Clear

**Strong evidence:**
- Have spent money on Arabic before (books, tutors)
- Frustrated with YouTube/textbook approach (not loving it)
- Understand their current solution costs them time (quantifiable)
- Interested in structure/accountability
- Would accept $5 trial offer
- Can introduce other self-taught learners

**Weak evidence:**
- "I would pay if it was perfect" (condition is vague)
- "YouTube is great, I'd just want structure added"
- No past paid learning attempts
- Can't quantify time spent or barriers faced

## Common Patterns to Watch For

### Pattern: Retention Drops Week 2-3

If 8+ of 10 interviewees mention dropping off after 2 weeks:
- Problem is **real and validated**
- Feature priority: Progress tracking, streak mechanics, or structured curriculum
- Test: Do they respond to gamification or accountability?

### Pattern: Switching Costs High but Not Insurmountable

If most mention investment but also mention frustration:
- Switching barrier is **real** but **breakable with clear value**
- Implication: UI/UX quality matters (can't look cheap)
- Test: Would better grammar teaching break them free?

### Pattern: Grammar Is "Nice-to-Have" Not "Must-Have"

If Arabicle users don't mention grammar unprompted:
- Assumption may be wrong
- Problem: You're fixing a problem they don't perceive as acute
- Test: Ask harder "Why did you try to understand grammar?" questions

### Pattern: Community is Valued Higher Than Expected

If Telegram students worry most about losing peer relationships:
- Implication: Async community features > AI tutor
- Test: Would leaderboard or study buddy matching matter more?

### Pattern: Self-Taught Blocked by Free-to-Paid Transition

If most say "YouTube is good enough, just messy":
- Problem: Not that YouTube is bad, but that structure is missing
- Opportunity: Lightweight structure + affordability might work

## Decision Framework After Interviews

### If You Have Strong Validation (8+ assumptions validated)

**Decision:** Proceed to MVP or waitlist
- You have behavioral evidence from 15-30 customers
- Problem is understood and acute
- Willingness to pay is demonstrated
- Proceed to building MVP or landing page + waitlist

### If You Have Mixed Validation (4-7 assumptions validated)

**Decision:** Additional targeted interviews
- Pick 2-3 assumptions that feel weakest
- Run 5 more focused interviews on just those
- Look for pattern contradictions or surprising findings
- Then reassess readiness

### If You Have Weak Validation (<4 assumptions validated)

**Decision:** Pivot or investigate deeper
- Either your segment is wrong, or your assumptions are
- Pick one assumption that contradicts findings
- Dig into root cause: Is the problem not real? Is pricing wrong? Is segment misidentified?
- Consider: Are you talking to the right people?

## Interview Execution Checklist

Before each interview:
- [ ] Know your learning goal (which assumption testing?)
- [ ] Print the right script (correct segment)
- [ ] Have notebook ready (take detailed notes)
- [ ] Request recording permission
- [ ] Identify 1 specific behavior to observe
- [ ] Review red flags (what to watch for)

During interview:
- [ ] Introduce yourself without pitching
- [ ] Listen 80%, talk 20%
- [ ] Take notes (show you care)
- [ ] Ask for demonstrations
- [ ] Follow energy (dig when they animate)
- [ ] Note exact quotes
- [ ] Avoid pitching

After interview:
- [ ] Document key findings while fresh (10 minutes)
- [ ] Extract metrics (currency, behavior, emotion)
- [ ] Note assumptions validated/challenged
- [ ] Identify surprising findings
- [ ] Update your running assumption tracker
