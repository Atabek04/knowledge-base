**Verified against his physical copy on 2026-09-04:**

- His table of contents matches the US 5th edition page for page, every chapter, every section title, every page number.
- §1.1 exercise 23 in his copy is the True/False block, on page 11, beginning "a. Every elementary row operation is reversible."

That second check is the important one. His copy is a **Global Edition**, and Global Editions
sometimes carry altered exercises. Exercise 23 landing exactly where the US edition puts its
True/False block means the exercise numbering below can be used directly. If a future exercise
number ever fails to match what he reads aloud, stop trusting this table and confirm each one.

Where the two-column exercise layout defeated text extraction, the entry says **unconfirmed**.
A blank is a blank. A wrong exercise number is worse than a gap.

## How every Lay section is built

1. Body text with numbered **EXAMPLE**s.
2. A short **PRACTICE PROBLEMS** block, usually two to four, immediately before the exercises.
3. The numbered **EXERCISES** set.
4. **SOLUTIONS TO PRACTICE PROBLEMS** at the very end of the section.

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Point 4 is why the Practice Problems are the correct warm-up: he gets fully worked solutions
without touching an answer key.</mark> One syllabus states the same workflow explicitly, that
students should do all Practice Problems first and only then continue to the exercises.

Most exercise sets contain a **two-problem True/False block**, worded "In Exercises N and N+1,
mark each statement True or False. Justify each answer." Each of the two carries lettered
sub-statements, so two exercises is really eight to twelve conceptual checks. **These are your
primary diagnostic.** §1.1 uses a variant worth quoting to him once, because it explains the
whole device: the statements "are either quoted directly, restated slightly but still true, or
altered in some way that makes them false in some cases."

Chapter-level True/False also exists: each chapter's Supplementary Exercises open with "Mark
each statement True or False. Justify each answer" and a long lettered list. Chapter 1's runs
to at least sub-item o. That is the natural end-of-chapter instrument.

## The exercise policy

From his own tracker, and it overrides the syllabus column above.

1. **Practice Problems first**, always. They calibrate and they self-check.
2. **Four to six odd-numbered computational exercises.** Odds have answers in the back, so he self-checks.
3. **The True/False exercise in every set.** Never skip it. It is the conceptual gold in Lay.
4. **Skip** application exercises unless the application is ML-relevant, and skip the MATLAB ones.
5. **Escalation.** Three easy in a row, skip ahead. Two hard in a row, stop grinding and take it apart with the hint ladder.

Split each set across two sessions days apart, and stop after the first few correct rather
than overlearning.

## Editions

### 5th against 6th

Chapters 1, 2, 3 and 7 keep identical section numbers and titles. What moved:

| Topic | 5th ed | 6th ed |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | 4.6 | folded into 4.5 |
| Change of Basis | 4.7 | **4.6** |
| Digital Signal Processing | absent | 4.7, new |
| Markov Chains | 4.9 | **5.9**, moved chapters |
| §6.6 title | Applications to Linear Models | **Machine Learning and Linear Models** |

The 6th edition also adds **Reasonable Answers** boxes and exercises, which are inserted into
existing exercise sets and therefore **shift every exercise number downstream of the
insertion point**, plus end-of-chapter Projects
([Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/linear-algebra-and-its-applications/P200000006235?view=educator)).

**Standing habit.** Name problems by descriptor first and number second: "the True/False pair
in §6.5 Least-Squares Problems", or "the Practice Problems for §1.7 Linear Independence".
Descriptors survive renumbering. Give the number only for the edition he has confirmed.

### Global against US

His copy is a Global Edition, ISBN 9781292092232, with its own instructor's solutions manual.
Resellers describe Global editions as differing in binding, ISBN, and "in rather rare
occasions, varying exercises at the end of the book". No source states section by section
which Lay 5e Global exercises differ.

**This was resolved empirically for this vault.** His table of contents matches the US edition
page for page, and §1.1 exercise 23 is the True/False block exactly as the US edition has it.
Treat the tables above as usable. If any single exercise ever fails to match what he reads
aloud, revert to asking him to read the first line of each exercise before working it.
